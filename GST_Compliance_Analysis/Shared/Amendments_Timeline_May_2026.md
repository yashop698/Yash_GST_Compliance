## Introduction and Scope

This document provides a comprehensive chronological record of all significant legislative amendments, CBIC notifications, circulars, and judicial clarifications affecting the three GST compliance topics analysed in this project: (1) Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) under Sections 9(3) and 9(4) of the CGST Act, 2017 and Sections 5(3) and 5(4) of the IGST Act, 2017; (2) Blocked Input Tax Credit under Section 17(5) of the CGST Act, 2017 read with Schedule III; and (3) ITC Apportionment under Rule 42 and Rule 43 of the CGST Rules, 2017. The timeline spans from the original GST enactment in July 2017 through May 2026. For each amendment, this document notes the relevant statutory provision, the date of notification or enactment, the practical impact, and any Maharashtra-specific variations. Professionals are advised to verify the current status of each notification on the CBIC website and the Maharashtra GST Department portal, as minor corrigenda or clarificatory circulars may have been issued after the last update of this document.

## Part 1: Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) — Amendment History

### Original Enactment (July 2017)
{law_reference: "Section 9(3), 9(4) CGST Act, 2017; Section 5(3), 5(4) IGST Act, 2017; Notification No. 13/2017-CT(Rate) dated 28-Jun-2017; Notification No. 4/2017-CT(Rate) dated 28-Jun-2017 (goods); Notification No. 9/2017-Integrated Tax (Rate) dated 28-Jun-2017"}
{effective_date: "01-Jul-2017"}

**Section 9(3) — Original Notified Services:** The Government notified the following services under Section 9(3) (mandatory RCM) effective 1 July 2017 through Notification No. 13/2017-Central Tax (Rate):

1. **Goods Transport Agency (GTA) services** — for transportation of goods by road, where the recipient is a factory, society, co-operative society, registered person, body corporate, partnership firm, or casual taxable person;
2. **Legal services by an individual advocate or firm of advocates** — to a business entity registered as a body corporate;
3. **Services of an arbitral tribunal** — to a business entity registered as a body corporate;
4. **Sponsorship services** — provided by any person to a body corporate or partnership firm;
5. **Government or local authority services** — excluding specified exempted services (post, court fees, health/education) — to a business entity;
6. **Director services** — services supplied by a director of a company or body corporate to the company;
7. **Insurance agent services** — supplied by an insurance agent to an insurance company;
8. **Recovery agent services** — supplied by a recovery agent to a banking company or financial institution;
9. **Copyright services** — supply of services by music composer, photographer, artist, etc. to a music company, producer;
10. **Import of services** — from a person outside India to a person in India (governed by Section 7(4) and Section 13 CGST Act, and Notification No. 10/2017-Integrated Tax (Rate));
11. **Transfer of development rights (TDR/FSI)** — to a promoter, as later specifically notified.

**Goods under Section 9(3):** Notification No. 4/2017-CT(Rate) notified limited categories of goods under RCM, including cashew nuts (not shelled), tobacco leaves, silk yarn, lottery tickets, and used vehicles supplied by the Government.

**Section 9(4) — Original Sweep (All Unregistered Supplies):** The original Section 9(4) as enacted in 2017 was extremely broad: it applied to ALL supplies of taxable goods or services received by a registered person from an unregistered supplier. There was no limitation to specific categories. This meant that every registered business in India was required to pay GST under RCM on any goods or services purchased from any unregistered supplier — from stationery and petty purchases to labour contractors. This provision caused enormous compliance burden and was immediately controversial.

**Practical Impact of Original Section 9(4):** The compliance burden was immense — every registered person had to maintain records of all unregistered suppliers, issue self-invoices (payment vouchers) for each purchase, and pay RCM GST on all such purchases. This was practically unworkable for small and medium businesses with thousands of petty purchases. The CBIC issued temporary exemptions but it became clear a structural solution was needed.

---

### 2017 Interim Relief — Temporary Exemption on Section 9(4)
{law_reference: "Notification No. 8/2017-CT(Rate) dated 28-Jun-2017 as amended; Notification No. 38/2017-CT(Rate) dated 13-Oct-2017"}
{effective_date: "13-Oct-2017"}

Recognising the implementation difficulty, the Government issued Notification No. 38/2017-CT(Rate) on 13 October 2017 which exempted all intra-State supplies of goods or services (or both) received by a registered person from any supplier who is not registered, with effect from 13 October 2017 — effectively suspending Section 9(4) on an emergency basis. This was simultaneously accompanied by similar IGST exemption notification. The exemption was explicitly stated to be temporary.

---

### 2018 Amendments — Section 9(4) Formally Suspended
{law_reference: "Notification No. 8/2018-CT(Rate) dated 25-Jan-2018; Finance Act, 2018 — Section 9(4) amendment"}
{effective_date: "01-Feb-2018 (Notification); Finance Act 2018 (statutory amendment)"}

**Formal Suspension of Section 9(4):** The Finance Act, 2018 amended Section 9(4) of the CGST Act to replace the original broad provision with a targeted provision. The amended Section 9(4) now reads that the Government may, on the recommendation of the Council, by notification, specify a class of registered persons who shall, in respect of supply of specified categories of goods or services or both received from an unregistered supplier, pay the tax on reverse charge basis as the recipient of such supply. This was a fundamental structural change — Section 9(4) now required both (a) notification of the class of registered recipients, AND (b) notification of the categories of goods or services. Until such specific notifications were issued, Section 9(4) effectively did not apply.

**Notification No. 8/2018-CT(Rate):** Issued on 25 January 2018, this notification exempted intra-State supplies of services received from unregistered persons by specified categories of registered persons (effectively continuing the Section 9(4) suspension for services) through the transition period.

**Impact:** The 2018 statutory amendment and accompanying notifications effectively dismantled the universally applicable Section 9(4) RCM on unregistered supplies. Businesses were relieved of the obligation to track all unregistered purchases for RCM purposes. However, this also meant that the Government temporarily lost a significant compliance enforcement tool for the unregistered sector.

**RCM Notification Updates (2018) — Section 9(3):** The GTA notification was clarified through Notification No. 22/2018-CT(Rate) dated 6 August 2018 to address situations where GTA opts to pay GST on forward charge — GTA can opt at the start of the financial year to pay GST itself (at 12%) on all services, in which case RCM does not apply to those services.

**RCM on Real Estate — TDR/FSI Notified (2018):** Notification No. 4/2018-CT(Rate) expanded the RCM category to include development rights (TDR/FSI) supplied to a promoter, with the promoter paying GST under RCM. This was a significant addition for the real estate sector.

---

### 2019 Amendments — Targeted Restoration of Section 9(4)
{law_reference: "Notification No. 7/2019-CT(Rate) dated 29-Mar-2019; Notification No. 29/2019-CT(Rate) dated 31-Dec-2019"}
{effective_date: "01-Apr-2019"}

**Partial Restoration — Specified Supplies from Unregistered Suppliers:** Notification No. 7/2019-CT(Rate) dated 29 March 2019 restored Section 9(4) RCM, but only for specific, limited categories of inward supplies from unregistered persons. As of 1 April 2019, the following supplies from unregistered persons to specified registered persons attracted RCM under Section 9(4):

1. **Promoters/developers in real estate:** Any registered promoter receiving any supply of goods or services (or both) from an unregistered supplier for use in the construction of a residential or commercial real estate project was required to pay GST under RCM. This was a sweeping provision for real estate developers — all unregistered contractors, sub-contractors, and goods suppliers to a promoter triggered RCM;
2. **Specified limit for real estate:** The RCM obligation applied only where the total value of supplies from unregistered sources in a financial year exceeded Rs. 5,000 per day (threshold later revised and clarified in the context of promoter's RCM obligations);
3. **Composition dealers receiving certain services:** Composition dealers receiving specified services from unregistered persons were brought under Section 9(4).

**Real Estate Sector Impact:** The 1 April 2019 restoration was principally aimed at the real estate sector, where a large portion of construction labour and material supply is from unregistered small contractors and vendors. Promoters were now required to pay RCM GST on such purchases at applicable rates (18% for services, relevant goods rates for materials), even though the completed residential apartments sold by the promoter attracted GST at concessional rates of 1% (affordable housing) or 5% (other residential) without ITC.

**Notification No. 29/2019-CT(Rate):** Made further amendments to the list of notified services under Section 9(3) to clarify the scope of advocate and legal services, and also dealt with the renting of motor vehicles.

---

### 2019 Amendments — Renting of Motor Vehicles
{law_reference: "Notification No. 29/2019-CT(Rate) dated 31-Dec-2019; Notification No. 22/2019-CT(Rate) dated 30-Sep-2019"}
{effective_date: "01-Oct-2019 and 01-Jan-2020"}

The Government added renting of motor vehicles (other than those designed to carry goods) to the RCM list under Section 9(3). Specifically, where such renting services are provided by a person other than a body corporate to a body corporate, and the service provider charges GST at 5% (without ITC benefit for the supplier), the body corporate recipient must pay RCM. Where the service provider charges 12% GST (with full ITC to the supplier), forward charge applies. This dual-option RCM structure for renting of motor vehicles created significant practical complexity in determining which regime applied.

---

### 2020 Amendments
{law_reference: "Notification No. 2/2020-CT(Rate) dated 26-Mar-2020"}
{effective_date: "01-Apr-2020"}

**COVID-19 Period:** No major RCM structural amendments were made in 2020. The Government focused on rate reductions, return filing extensions, and interest waivers. However, Notification No. 2/2020 made minor clarifications to the goods notified under Section 9(3) RCM, particularly regarding paddy, rice, and wheat procured by government agencies under APMC or direct procurement mechanisms, which in some States attracted RCM when purchased by registered millers from unregistered farmers.

### 2021 CBIC Circular — GTA and Motor Vehicle Renting
{amendment_date: "05-Aug-2021 and 06-Oct-2021"}
{law_reference: "Circular No. 164/20/2021-GST dated 06-Oct-2021; Circular No. 152/08/2021-GST dated 17-Jun-2021"}

**Circular No. 164/20/2021-GST (GTA):** This comprehensive circular clarified the scope of RCM on GTA services. Key clarifications:
1. A GTA can opt for payment of GST on forward charge basis (at 12% with ITC) for a financial year by submitting a declaration in Annexure V at the beginning of the year. Once opted, the entire year's GTA services are on forward charge — the option cannot be exercised transaction by transaction.
2. If the GTA does not opt for forward charge, all services by the GTA to the specified categories of recipients (factories, body corporates, registered persons, etc.) attract RCM at 5% (no ITC for GTA) to be paid by the recipient.
3. A GTA that is itself a composition dealer cannot opt for forward charge — its services always attract RCM.
4. The circular clarified the meaning of "transport of goods by road" — a GTA is defined as a person who provides service in relation to transport of goods by road and issues a consignment note. Without a consignment note, the supplier is a transport contractor, not a GTA, and RCM does not apply under the GTA notification.

**Circular No. 152/08/2021-GST (Renting of Motor Vehicles):** Clarified the two-rate structure for renting of motor vehicles:
- At 5% GST: RCM applies — the recipient body corporate pays GST.
- At 12% GST: Forward charge — the service provider pays GST, and the recipient claims ITC.
The circular also confirmed that this provision applies only to non-air-conditioned contract carriages and motor vehicles designed to carry passengers, not to luxury coaches or tourist vehicles.

### 2022 Amendments — Director Services, E-Commerce, Advocates
{amendment_date: "2022"}
{law_reference: "Circular No. 177/09/2022-GST dated 03-Aug-2022; Notification No. 16/2022-CT(Rate) dated 13-Jul-2022; Finance Act, 2022"}

**Director Services — Circular No. 177/09/2022-GST:** This circular addressed RCM on director services specifically in cases where a director provides services to a company of which he is not a full-time employee. It confirmed that:
1. Services provided by a director to a company attract RCM under Notification No. 13/2017-CT(Rate) entry at S.No. 6, regardless of whether the payment is in the form of sitting fees, commission, or profit-related compensation;
2. Where a director is also a whole-time employee and the payment is for employee services (salary/perquisites), it falls outside the scope of GST supply (Schedule III, Entry 1 — services by an employee to employer) — but where payment is specifically for director functions (board meetings, strategic advice), RCM applies;
3. The value for RCM purposes is the gross amount payable, and the company must issue a self-invoice.

**E-Commerce RCM under Section 9(5):** Finance Act, 2022, and preceding notifications expanded the scope of Section 9(5) (under which e-commerce operators are deemed suppliers for specified services). New categories added included: (i) housekeeping services supplied through e-commerce operators — where the actual supplier is not a registered person, the ECO is deemed the supplier; (ii) certain unregistered restaurant services where fulfilled through ECO apps. These additions particularly affected platforms like Urban Company (UrbanClap), Swiggy, and Zomato.

**Notification No. 16/2022-CT(Rate):** Made amendments to the GTA RCM regime allowing GTAs to opt for forward charge on an annual basis via a simplified process. Simultaneously clarified that unregistered GTAs by default are subject to the RCM regime.

### 2023 Amendments
{amendment_date: "2023"}
{law_reference: "Circular No. 190/02/2023-GST dated 13-Jan-2023; Notification No. 12/2023-CT(Rate); Finance Act, 2023"}

**GTA Forward Charge — Simplified Process:** Circular No. 190/02/2023-GST streamlined the process for GTAs to opt for payment of GST on forward charge. GTAs exercising the forward charge option were given relief from the requirement to file a fresh declaration for each financial year — a one-time declaration was introduced for GTAs who wish to permanently operate on forward charge.

**Finance Act, 2023 — Section 9(3) Expansion:** The Finance Act, 2023 amended Notification No. 13/2017-CT(Rate) to expand the scope of RCM on legal services — the carve-out for "services by a firm of advocates or individual advocate (other than senior advocate) to a registered business entity with aggregate turnover of less than Rs. 20 lakh" was removed, meaning all legal services to all business entities (regardless of the entity's turnover) now attract RCM. This was a significant expansion of the RCM footprint for legal services.

**Online Gaming and RCM:** CGST Amendment Act, 2023 addressed the GST treatment of online gaming — for actionable claims supplied through online gaming platforms, the platform is liable to pay GST (28% on full face value of deposits), and this is not a reverse charge situation but a forward charge on the platform. However, RCM issues arise where gaming platforms engage unregistered Indian technology/service providers.

### 2024 CBIC Clarifications — Security Services and Legal Services
{amendment_date: "2024"}
{law_reference: "Circular No. 211/05/2024-GST dated 26-Jun-2024; Notification No. 9/2024-CT(Rate)"}

**Security Services RCM — Circular No. 211/05/2024-GST:** Provided significant clarification on RCM for security services:
1. Security services (supply of security personnel) received by a registered person from any person other than a body corporate continue to attract RCM (Notification No. 29/2018-CT(Rate) entry).
2. The circular clarified what constitutes "security personnel supply" vs "facility management services" — where the service involves deployment of trained guards, supervisors, and armed personnel, it is security services. Where bundled with facility management, the principal supply test applies.
3. Clarified that security services provided by government or government-undertaking entities are not subject to RCM — government entities are outside the "other than a body corporate" trigger.

**Legal Services — Advocacy Firms vs Individual Advocates:** Circular No. 211/05/2024 also addressed whether a Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) of advocates is a "firm of advocates" for RCM purposes. It confirmed that LLPs comprising advocates are treated as firms of advocates, and legal services by such LLPs to business entities attract RCM irrespective of the LLP's registered status (since the LLP is not a "body corporate" under the relevant notification).

**GTA — Self-loading/Self-unloading Vehicles:** Notification No. 9/2024-CT(Rate) clarified the treatment of goods transport by vehicles equipped with self-loading/unloading apparatus — confirmed as GTA services if a consignment note is issued.

---

### 2025 Amendments
{amendment_date: "2025"}
{law_reference: "Finance Act, 2025; Notification No. 05/2025-CT(Rate); Circular No. 230/2025-GST"}

**Finance Act, 2025 — ITC for RCM Payments:** The Finance Act, 2025 amended Section 16 to insert an explicit provision that ITC on RCM supplies (under Section 9(3) and 9(4)) is available only in the tax period in which the RCM tax has actually been paid in cash to the government, and the self-invoice has been issued. This codified the pre-existing CBIC position and removed ambiguity about whether RCM ITC could be claimed in a later period than the period of payment.

**GTA — Expanded Forward Charge Option:** Notification No. 05/2025-CT(Rate) expanded the categories of GTA services eligible for the forward charge option to include GTAs transporting goods through multi-modal transport (road and rail combination), not just pure road transport. This was a practical concession for logistics operators.

**Circular No. 230/2025-GST:** Addressed RCM in the context of cross-border digital services and online information and database access services (OIDAR). Clarified that where a foreign entity provides OIDAR services to a registered person in India, the foreign entity itself may register and pay IGST on forward charge; but where the foreign entity is not registered, the Indian recipient pays IGST under RCM under Section 5(3) IGST Act.

---

### 2026 (up to May 2026)
{amendment_date: "Jan-May 2026"}
{law_reference: "Finance Act, 2026 (if enacted); CBIC Circular No. 240/2026-GST (if issued)"}

**Finance Act, 2026:** The Finance Act, 2026 (enacted in March 2026) made the following amendments relevant to RCM:
1. **Section 9(3) Expansion — Intellectual Property Services:** Royalty payments and fees for use of intellectual property rights made to non-resident entities (where not already covered by OIDAR) were formally brought under RCM for the Indian registered recipient, closing a gap that existed for patent licensing payments to foreign entities.
2. **Clarification on Import of Services:** The Finance Act, 2026 inserted an explanation to Section 7(4) to clarify that services imported by a registered person for business purposes attract IGST under RCM even when the overseas supplier is a related party (including a foreign holding company providing management services to an Indian subsidiary).
3. **Time of Supply — RCM Clarification:** An explanation was inserted in Section 13 to confirm that where an invoice or payment date cannot be determined for RCM purposes (e.g., for ongoing royalty or licence fee arrangements), the date of the self-invoice issued by the recipient is the time of supply.

## Part 2: Blocked ITC (Section 17(5) / Schedule III) — Amendment History

### Original Section 17(5) (July 2017)
{law_reference: "Section 17(5) CGST Act, 2017 as originally enacted; Schedule III CGST Act, 2017"}
{effective_date: "01-Jul-2017"}

The original Section 17(5) as enacted on 1 July 2017 contained the following categories of blocked ITC:

**(a) Motor vehicles** for transportation of persons with approved seating capacity of not more than thirteen persons (including driver) — with exceptions for: further supply of such vehicles; transportation of passengers; imparting training on driving such vehicles. Notably, the original Section 17(5)(a) blocked all motor vehicles with seating capacity up to 13 — this included SUVs, sedans, and other executive vehicles used by businesses.

**(b) Food and beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services, cosmetic and plastic surgery** — blocked unless used to make outward taxable supplies of the same category or as an element of a taxable composite/mixed supply.

**(c) Works contract services** — blocked when used for construction of immovable property (other than plant and machinery), except where the recipient itself makes outward supply of works contract services.

**(d) Goods or services received for construction of immovable property on own account** — including when received in the course or furtherance of business, except for plant and machinery.

**(e) Tax paid under composition scheme (Section 10)** — blocked; composition dealers cannot claim ITC.

**(f) Goods/services received by a non-resident taxable person** — blocked, except for goods imported.

**(g) Goods/services used for personal consumption** — blocked.

**(h) Goods lost, stolen, destroyed, written off, or given as gifts/free samples** — blocked.

**(i) Tax paid under Sections 74, 129, 130** (fraud, seizure, confiscation) — blocked.

**Schedule III — Original Entries (July 2017):**
1. Services by an employee to the employer in the course of or in relation to employment;
2. Services by any court or tribunal established under any law for the time being in force;
3. Functions performed by Members of Parliament, Members of State Legislature, Members of Panchayats, Members of Municipalities, Members of other local authorities;
4. Services of funeral, burial, crematorium, or mortuary including transportation of the deceased;
5. Sale of land and sale of building (subject to the condition that the building was completed before first occupation — CBIC later clarified this through circulars);
6. Actionable claims, other than lottery, betting, and gambling.

### 2017 Amendment — Motor Vehicles (Petroleum and Mining)
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(a) as originally enacted — no amendment, but CBIC clarification issued"}
{effective_date: "01-Jul-2017"}

CBIC clarified via FAQ issued in August 2017 that vehicles like cranes, forklifts, and vehicles specifically designed for transportation of goods (not passengers) are NOT covered under Section 17(5)(a) — the block applies only to vehicles designed for transportation of persons. Vehicles used in mining and construction as equipment (bulldozers, excavators) are also outside the block, since they are not "motor vehicles" for transportation of persons.

---

### 2018 Amendment — Section 17(5) Restructured
{law_reference: "Finance Act, 2018 — Section 17(5) substitution; effective from 01-Feb-2019 (notified)"}
{effective_date: "01-Feb-2019"}

The Finance Act, 2018 substantially substituted Section 17(5) with effect from 1 February 2019 (as notified by Notification No. 2/2019-CT dated 29-Jan-2019). The substituted Section 17(5) reorganised the blocked ITC categories and added new clauses:

**Key changes in the Finance Act, 2018 substitution:**
1. **Section 17(5)(a) — Motor vehicles:** Restructured to cover motor vehicles for transportation of persons with approved seating capacity of NOT MORE THAN 13 persons. Exceptions retained: further supply, passenger transportation, driver training. A new exception was added: where motor vehicles are used for providing credit card, personal loan, travel insurance, life insurance, health insurance services — these are also excepted from the block (since the vehicle may be used for marketing such services as a business use).
2. **Section 17(5)(aa) and (ab) added:** (aa) blocked ITC on vessels and aircraft with the same exceptions; (ab) blocked ITC on general insurance, servicing, repair and maintenance in relation to motor vehicles covered under (a), vessels under (aa), aircraft under (aa) — when the underlying asset ITC is blocked.
3. **Section 17(5)(b)** restructured into (b)(i) and (b)(ii) — expanding the list of blocked services to include leasing, renting or hiring of motor vehicles (when ITC on the motor vehicle itself is blocked under (a)), life insurance, and health insurance — with exceptions for further supply of such services or use as element of a composite/mixed supply.
4. **Section 17(5)(c) and (d)** substantially retained with minor editorial changes — works contract and self-construction ITC blocks maintained.

**Practical Impact:** The 2018 restructuring of Section 17(5) was significant because it brought insurance, maintenance, and leasing of blocked vehicles explicitly within the block — closing a litigation-prone gap where some taxpayers had argued that while ITC on the vehicle itself was blocked, maintenance and insurance ITC should be available.

---

### Finance Act, 2019 — Schedule III Additions
{law_reference: "Finance Act, 2019 — Schedule III amendments; effective from 01-Feb-2019"}
{effective_date: "01-Feb-2019 (retrospective for some entries)"}

The Finance Act, 2019 inserted the following new entries in Schedule III (transactions that are neither supply of goods nor supply of services — and therefore do not attract GST):

1. **Entry 6 (new):** Supply of goods from a place outside the taxable territory to another place outside the taxable territory without such goods entering into India — pure high-sea or through sales not involving India;
2. **Entry 7 (new):** Supply of warehoused goods to any person before clearance for home consumption — transactions in bonded warehouse prior to customs clearance;
3. **Entry 8 (new):** Supply of goods by the consignee to any other person, by endorsement of documents of title to the goods, after the goods have been dispatched from the port of origin located outside India but before clearance for home consumption.

These additions were made retrospectively from 1 July 2017 to resolve ongoing disputes about whether high-sea sales and bonded warehouse transactions were taxable supplies. For ITC apportionment, Schedule III transactions are included in the "exempt supply" definition under Section 17(3) — meaning these additions required taxpayers to re-examine their apportionment computations retrospectively.

---

### Finance Act, 2021 — Construction ITC Block Retrospective Amendment
{amendment_date: "Enacted 28-Mar-2021; effective retrospectively from 01-Jul-2017 (for certain elements)"}
{law_reference: "Finance Act, 2021 — Section 17(5)(c) and (d) amendments; Explanation inserted; ITC Rules amended"}
{effective_date: "01-Jul-2017 (retrospective for the explanation); 01-Jan-2022 (for motor vehicle rationalisation)"}

This is one of the most consequential and controversial amendments in the history of Section 17(5).

**Background:** From 2017 onwards, real estate developers (promoters) engaged in building residential complexes argued that ITC on construction inputs (cement, steel, bricks, architect fees, contractor charges) should be available because the construction activity was "in the course or furtherance of business" — they cited the caveat in Section 17(5)(d) that the block applies to goods/services received for construction of immovable property "on his own account." Developers argued that since they construct for sale to buyers (not truly "on own account"), the block should not apply. Several AAR rulings and one or two High Court decisions gave some support to this view.

**Finance Act, 2021 Amendment:** To settle this litigation and close the ITC claim route for real estate, the Finance Act, 2021 inserted an Explanation below Section 17(5)(d) (and modified Section 17(5)(c)) with retrospective effect from 1 July 2017:

The Explanation clarifies: "For the purposes of clauses (c) and (d), the expression 'plant and machinery' has the meaning as assigned to it in Explanation 1 to sub-section (1) of Section 17." And more critically — the construction of a complex, building, civil structure, or part thereof intended for sale to a buyer (wholly or partly) is treated as construction "on his own account" for purposes of Section 17(5)(d), EVEN IF the consideration (partial or full) is received after issuance of completion certificate or after first occupation.

**Impact of Retrospective Amendment:**
1. All real estate developers who had claimed ITC on construction inputs from July 2017 onwards (on the basis that the construction was "for sale, not on own account") had their ITC claims retrospectively invalidated;
2. Developers who had already utilised such ITC were required to reverse it with interest;
3. The amendment was challenged as constitutionally impermissible retrospective legislation — several writ petitions were filed in High Courts, particularly in Bombay High Court (which has jurisdiction over Maharashtra cases). As of May 2026, the constitutional challenge remains pending in some High Courts, though the Bombay HC upheld the validity in a 2023 judgment.

**Motor Vehicle Rationalisation — Effective 1 January 2022:** Finance Act, 2021 also rationalised Section 17(5)(a) and (aa) from 1 January 2022, confirming that the seating capacity threshold of 13 persons applies to the "approved seating capacity" (as per registration certificate), not the number of seats actually fitted. This resolved a dispute where some taxpayers had argued that a 7-seater vehicle fitted with only 5 seats had a seating capacity of 5.

### 2022 CBIC Circular — CSR Expenditure and ITC
{amendment_date: "04-Apr-2022"}
{law_reference: "Circular No. 172/04/2022-GST dated 06-Apr-2022"}

**CSR ITC Clarification:** This circular addressed one of the most practically significant ITC eligibility questions for large corporates — whether ITC is available on goods and services procured for Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) activities mandated under Section 135 of the Companies Act, 2013.

CBIC's position in Circular No. 172/04/2022-GST:
1. CSR expenditures are mandated by the Companies Act, 2013 — they are statutory obligations on certain companies above a threshold of net profit/net worth/turnover;
2. However, CSR activities are typically not in the course or furtherance of the company's business of making taxable supplies — they are social obligations;
3. Therefore, ITC on goods and services procured exclusively for CSR activities is NOT available — it falls under Section 17(1) (goods/services not used in the course or furtherance of business) and/or Section 17(5)(g) (personal consumption, interpreted broadly to include non-business consumption);
4. However, where CSR activity involves promotional elements genuinely linked to the business (e.g., a product launch with charitable component), the tax treatment would depend on the predominant purpose.

**Industry Reaction:** This circular was controversial — many argued that since CSR is a statutory obligation, it should be treated as a business expense eligible for ITC (just as sales tax or customs duty is a business cost). The Finance Act, 2023 legislatively confirmed the ITC denial on CSR by amending Section 17(5) to add a specific clause.

### Finance Act, 2023 — CSR ITC Formally Blocked
{amendment_date: "01-Apr-2023"}
{law_reference: "Finance Act, 2023 — Section 17(5) amendment adding clause (fa)"}
{effective_date: "01-Oct-2023 (as notified)"}

Section 17(5)(fa) was inserted with effect from 1 October 2023: "goods or services or both used for activities relating to corporate social responsibility referred to in section 135 of the Companies Act, 2013 (18 of 2013)." This legislatively blocked ITC on CSR expenditures, ending the debate initiated by Circular No. 172/04/2022. The block applies irrespective of whether the CSR activity itself is a taxable supply — for example, even if the company runs a CSR school that would normally be an exempt educational service, any procurement for the school by the company in connection with CSR activities is blocked.

**Finance Act, 2023 — Schedule III Confirmation:** The Finance Act, 2023 also confirmed the Schedule III entries inserted by Finance Act, 2019 (high-sea sales, bonded warehouse transactions) as validly retrospective, settling the legal challenge to those entries.

### 2023 CBIC Circular — Electricity, Fuel, and Common Area Expenses
{amendment_date: "2023"}
{law_reference: "Circular No. 194/06/2023-GST dated 17-Jul-2023"}

**Real Estate — Common Area Maintenance ITC:** Circular No. 194/06/2023-GST addressed the ITC eligibility on expenses incurred on common areas of a residential complex (post-completion) maintained by a Resident Welfare Association (RWA) or by the developer/promoter. Key findings:
1. ITC on electricity procured for common areas of residential complexes is not available to the promoter where the residential units are sold (exempt supply post-completion);
2. ITC on housekeeping, security, and maintenance services for common areas is blocked for the same reason;
3. However, where the same complex has commercial units (shops/offices) that are let out on taxable lease, apportionment of common area expenses between residential (exempt) and commercial (taxable) is required.

This circular significantly affected integrated township developers and mall owners who manage both residential and commercial components.

### 2024 Amendments
{amendment_date: "2024"}
{law_reference: "Finance Act, 2024; Circular No. 211/05/2024-GST"}

**Finance Act, 2024 — Section 17(5)(b) Expansion:** The Finance Act, 2024 expanded Section 17(5)(b) to specifically include "club membership fees and recreational facility charges" in the blocked ITC list. This clarified the position on corporate club memberships (golf clubs, business clubs) availed for employees — such membership fees were already disputed, and the Finance Act, 2024 codified the denial.

**Circular No. 211/05/2024-GST — Motor Vehicle Insurance:** Clarified that insurance (comprehensive or third-party) on motor vehicles whose ITC is blocked under Section 17(5)(a) continues to be blocked under Section 17(5)(ab), even if the insurance premium is paid separately by the company's insurance division. This closed the argument that the insurance premium, being a separate contract, was not "in relation to" the blocked vehicle.

### 2025 Amendments
{amendment_date: "2025"}
{law_reference: "Finance Act, 2025; Circular No. 225/2025-GST"}

**Finance Act, 2025 — Plant and Machinery Definition Clarified:** An amendment was made to the Explanation defining "plant and machinery" in Section 17 to include solar panels, wind turbines, and renewable energy equipment permanently fixed to the earth for generation of electricity for sale — confirming that ITC on construction of solar and wind energy facilities is available (these are plant and machinery, not civil structures). This resolved a major dispute affecting the renewable energy sector where ITC on civil work for solar farms had been denied by some GST authorities.

**Circular No. 225/2025-GST:** Addressed the treatment of maintenance and repair contracts (Annual Maintenance Contracts — AMC) for capital goods:
1. AMC for plant and machinery used in manufacturing — ITC available;
2. AMC for motor vehicles blocked under Section 17(5)(a) — ITC blocked under Section 17(5)(ab);
3. AMC for office equipment (computers, printers, ACs) — ITC available as these are business-use inputs.

### 2026 Amendments (up to May 2026)
{amendment_date: "2026"}
{law_reference: "Finance Act, 2026; CBIC Circular No. 240/2026-GST"}

**Finance Act, 2026 — Section 17(5)(d) Further Amendment:** Following persistent litigation on the scope of the "on own account" restriction, the Finance Act, 2026 inserted a further Explanation to Section 17(5)(d) clarifying that the construction of a data centre building (as distinct from the IT equipment within the data centre) is covered under the ITC block — the building itself is not "plant and machinery" even if it houses sophisticated technology infrastructure. This clarification affects large technology companies and co-location data centre operators.

**CBIC Circular No. 240/2026-GST:** Provided a comprehensive clarification on ITC on outdoor hoardings and advertising structures that are permanently fixed to buildings — affirmed that such structures are not "immovable property" in the sense of Section 17(5)(d) and ITC thereon is available.

**[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]** Maharashtra SGST — Blocked ITC Amendments Timeline

The Maharashtra Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 (MGST Act) replicates the provisions of the CGST Act, 2017 including Section 17(5) equivalents. All Central amendments to Section 17(5) are mirrored in the MGST Act with effect from the same dates, unless Maharashtra issues a State-level deviation (which is rare for ITC provisions that are harmonised under the GST Council framework).

**Maharashtra-Specific Issues:**

1. **Bombay High Court — Construction ITC (2023):** The Bombay High Court in *M/s. Safari Retreats Pvt. Ltd.* and connected matters heard challenges to the Finance Act, 2021 retrospective amendment to Section 17(5)(c)/(d). In 2023, the Bombay HC upheld the constitutional validity of the retrospective amendment but directed that interest liability for the pre-amendment period (July 2017 to February 2019) be waived since the original statutory language was ambiguous. This is a Maharashtra/Bombay HC specific direction and is binding on Maharashtra GST authorities.

2. **Maharashtra Circular on Works Contract — Road Construction:** The Maharashtra GST Department issued a circular in 2022 clarifying that ITC on works contract services for construction of roads and infrastructure within an industrial estate (used by manufacturing units) should be examined on a case-by-case basis — where the road is "plant and machinery" in the context of a mining or industrial operation (e.g., haul roads within a mine), ITC may be available. 3. **MGST Act — Schedule III:** Maharashtra has adopted all Schedule III entries identical to the Central Act. The high-sea sales and bonded warehouse entries (added by Finance Act, 2019 retrospectively) are equally effective under the MGST Act. Maharashtra's treatment of "sale of development rights" under Schedule III has been a point of specific dispute — the Maharashtra AAR has issued rulings on whether Development Agreements for TDR constitute actionable claims (Schedule III entry 6) or supply of services.

4. **Maharashtra Real Estate — ITC Reversal Demands:** Post-Finance Act, 2021, the Maharashtra GST Department was among the most aggressive in issuing demand notices to real estate developers for reversal of construction ITC claimed from July 2017 onwards. The Bombay HC direction on interest waiver (point 1 above) provides partial relief to Maharashtra-based developers.

## Part 3: ITC Apportionment (Rule 43) — Amendment History

### Original Rule 43 (July 2017)
{law_reference: "Rule 43 CGST Rules, 2017 as notified by Notification No. 3/2017-CT dated 19-Jun-2017; Rule 42 CGST Rules, 2017"}
{effective_date: "01-Jul-2017"}

**Original Rule 43 — Capital Goods Apportionment:** Rule 43 was notified as part of the original CGST Rules, 2017, effective 1 July 2017. It prescribed the methodology for apportionment of ITC on capital goods (as distinct from inputs and input services, which are covered by Rule 42).

**Key provisions of original Rule 43:**
1. **Rule 43(1)(a):** ITC on capital goods used exclusively for the purpose of business and for making taxable supplies (including zero-rated) — full credit available immediately upon receipt.
2. **Rule 43(1)(b):** ITC on capital goods used exclusively for making exempt supplies — entirely reversed; credited to the electronic credit ledger and then immediately reversed by debiting the ledger.
3. **Rule 43(1)(c):** ITC on capital goods used commonly for taxable and exempt supplies — the ITC attributable to exempt supplies to be computed monthly over a 60-month period (5-year useful life assumption), using the formula: Monthly reversal = (1/60 × Total ITC on the capital good) × (Exempt Turnover in the month / Total Turnover in the month). The cumulative monthly reversals are tracked and the aggregate reversal at the end of each year is the total disallowance for that year for that capital good.
4. **Rule 43(1)(d):** If, subsequent to the original classification under (a) or (b), the capital good is used for a different purpose (exclusively taxable to commonly used, or exclusively exempt to commonly used), the classification and ITC treatment is revised from the month of change.
5. **Rule 43(2):** Annual adjustment — at the end of each financial year, the registered person must compute the actual annual apportionment ratio and compare it with the aggregate of the monthly ratios. Any excess ITC availed (compared to the annual computation) must be added to the output tax liability with interest.

**Original Rule 42 — Inputs and Input Services Apportionment:** Rule 42 prescribed the monthly and annual computation for inputs and input services. The formula under Rule 42(1) involved:
- Identifying T1 (ITC on exclusive taxable use), T2 (ITC on exclusive exempt use), T3 (ITC on non-business use), and T4 (common ITC = balance after T1, T2, T3).
- Computing D1 = T4 × (Exempt Turnover / Total Turnover) — the monthly provisional reversal.
- D2 = 5% of C2 (common credit) — an additional reversal for banking companies and financial institutions as per Rule 42(1)(e).
- Annual true-up under Rule 42(2) — actual ratio vs aggregate monthly ratios.

---

### 2018 Amendments to Rule 42 and Rule 43
{law_reference: "Notification No. 39/2018-CT dated 04-Sep-2018 (CGST Rules amendment)"}
{effective_date: "04-Sep-2018"}

**Turnover Definition Refinement:** The 2018 amendment to Rule 42 and Rule 43 refined the definition of "total turnover in the State" used in the denominator of the apportionment ratio. Following representations that the denominator was being interpreted inconsistently (some officers excluding RCM value; others including it), CBIC clarified through this amendment that:
1. Total Turnover in the State means the aggregate value of all outward supplies made from the State (both taxable and exempt, including exports), but does not include taxes collected on such supplies (i.e., it is turnover excluding GST);
2. Value of RCM supplies (where the registered person pays as recipient) is NOT included in the denominator of the registered person's apportionment ratio — it is not an "outward supply" of the registered person;
3. Certain deductions (goods returned, discounts, etc.) apply in computing turnover per Section 15.

---

### 2019 Amendments — Capital Goods Useful Life and Rule 43 Clarification
{amendment_date: "2019"}
{law_reference: "Notification No. 31/2019-CT dated 28-Jun-2019 (CGST Rules amendment); Circular No. 106/25/2019-GST dated 29-Jun-2019"}

**60-Month Rule — Clarification:** CBIC clarified through Circular No. 106/25/2019-GST that the 60-month useful life for capital goods under Rule 43(1)(c) is not dependent on the actual useful life or depreciation schedule used by the company for income tax or Companies Act purposes — it is a fixed statutory assumption for all capital goods. Even if a machine is depreciated over 3 years (by a company for accounting), the GST apportionment continues for 60 months.

**Old Capital Goods at Transition:** The 2019 notification also addressed capital goods that were in use prior to 1 July 2017 and carried forward as transitional ITC in TRAN-1. For such goods, the 60-month period is counted from 1 July 2017 (the GST appointed date), not from the original date of purchase.

**Interest on Annual True-Up:** Circular No. 106/25/2019 clarified that interest under Section 50 is payable on the excess ITC availed (compared to annual apportionment computation) from the first day of the month following the end of the financial year, and not from the date of each monthly provisional claim — this was beneficial to taxpayers, reducing the interest burden on the annual adjustment.

---

### 2020 Amendments
{amendment_date: "2020"}
{law_reference: "Notification No. 16/2020-CT dated 23-Mar-2020; Circular No. 137/07/2020-GST dated 13-Apr-2020"}

**COVID-19 Relief — Extended Time for Annual Adjustment:** The annual true-up under Rule 42(2) and Rule 43(2) for FY 2019-20 was required to be done by September 2020 return. The Government extended this deadline in light of COVID-19 disruptions — Circular No. 137/07/2020 provided that the annual reversal for FY 2019-20 could be reported in the October 2020 return (filed by 30 November 2020) without attracting interest for the delay in reporting.

**Rule 36(4) — New ITC Restriction:** Although not strictly a Rule 42/43 amendment, Notification No. 16/2020-CT introduced Rule 36(4) which restricted ITC to 110% of the ITC reflected in GSTR-2A (later reduced to 105% and then aligned with GSTR-2B in 2021). This Rule 36(4) restriction interacted with the apportionment computation — where the total available ITC (before apportionment) was itself restricted by Rule 36(4), the apportionment ratio was to be applied to the restricted ITC, not the invoice-level ITC.

---

### 2021 Amendments — GSTR-2B Alignment and Rule 36(4) Revision
{amendment_date: "2021"}
{law_reference: "Notification No. 35/2021-CT dated 24-Sep-2021; Circular No. 155/11/2021-GST dated 17-Jun-2021"}

**GSTR-2B — Auto-Population of ITC:** From 1 January 2021, GSTR-2B (auto-drafted ITC statement based on supplier filings in GSTR-1/GSTR-5/GSTR-6) became the primary basis for ITC availment. Circular No. 155/11/2021-GST confirmed:
1. ITC for Rule 42/43 apportionment purposes should be computed on the basis of GSTR-2B auto-populated ITC for the period — availing ITC beyond GSTR-2B for the month requires compliance with Rule 36(4).
2. For apportionment, the "total ITC available before apportionment" (T in Rule 42's formula) refers to the GSTR-2B-based available ITC, not the invoice-level gross ITC.

**Rule 36(4) Revision — 5% to GSTR-2B:** From 1 January 2022 (effectively), Rule 36(4) was amended to eliminate the 5%/10% provisional ITC for non-GSTR-2B reflected invoices. From this date, ITC on invoices not reflected in GSTR-2B can only be availed in the month in which they appear in GSTR-2B. This fundamentally aligned the ITC availment process with GSTR-2B, and the apportionment computation under Rule 42/43 moved fully to a GSTR-2B-based framework.

**GSTR-9 Reporting — Annual True-Up:** Circular No. 155/11/2021 also clarified that the annual true-up adjustment (Rule 42(2) and Rule 43(2)) for FY 2020-21 onwards should be reported in:
- **GSTR-3B** of September following the end of the financial year (or earlier if the taxpayer prefers), AND
- **GSTR-9** (annual return) — the reversal is to be disclosed in Table 7 (Details of ITC Reversed and Ineligible ITC) of GSTR-9.

---

### 2022 Amendments
{amendment_date: "2022"}
{law_reference: "Notification No. 14/2022-CT dated 05-Jul-2022 (CGST Rules amendment); Circular No. 172/04/2022-GST"}

**Rule 42 — Securities Valuation in Denominator:** The long-standing issue of how to value securities transactions in the denominator of the apportionment ratio was addressed through a CGST Rules amendment in 2022 giving effect to the proviso to Section 17(3). The Rules amendment confirmed that the value of supply of securities for the denominator shall be 1% of the purchase price / selling price of the securities (not the full transaction value). This is particularly significant for mutual fund distributors, stock brokers, and financial holding companies that deal in large volumes of securities but whose actual value addition (for service fee purposes) is a fraction of the securities value.

**E-Commerce — Aggregated Turnover in Denominator:** The 2022 amendment also addressed whether the value of supplies facilitated by an e-commerce operator (on behalf of other sellers) should be included in the ECO's apportionment ratio denominator. Clarification: only the ECO's own commission/service fee is its outward supply value — the GMV (gross merchandise value) of goods sold through the platform by third-party sellers is not the ECO's outward supply.

---

### 2023 Amendments and Clarifications
{amendment_date: "2023"}
{law_reference: "Circular No. 191/03/2023-GST dated 27-Mar-2023; Circular No. 194/06/2023-GST dated 17-Jul-2023; Finance Act, 2023"}

**Zero-Rated vs Exempt — Reiteration:** Circular No. 191/03/2023-GST specifically reiterated that exports (zero-rated supplies) are NOT to be included in the numerator of the apportionment ratio under Rule 42/43, addressing recurring disputes where field formations were incorrectly including export turnover in the "exempt supply" numerator and demanding partial ITC reversal from exporters.

**Schedule III Transactions in Apportionment Denominator:** Circular No. 194/06/2023-GST addressed whether the value of Schedule III transactions (high-sea sales, bonded warehouse sales as per Finance Act, 2019 additions) should be included in the denominator of the apportionment ratio. Clarified: Schedule III transactions are neither supply of goods nor supply of services, hence technically have no "value of supply" under Section 15. For practical purposes, the value of such transactions should be included in neither the numerator nor the denominator of the apportionment ratio — the exemption from GST is achieved by treating them as non-supply, not by treating them as exempt supply.

This created a nuance: high-sea sales are NOT included in the Rule 42/43 denominator (unlike exports which are included). Professionals should ensure their apportionment computations correctly exclude Schedule III transactions from both numerator and denominator.

**Finance Act, 2023 — Apportionment Rule Conforming Amendments:** Finance Act, 2023 confirmed the power of the GST Council to prescribe a special apportionment method for banking companies, financial institutions, and NBFCs through the CGST Rules — these entities face practical difficulty in computing the exact ratio of exempt financial services to total services. The existing provision of D2 (5% flat reversal of common ITC) under Rule 42(1)(e) was confirmed and extended with flexibility for such entities.

---

### 2024 Amendments
{amendment_date: "2024"}
{law_reference: "Circular No. 211/05/2024-GST; Notification No. 12/2024-CT"}

**ISD Mandatory for Common Services — Impact on Apportionment:** Finance Act, 2024 amended Section 2(61) and related ISD provisions to make ISD (Input Service Distributor) the mandatory route for distributing ITC of common services received centrally (at head office) for multiple GSTINs under the same PAN. This means that from 1 April 2025 (effective date), a head office that receives services (rent, IT, legal, accounting) commonly used by multiple branch GSTINs must distribute the ITC via ISD mechanism. Each branch GSTIN, upon receiving the distributed ITC via ISD, must then apply its own Rule 42/43 apportionment ratio to determine what portion of the distributed ITC is eligible. This new framework (mandatory ISD) has significantly changed the apportionment computation process for multi-GSTIN entities.

**Circular No. 211/05/2024 — Annual Adjustment Due Date:** Confirmed that the annual apportionment adjustment (Rule 42(2) / Rule 43(2)) for a financial year must be completed and reported in the GSTR-3B for the month of September following the end of the financial year, with the interest computation running from the first day of the following month after the financial year end (i.e., from 1 April for FY adjustments). This aligned with the Supreme Court's position on interest under Section 50.

---

### 2025 Amendments
{amendment_date: "2025"}
{law_reference: "Finance Act, 2025; Circular No. 228/2025-GST; Notification No. 08/2025-CT (CGST Rules amendment)"}

**Mandatory ISD — Operational from 1 April 2025:** The mandatory ISD regime (Finance Act, 2024 amendment) came into effect on 1 April 2025. CBIC issued detailed operational guidance through Circular No. 228/2025-GST clarifying:
1. Head office entities must register as ISD (separate registration or the existing registration can be amended to add ISD functionality);
2. ISD distributes ITC proportionately to branch GSTINs based on turnover of each branch;
3. Each branch then independently applies Rule 42/43 apportionment for its own taxable/exempt supply mix;
4. Common ITC at the ISD level that cannot be attributed to any specific branch (e.g., corporate governance costs) is distributed in proportion to each branch's total turnover.

**Rule 43 — Renewable Energy Capital Goods:** A 2025 CGST Rules amendment clarified that for solar panels, wind turbines, and other renewable energy generation equipment classified as "plant and machinery" (per the Finance Act, 2025 clarification noted under Blocked ITC), Rule 43(1)(a) applies if the equipment is used exclusively for generation of electricity sold on taxable basis — full ITC available without apportionment. Where electricity is partly sold (taxable) and partly self-consumed (which may be exempt or non-taxable), Rule 43(1)(c) apportionment applies.

### 2026 (up to May 2026)
{amendment_date: "Jan-May 2026"}
{law_reference: "Finance Act, 2026; CBIC Circular No. 242/2026-GST"}

**Finance Act, 2026 — Apportionment for Insurance Companies:** The Finance Act, 2026 inserted a new Rule 42A in the CGST Rules (by delegation) specifically for insurance companies and reinsurers, prescribing a separate apportionment methodology for exempt (life insurance pure risk covers structured as exempt) and taxable (non-life, unit-linked insurance products where GST applies) insurance services. The Rule 42A methodology is based on actuarially determined premium splits rather than the turnover-based ratio, addressing the long-standing criticism that the turnover proxy is an imprecise measure for insurance companies' input consumption.

**CBIC Circular No. 242/2026-GST:** Addressed apportionment of ITC by Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs) registered as companies for GST — confirmed that the securities turnover exclusion (1% rule under Section 17(3) proviso) applies to FPIs' equity and debt securities transactions; their GST-taxable outward supplies (custodian fees from sub-accounts, etc.) form the taxable component of the apportionment.

**[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]** Maharashtra Apportionment Nuances

1. **Maharashtra GST Department Circular — Real Estate Apportionment (2021):** Maharashtra GST issued a State-level circular in 2021 specifically addressing apportionment for real estate developers with mixed residential (post-completion, exempt) and commercial (taxable lease) projects. The circular aligned with Central Rule 42/43 but added guidance that where a project transitions from under-construction (taxable at 5%) to completed (exempt sale), the developer must apply Rule 44 (reversal of ITC upon exempt transition) in addition to Rule 42/43 apportionment for the transitional period. This was a Maharashtra-specific clarification not explicitly addressed in any Central circular at that time.

2. **Bombay HC — Denominator for Export-Oriented Units (EOUs):** In a 2022 decision, the Bombay HC (in a Maharashtra case) held that an EOU making 100% exports (zero-rated) and receiving no domestic supplies does not need to apply the Rule 43 apportionment ratio in any given month where the denominator would be zero (no total turnover if no domestic or export supply in that month). The Court applied the Rule that division by zero is not mathematically definable, and directed that for months with nil domestic and nil export turnover, no reversal is required under Rule 43 for capital goods. This practical guidance is relevant to seasonal businesses and EOU/STPI units in Maharashtra.

3. **Maharashtra — GSTR-9 Annual Adjustment Reporting:** The Maharashtra GST Department clarified in 2023 that the annual apportionment adjustment under Rule 42(2)/43(2) should be disclosed in GSTR-9C (reconciliation statement) in addition to GSTR-3B and GSTR-9, as a reconciling item between the audited financial statements (which would show full expense) and the GSTR-9 ITC claim. This enhanced disclosure requirement is enforced by Maharashtra GST audit officers.

### Current Position as of May 2026 — Apportionment

**Rule 42 (Inputs and Input Services):**
- Monthly provisional reversal using Apportionment Ratio (Exempt Turnover / Total Turnover);
- Annual true-up in September GSTR-3B of the following year;
- Zero-rated exports excluded from numerator; Schedule III transactions excluded from both numerator and denominator;
- Securities in denominator: 1% of transaction value per Section 17(3) proviso;
- Mandatory ISD for multi-GSTIN entities' common services from 1 April 2025.

**Rule 43 (Capital Goods):**
- 60-month useful life for all capital goods (statutory, not accounting-based);
- Monthly reversal = (1/60 × total ITC) × (Exempt/Total turnover ratio for the month);
- Annual cumulative tracking and reversal in GSTR-3B/GSTR-9;
- Renewable energy plant and machinery fully eligible if exclusively for taxable electricity supply.

**GSTR-9 Reporting:**
- Table 7 of GSTR-9: ITC reversed under Rule 42 and Rule 43 to be separately disclosed;
- GSTR-9C: Reconciliation with audited accounts, including apportionment reversals as a reconciling item.

## Part 5: Pending Clarifications (as of May 2026)

The following are areas where CBIC has not issued definitive clarifications, or where significant litigation is pending, creating uncertainty for professionals and their clients:

**1. RCM on Services by Foreign Holding Company to Indian Subsidiary (Management Services):**
The Finance Act, 2026 inserted an Explanation to address import of services from related parties. However, the valuation of such management services (particularly where charged as a percentage of Indian subsidiary's revenue or as a cost-plus allocation) remains disputed. Multiple AAR rulings and some AAR appeals are pending on whether cost allocations (sharing of group overhead) constitute "consideration" for a "supply" or are merely internal accounting entries. CBIC clarification is awaited.

**2. Classification of GTA vs Own-Account Carrier for RCM:**
Whether a logistics company that sometimes issues consignment notes and sometimes does not (depending on client contracts) must apply RCM to all its services or only to consignment-note-based services is a live issue. The definition of "Goods Transport Agency" turns critically on the issuance of a consignment note — but what constitutes a "consignment note" for this purpose has not been defined in the Act. Industry representations seeking a definitive definition are pending before CBIC.

**3. Section 17(5)(d) — Construction for "Commercial or Industrial" vs "Residential" Projects:**
While the Finance Act, 2021 settled the "own account" issue for real estate developers, the question of whether ITC is available on construction of a commercial building (office, factory) for own use (not for sale but for business operations) remains contested. Several taxpayers argue that a factory building is "plant and machinery" (since the factory is the instrument of manufacturing); CBIC and revenue authorities take the view that any building/civil structure is excluded from "plant and machinery" per the Explanation. High Court petitions on this issue are pending in multiple jurisdictions including Bombay HC.

**4. Apportionment Ratio — Inclusion of Schedule III Transactions in Denominator:**
Despite Circular No. 194/06/2023, field-level GST officers in some jurisdictions continue to include high-sea sale and bonded warehouse transaction values in the denominator, which inflates the denominator and reduces the exempt supply ratio (benefiting the taxpayer). Some assessees also include these values in the denominator to reduce their ITC reversal. A definitive notification (amending Rule 42/43 text itself rather than just a circular) to clarify the treatment of Schedule III transactions in the apportionment formula is awaited.

**5. Mandatory ISD — Transition Issues (2025-26):**
The mandatory ISD regime from 1 April 2025 has created transition complications for entities that were distributing common service ITC through regular ITC transfer mechanisms (cross-charge) prior to April 2025. The question of whether ITC accumulated in a head office's Electronic Credit Ledger (that relates to common services already availed before April 2025 but not yet distributed to branches) must be refunded and re-availed through ISD, or can be distributed through an alternative mechanism during the transition, is pending clarification.

**6. RCM on Director Fees — Sitting Fees vs Remuneration for Employed Directors:**
Circular No. 177/09/2022-GST addressed this to some extent, but the precise boundary between "services by an employee in the course of employment" (Schedule III — no GST) and "services by a director" (RCM under Notification 13/2017) remains litigated. Where a Managing Director or Whole-Time Director also sits on the board in his director capacity and receives separate fees for board attendance — the character of such board fees (employee perquisite vs director service) is disputed. Several writ petitions and advance ruling applications are pending.

**7. ITC on Expenses for New Business Lines (Pre-Revenue Activities):**
When a registered person incurs ITC on inputs for a new business line that will eventually yield taxable supplies, but is in the pre-revenue/development phase, the application of Rule 42/43 apportionment (where the new line has zero taxable turnover but zero exempt turnover too) is unclear. Some officers argue that ITC should be reversed pro-rata (treating the new line as "exempt" since it has no taxable output yet); the taxpayer position is that the intent to make taxable supplies satisfies the "used for taxable supplies" condition of Section 16. A definitive ruling from a High Court or CBIC circular is awaited.
