## Who is Liable to Pay RCM
{law_reference: "Section 9(3), 9(4) CGST Act, 2017; Section 2(94) CGST Act, 2017 — definition of 'registered person'"}

The statutory liability to pay tax under RCM is imposed exclusively on the **recipient** of the supply. Both Section 9(3) and 9(4) use identical language: "the tax on which shall be paid on reverse charge basis by the recipient of such goods or services or both." The liability is not shared, not allocated proportionally, and not contingent on any action by the supplier; it vests entirely and unconditionally in the recipient from the moment the time of supply is triggered.

**The "Registered" Requirement:** Section 9(4) explicitly applies only where the recipient is a "registered person" within the meaning of Section 2(94) — a person registered under Section 25. For Section 9(3), the provision does not on its face restrict itself to registered recipients, but in practice the notifications under Section 9(3) are structured so that the recipient is typically a registered business entity. An individual receiving notified services for personal purposes (outside the course of business) does not, in most cases, fall within the ambit of Section 9(3) notifications.

**What if Both Parties are Registered?** In Section 9(3) scenarios (notified category RCM), it is entirely common for both parties to be registered. For example, a registered legal firm provides services to a registered manufacturing company: the firm is registered, the manufacturer is registered, yet RCM applies because the supply falls within the Section 9(3) notification for "legal services by an individual advocate or firm of advocates." In such cases:
- The supplier (law firm) does not charge GST on their invoice; they issue an invoice without GST or with a notation "Supply under Reverse Charge — Tax payable by recipient"
- The recipient (manufacturer) self-assesses and pays CGST+SGST or IGST as applicable
- The supplier has no GST liability on this supply but must report it in their GSTR-1 as a supply on which the recipient is liable to pay tax (Table 4B of GSTR-1)

**Dual Registration Scenarios:** A person may hold multiple GST registrations across states (mandatory state-wise registration under Section 25(1)). RCM liability is determined registration-wise: the GSTIN of the establishment that actually received the supply is the liable entity, regardless of other registrations the same legal person may hold.

## Composition Dealers and RCM
{law_reference: "Section 10 CGST Act, 2017; Section 10(4) CGST Act, 2017; Rule 4 CGST Rules, 2017; Circular No. 1/1/2017-CT dated 26.06.2017"}

Composition dealers — persons who opt for the composition scheme under Section 10 — occupy a unique and commercially disadvantageous position with respect to RCM.

**Obligation to Pay RCM:** A composition dealer is a "registered person" and therefore fully subject to the RCM obligations under Section 9(3) and 9(4). When a composition dealer receives services from a GTA, legal services from an advocate, security services from an unregistered security agency, or any other notified RCM category, the composition dealer must pay the applicable GST under reverse charge from the cash ledger. There is no exemption from RCM liability for composition dealers in their capacity as recipients.

**No ITC for Composition Dealers:** Section 10(4) of the CGST Act expressly prohibits composition taxpayers from claiming any Input Tax Credit. This applies equally to ITC arising from RCM payments. The result is that for a composition dealer:
- RCM tax is paid → cannot be recovered as ITC → becomes a permanent cost
- Under the regular GST regime, RCM tax paid → ITC available → net cost is zero (cash-flow timing apart)
- Under composition scheme, RCM tax = additional, irrecoverable tax cost

**Impact on Pricing and Cost Structure:** This creates a structural cost disadvantage for composition dealers relative to regular taxpayers when procuring services in notified RCM categories. A composition dealer in the restaurant business (1% composition levy) who engages a goods transport agency must pay 5% GST on RCM without any ITC offset. The effective tax cost on freight becomes 5% — an absolute cost increase. This must be factored into pricing or absorbed in margins.

**Practical Example:**
A composition dealer (retail trader, 1% composition rate) purchases goods worth ₹5,00,000 from local unregistered suppliers in a month and engages a GTA for transportation at ₹50,000 freight charges.

- Composition levy on turnover: ₹5,00,000 × 1% = ₹5,000 (paid from cash)
- RCM on GTA services: ₹50,000 × 5% = ₹2,500 (paid from cash — if GTA has not paid GST themselves)
- ITC on RCM: ₹0 (blocked by Section 10(4))
- Net additional cost due to RCM: ₹2,500 (permanent cost, not recoverable)

**Composition Dealer as Supplier — Not Subject to RCM:** When a composition dealer supplies goods or services to a registered recipient, the composition dealer pays composition levy on their turnover; the recipient does not pay RCM on such purchases. The composition dealer's supply does not attract Section 9(4) RCM on the recipient, because Section 9(4) applies to supplies from "unregistered" persons and a composition dealer is a registered person (even if under a special scheme). This is an important clarification that prevents double-counting of levy.

## Exempted Supplies and RCM
{law_reference: "Section 11 CGST Act, 2017; Section 2(47) CGST Act — 'exempt supply'; Rule 4 CGST Rules, 2017; Notification No. 12/2017-CT(Rate) dated 28.06.2017"}

**Core Principle:** RCM is a mechanism for collecting tax on a taxable supply. If the supply is exempt from GST, there is no taxable event, and therefore no RCM can arise. This principle flows from Rule 4 and the structure of the Act itself.

**Section 11 Exemptions:** Section 11 of the CGST Act empowers the Central Government to exempt, by notification, any goods or services or both from GST, either absolutely or subject to conditions. Notification No. 12/2017-CT(Rate) provides a comprehensive list of exempt services. Where a service falls within the exemption notification, even if the supply would otherwise fall within a Section 9(3) category, the exemption overrides and no RCM applies.

**Important Nuance — Conditional Exemptions:** Some exemptions are conditional. For example, services by a Goods Transport Agency where the consignment value is less than ₹1,500 for a single consignment or the total freight is less than ₹750 for all consignments transported in a single goods carriage is exempt. If the GTA's service falls below these thresholds, no RCM applies. Above the threshold, full RCM applies. There is no partial RCM on the excess — it is threshold-triggered: once exceeded, the full amount attracts RCM (not just the amount above the threshold).

**Partial Exemption — Mixed Taxable and Exempt Services:** Where a single supplier provides a bundle of services, some exempt and some taxable, the RCM applies only to the taxable portion. If the consideration is not separately stated, valuation rules under Section 15 and Rule 27 may need to be applied to determine the taxable portion.

**Government Services — Notification No. 12/2017:** Many services provided by government and local authorities to businesses are taxable under Section 9(3) RCM (recipient pays). However, Notification No. 12/2017-CT(Rate) exempts several government services, such as services by the Reserve Bank of India, services by a foreign diplomatic mission, services provided by a municipality by way of any activity in relation to any function entrusted to a municipality, and others. Where government services fall within Notification No. 12/2017, they are exempt and no RCM applies on the business recipient.

## Government Entities and Public Sector Undertakings
{law_reference: "Section 9(3) CGST Act, 2017; Notification No. 12/2017-CT(Rate) dated 28.06.2017; Notification No. 13/2017-CT(Rate); Section 2(53) CGST Act — 'Government'"}

**Government as Recipient:** When a government department or ministry is the recipient of a notified RCM service and if it is registered under GST (many government departments are registered for deducting TDS under Section 51 or for making RCM payments), RCM obligations apply to the government recipient in the same manner as any registered person. Government departments registered under Section 51 as TDS deductors are "registered persons" for RCM purposes.

**Notification No. 12/2017 — Service Recipient-Based Exemptions:** Several entries in Notification No. 12/2017 provide service-specific exemptions when services are provided "to the Central Government, State Government, Union Territory, local authority, governmental authority or government entity." For example, services provided to a government entity by way of construction of a school or hospital are exempt. In such cases, even if the supplier would otherwise be in a Section 9(3) category, the exemption applies and RCM does not arise.

**PSU Treatment — Regular Registered Person:** Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) incorporated as companies under the Companies Act are treated as regular registered persons for all GST purposes, including RCM. A PSU does not receive any special exemption or relaxation in RCM compliance by virtue of being a government undertaking. The PSU's procurement team must identify RCM supplies, issue self-invoices and payment vouchers, pay from cash ledger, and claim ITC on the same footing as any private sector company.

## SEZ Developers and Units
{law_reference: "Section 16(1) IGST Act, 2017; Section 2(5) and 2(6) IGST Act — definitions; Section 26 SEZ Act, 2005; Circular No. 48/22/2018-GST dated 14.06.2018"}

**Supply from DTA to SEZ — Zero-Rated Status:** Under Section 16(1)(b) of the IGST Act, supply of goods or services or both to a SEZ developer or SEZ unit for authorised operations is treated as a "zero-rated supply." The DTA supplier is entitled to supply without GST (on a Letter of Undertaking/Bond) or supply with IGST and claim refund.

**RCM in SEZ Context:** When an SEZ unit or developer receives services that would otherwise attract RCM under Section 9(3) (e.g., GTA services for transportation within or to the SEZ, security services from an individual), the SEZ unit, being a registered person, is liable to pay RCM. However, the question of ITC differs:
- SEZ units can claim ITC of IGST paid under RCM for use in authorised operations
- Alternatively, SEZ units can claim refund of such IGST under Section 54 as a zero-rated supply if the procurement is for further zero-rated supplies

**Import of Services by SEZ:** When an SEZ unit imports services from a foreign supplier (offshore software development, management consulting, etc.), IGST applies on RCM basis under Section 5(3) IGST Act. The IGST so paid is available as ITC for the SEZ unit's domestic GST liabilities or eligible for refund if exclusively used for zero-rated authorised operations.

**DTA Supply to SEZ — Mis-Classification Risk:** A common compliance error is treating DTA-to-SEZ supplies as regular domestic supplies and charging CGST+SGST instead of treating them as zero-rated under IGST. If the DTA supplier charges CGST+SGST, the SEZ unit pays those taxes (or the RCM applies incorrectly), and the SEZ unit cannot claim refund of incorrectly charged CGST+SGST. Circular No. 48/22/2018-GST clarified that IGST (not CGST+SGST) is the correct levy for supplies to SEZ.

## Threshold-Based Exceptions
{law_reference: "Section 9(4) CGST Act, 2017 as amended by CGST (Amendment) Act, 2018; Notification No. 07/2019-CT(Rate) dated 29.03.2019"}

**Historical Aggregate Threshold Under Old Section 9(4):** Under the original Section 9(4) (operative from 01.07.2017 to the 2018 suspension), there was no value-based threshold within the provision itself — every purchase from an unregistered supplier by a registered person attracted RCM, regardless of amount. The GSTN portal and compliance forms required tracking even ₹100 purchases from the local tea vendor.

**Current Position — Category-Based, Not Threshold-Based:** The amended Section 9(4) (post-01.02.2019) moves away from threshold-based inclusion to category-based inclusion. The government notifies specific categories of services (currently principally the author-to-publisher services under Notification No. 07/2019-CT(Rate)) where unregistered supplier RCM applies. For categories not covered by the notification, Section 9(4) does not apply at all — irrespective of the transaction value.

**Whether Aggregate Annual Value Threshold Affects Section 9(4) Liability:** As of May 2026, there is no separate aggregate annual value threshold carving out Section 9(4) liability for small transactions within a notified category. If a transaction falls within a notified category, full RCM applies even for a single small-value transaction. The threshold concept applies only to the supplier's registration (if the unregistered supplier's aggregate turnover remains below the Section 22 threshold, they remain unregistered, which is what triggers Section 9(4) in the first place — but the Section 9(4) RCM on the recipient has no independent value threshold).

**Relevance of Suspension History:** The 2017-2019 suspension history is relevant to: (a) assessments covering those periods, where no Section 9(4) liability arose; (b) understanding the legislative policy shift away from a broad catch-all approach; and (c) distinguishing pre-amendment and post-amendment compliance obligations when advising on due diligence covering multiple years.

## Nil-Rated vs Exempt Distinction for RCM
{law_reference: "Section 2(47) CGST Act — 'exempt supply'; Section 2(78) CGST Act — 'nil rated supply'; Section 17(2) CGST Act; Rule 4 CGST Rules, 2017"}

This distinction is among the most frequently misunderstood areas in GST practice and has direct implications for RCM applicability.

**Nil-Rated Supply (Section 2(78)):** A nil-rated supply is a taxable supply on which the rate of GST is 0% under Schedule I of the GST rate notifications. The supply is NOT exempt — it is taxable, but at zero rate. Suppliers of nil-rated goods/services are required to register (if crossing the turnover threshold), file returns, and issue tax invoices (at 0% rate). ITC is not available for inputs used in making nil-rated supplies under Section 17(2).

**Exempt Supply (Section 2(47)):** An exempt supply is one on which GST is not levied at all — by virtue of a Section 11 notification or a Section 6 notification (IGST). Suppliers of purely exempt supplies are not required to register (Section 23(1)(a)), provided they make no taxable supplies. Exempt supplies are not included in "aggregate turnover" for some purposes but are for others (Section 2(6) includes exempt supplies in aggregate turnover for registration threshold).

**RCM Impact of the Distinction:**

| Scenario | Nature | RCM Applicable? | Reasoning |
|----------|--------|----------------|-----------|
| Nil-rated goods from registered supplier | Taxable at 0% | No (9(4) doesn't apply; supplier is registered) | Supplier is registered; Section 9(4) targets unregistered |
| Nil-rated goods from unregistered supplier | Taxable at 0% | Technical point: supply is taxable, 0% rate — theoretically RCM applies but tax payable is zero | Practical impact is nil |
| Exempt services (e.g., healthcare) | Not taxable | No | No taxable supply exists; Rule 4 exclusion applies |
| Supply that is both nil-rated and exempt in one invoice | Mixed | No on nil-rated portion (0% tax); No on exempt portion (no taxable event) | Neither generates a GST outflow |

**Practical Example:** A pharmaceutical company (registered) purchases raw medicinal herbs from an unregistered tribal cultivator. Raw medicinal herbs may be nil-rated or exempt depending on the specific item. If exempt: no RCM. If nil-rated: technically an RCM supply, but GST at 0% means no actual tax payment. Practitioners must check the specific HSN to determine nil-rated vs. exempt classification.

**ITC Implications:** Even if RCM results in zero tax (nil-rated), the self-invoice should be issued and reported in GSTR-3B Table 3.1(d) at zero value, to maintain completeness of the audit trail and to avoid scrutiny queries about unreported inward supplies from unregistered persons.

## Exclusion Matrix — When RCM Does NOT Apply
{law_reference: "Rule 4 CGST Rules, 2017; Section 9(3), 9(4), 10, 11 CGST Act, 2017; Section 16 IGST Act, 2017"}

| Scenario | Governing Section/Rule | RCM Applicable? | Reason |
|----------|----------------------|----------------|--------|
| Supplier is registered (Section 9(4) scenario only) | Section 9(4) | No | Section 9(4) applies only to supplies from unregistered persons; registered supplier triggers forward charge |
| Supply is exempt under Section 11 | Section 11; Rule 4 | No | Exempt supply — no taxable event; RCM cannot arise without a taxable supply |
| Supply is nil-rated (0% GST) | GST Rate Notification | Technically applies, but tax = ₹0 | Supply is taxable at 0%; RCM technically triggered but no financial impact |
| Composition dealer is the supplier | Section 10 | No (Section 9(4) doesn't apply) | Composition dealer is registered; Section 9(4) is only for unregistered suppliers |
| Recipient is not a registered person (Section 9(4)) | Section 9(4) | No | Section 9(4) requires recipient to be a registered person |
| Supply is by way of export (zero-rated) | Section 16(1)(a) IGST | No | Export is zero-rated at the supplier level; RCM does not apply to outbound exports |
| Supply received by an SEZ for authorised operations | Section 16(1)(b) IGST | Depends on category | RCM applies if the supply falls under Section 9(3); but for Section 9(4), SEZ is registered and relevant category must be notified |
| Supply not in a notified category (Section 9(4)) | Section 9(4) as amended | No | Post-2019 amendment: Section 9(4) applies only to categories specifically notified; all other unregistered supplier purchases are outside RCM |
| GTA transporting exempt goods (e.g., agricultural produce) | Notification 12/2017-CT(Rate) | No | The underlying exemption extends to the transportation service |
| Services below conditional exemption threshold | Notification 12/2017-CT(Rate) | No | Threshold-based exemption in the service notification extinguishes RCM liability |
| Supplies to unregistered recipients (Section 9(4) RCM) | Section 9(4) | No | Recipient must be registered for Section 9(4) to apply |
| Petroleum crude, high speed diesel, motor spirit, natural gas, aviation turbine fuel | Section 9(2) CGST | No GST at all currently | These goods are outside the GST levy as per Section 9(2) — GST not yet notified on these products |

## Decision Flowchart: Is RCM Applicable?

The following step-by-step decision tree enables practitioners to determine RCM applicability systematically for any transaction:

**Step 1 — Is the Transaction a "Supply" Under GST?**
Determine whether the transaction constitutes a supply of goods, services, or both within the meaning of Section 7 of the CGST Act. If the transaction is not a supply (e.g., gift without consideration not in the course of business, actionable claim other than specified, transfer within same GSTIN), no GST (including RCM) applies. Proceed to Step 2 only if a "supply" exists.

**Step 2 — Is the Supply Taxable (Not Exempt/Non-GST)?**
Check whether the supply is covered by an exemption notification under Section 11, or whether the goods/services are outside the GST levy (e.g., petroleum products under Section 9(2), alcohol for human consumption, etc.). If the supply is exempt or outside GST: **RCM does not apply. Stop.**

**Step 3 — Is the Supply Covered by a Section 9(3)/5(3) Notification?**
Check the current Section 9(3) notification (Notification No. 13/2017-CT(Rate) as amended) and the goods notification (04/2017-CT(Rate) as amended). If the supply falls within a notified category: **RCM under Section 9(3) applies, regardless of whether the supplier is registered or unregistered.** The recipient is liable. Proceed to documentation compliance. Note: for inter-state supplies, Section 5(3) IGST applies instead.

**Step 4 — If Not a Section 9(3) Category, Is the Supplier Unregistered?**
If the supply is NOT in a Section 9(3) notification, determine whether the supplier is a registered person under GST. If the supplier is registered: forward charge applies. **RCM does not apply.** If the supplier is unregistered: proceed to Step 5.

**Step 5 — Is the Supply in a Section 9(4) Notified Category?**
Check the current Section 9(4) notification (Notification No. 07/2019-CT(Rate) as amended). If the unregistered supplier's supply falls within a notified category: **RCM under Section 9(4) applies.** If the unregistered supplier's supply is not in any Section 9(4) notification: **RCM does not apply** under the post-2019 amended law. (Note: if the period of assessment is before 01.02.2019 and Section 9(4) was operative, all purchases from unregistered persons attracted RCM — check the dates carefully.)

**Step 6 — Is the Recipient a Registered Person?**
For Section 9(4) RCM: the recipient must be a registered person. If the recipient is unregistered: Section 9(4) does not apply. For Section 9(3) RCM: the notification typically specifies the class of recipient (e.g., "any business entity located in the taxable territory") — verify the notification wording. For import of services under Section 5(3): even an unregistered Indian recipient may be liable for IGST if the import is for business purposes.

**Step 7 — Is There Any Conditional Exemption in the Notification?**
Some entries in Section 9(3) notifications carry conditions or value thresholds (e.g., GTA freight thresholds). Verify that the transaction satisfies all conditions of the notification entry. If a condition is not met: the exemption applies and **RCM does not arise.** If all conditions are met: **RCM applies fully.**

**Step 8 — Determine: IGST or CGST+SGST?**
Based on the place of supply rules under the IGST Act: if intra-state supply, CGST+SGST at applicable rates; if inter-state, IGST at full rate. For import of services, always IGST.

**Step 9 — Proceed to Compliance:** Issue self-invoice (if unregistered supplier), issue payment voucher at time of payment, report in GSTR-3B Table 3.1(d), pay from cash ledger, and thereafter claim ITC in Table 4A(3).

## **[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]** Maharashtra SGST RCM Nuances
{law_reference: "MGST Act, 2017 (Maharashtra Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017); MGST Rules, 2017; Maharashtra GST Department Circular; Maharashtra AAR Orders"}

**Maharashtra SGST Act and Mirror Notifications:** The Maharashtra GST Act, 2017 (MGST Act) is structured identically to the CGST Act. Section 9(3) and 9(4) of the MGST Act mirror the CGST provisions precisely, and the Maharashtra government issues corresponding state notifications that adopt the same categories as the Central Government notifications. In practice, every CGST Section 9(3) notification is implemented simultaneously under the MGST Act so that CGST and SGST components of RCM are both captured. Maharashtra practitioners need not track separate state notifications for the categories list; the central and state notifications are coterminous.

**Maharashtra AAR — Key Positions on RCM:**

- **GTA Services — Maharashtra AAR in M/s. Maharashtra Airport Development Company (2019):** The Maharashtra Authority for Advance Ruling (MAAR) held that GTA services received from a registered GTA (who had not paid GST under forward charge) attract RCM under Section 9(3) on the recipient. The ruling clarified that even when a GTA opts to pay GST under forward charge (at 12%), the transaction must be confirmed in writing — in the absence of such confirmation, the recipient is safest to treat the transaction as RCM at 5%.

- **Security Services — Maharashtra Manufacturing Units:** MAAR rulings have consistently held that security services provided by any person other than a body corporate to a registered manufacturing unit attract RCM under Notification 29/2018-CT(Rate). Several large Maharashtra manufacturers initially had disputes with proprietary or partnership security agencies where the supplier's legal form (whether body corporate) was unclear. MAAR clarified that limited liability partnerships (LLPs) are not "body corporates" for this purpose, and hence security services by LLPs attract RCM. This is a Maharashtra-prevalent issue given the large number of LLP-form security agencies operating in the Pune-Mumbai industrial corridor.

- **Legal Services by Maharashtra Advocates:** Advocates registered with the Bar Council of Maharashtra and Goa who provide services to business entities attract RCM under Section 9(3). MAAR rulings have clarified that "firm of advocates" includes partnerships registered under the Indian Partnership Act. Senior counsel (designated Senior Advocates) providing advisory services through a law firm arrangement also fall within the notification category.

**Maharashtra SGST Officers — Assessment Approach:** Maharashtra SGST officers during scrutiny assessments have, in several cases examined post-2022, focused on:
1. **Completeness of self-invoice register:** SGST audit teams verify that self-invoices are serially numbered, complete, and match GSTR-3B Table 3.1(d) disclosures. Gaps or missing serial numbers trigger demand notices.
2. **Cash ledger payment verification:** Officers cross-verify that RCM payments are reflected in the Electronic Cash Ledger and that ITC claims in Table 4A(3) correspond to actual payments — not to notional or accrual-basis credits.
3. **Unregistered supplier identification:** Maharashtra SGST officers frequently issue notices asking registered businesses to disclose all purchases from unregistered suppliers, even for periods after the Section 9(4) suspension, as a general audit query. Practitioners must respond precisely, distinguishing the operative law for each assessment year.

**Dual Assessment Risk in Maharashtra:** Due to Maharashtra's large and active SGST administration (largest state GST collection in India), registered businesses face a higher probability of dual scrutiny — both CGST (by Central Tax officers) and SGST (by Maharashtra SGST officers) in the same year, under the "cross-empowerment" framework. While in theory only one authority audits a taxpayer in a financial year (subject to the cross-empowerment assignment), RCM-specific notices can be issued independently. Maharashtra practitioners should ensure that RCM compliance records are audit-ready at all times, not merely at annual return filing time.

**Maharashtra-Specific Exemptions:** As of May 2026, the Maharashtra government has not issued any RCM-specific exemptions that differ from the Central Government's exemption notifications. Maharashtra follows the uniform GST Council position on exempt categories. Any state-specific exemption (if and when issued) would be valid only for the SGST component (9% of the total rate), not for the CGST component, which remains governed by Central notifications. Maharashtra practitioners should monitor the Maharashtra SGST Department's website and official circulars for any state-level clarifications that modify the application of central RCM provisions within the state.

**Maharashtra AAR Binding Precedent:** Advance Rulings by the Maharashtra AAR (MAAR) are binding only on the applicant and the jurisdictional officers for that applicant. They are not binding on other taxpayers. However, MAAR rulings are persuasive and frequently adopted by Maharashtra SGST officers in similar fact situations during assessments of other taxpayers. CA/CS/CMA professionals advising Maharashtra clients should track MAAR RCM rulings as part of their practice intelligence, using them either as supporting authority (when the ruling favours the client) or as advance warning of assessment risk (when the ruling goes against the taxpayer's position).
