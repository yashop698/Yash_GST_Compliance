## Section 9(3) CGST Act — RCM on Notified Supplies
{law_reference: "Section 9(3) CGST Act, 2017; Notification No. 13/2017-Central Tax (Rate) dated 28.06.2017 and subsequent amendments"}

**Statutory Text of Section 9(3):**
> "The Government may, on the recommendations of the Council, by notification, specify categories of supply of goods or services or both, the tax on which shall be paid on reverse charge basis by the recipient of such goods or services or both and all the provisions of this Act shall apply to such recipient as if he is the person liable for paying the tax in relation to the supply of such goods or services or both."

**Comprehensive Annotation:**

*Who is the "Government" that notifies?* Under Section 9(3), "Government" means the Central Government acting on the recommendations of the GST Council. Notifications are issued under the authority of the Central Government and are applicable across all states and union territories for CGST purposes. Each state government issues a parallel notification under its SGST Act adopting an identical list, ensuring uniform application. The GST Council, a constitutional body under Article 279A, makes recommendations; the government is not bound to follow them but in practice does so universally. A notification under Section 9(3) has the force of law and, once issued, automatically makes RCM applicable from the effective date of the notification without requiring any further statutory amendment.

*What are "categories of supply"?* The term "categories" is deliberately broad, allowing the government to define RCM applicability by reference to: (a) the nature of goods or services (e.g., "legal services by an advocate"), (b) the class of supplier (e.g., "Goods Transport Agency"), (c) the nature of the transaction (e.g., "import of services"), or (d) combinations thereof. The categorisation need not mirror the HSN/SAC classification, though in practice most notifications are aligned to specific SAC headings to reduce interpretational ambiguity. Where the notification defines the category imprecisely, taxpayers must seek clarification through advance rulings or board circulars.

*How notifications interact with the main section:* Section 9(3) is the enabling provision — it is a "parent" clause. Without a supporting notification, Section 9(3) has no operational effect. The notification is therefore a subordinate legislation that operationalises the parent provision. The key notifications are: Notification No. 13/2017-Central Tax (Rate) dated 28.06.2017 (for services, as amended by subsequent notifications including Notifications 33/2017, 3/2018, 15/2018, 22/2019, and various others through 2024-2026) and Notification No. 04/2017-Central Tax (Rate) dated 28.06.2017 (for goods).

*Legislative history and evolution:* At the commencement of GST on 01.07.2017, the Section 9(3) notification for services covered eleven categories including GTA services, legal services by advocates, services by government/local authority (reverse charge on business recipients), import of services, security services (added later), and others. Services by a director to a company were added by Notification No. 13/2017-CT(Rate) itself. Security services (by any person other than a body corporate) were added by Notification No. 29/2018-CT(Rate) w.e.f. 01.01.2019, reflecting a phased expansion of the Section 9(3) list as the government gained experience with the GST regime.

## Section 9(4) CGST Act — RCM on Unregistered Supplier Supplies
{law_reference: "Section 9(4) CGST Act, 2017 as substituted by CGST (Amendment) Act, 2018; Notification No. 08/2017-CT(Rate); Notification No. 38/2017-CT(Rate); Notification No. 07/2019-CT(Rate)"}

**Statutory Text of Section 9(4) as currently in force (post-2018 amendment):**
> "The Government may, on the recommendations of the Council, by notification, specify a class of registered persons who shall, in respect of supply of specified categories of goods or services or both received from an unregistered supplier, pay the tax on reverse charge basis as the recipient of such supply of goods or services or both, and all the provisions of this Act shall apply to such recipient as if he is the person liable for paying the tax in relation to such supply of goods or services or both."

**Annotation — Original Broad Scope (01.07.2017 to 12.10.2017 and 01.04.2018 to 31.01.2019):**
The original Section 9(4), as enacted in 2017, required every registered person to pay GST under reverse charge on ALL purchases from unregistered suppliers, without any category restriction. This created a massive compliance burden: any small registered business making even trivial purchases (stationery, petty repairs, office supplies) from local unregistered vendors had to calculate and pay RCM on each such transaction, maintain self-invoices, issue payment vouchers, and file disclosures. For businesses with hundreds of petty cash transactions, this was operationally unworkable.

**The 2017 Suspension (13.10.2017 to 31.03.2018):** Recognising the compliance overload, the government suspended Section 9(4) entirely via Notification No. 38/2017-CT(Rate) dated 13.10.2017, providing immediate relief. The suspension was extended multiple times before the provision was fundamentally amended.

**The 2018 Legislative Substitution:** The CGST (Amendment) Act, 2018 (effective 01.02.2019) substituted the original Section 9(4) with the current narrower version — quoted above — which empowers the government to notify only specific classes of recipients and specific categories of goods/services for Section 9(4) RCM. This was a structural reform, not merely a deferral.

**Current Position as of May 2026:** Under Section 9(4) as amended, only specific categories notified by the government attract RCM from unregistered suppliers. The principal notification operative is Notification No. 07/2019-CT(Rate) dated 29.03.2019, which lists specific services (principally services by an author/book publisher to publishing companies and services supplied by a player, which may be amended and expanded from time to time) as attracting Section 9(4) RCM. Practitioners must verify the current notification in force as of the transaction date, as the Section 9(4) list has been amended by multiple subsequent notifications. The broad "catch-all" application to all unregistered supplier purchases no longer exists.

**The "registered person" requirement:** Both the old and new Section 9(4) apply only when the recipient is a registered person. An unregistered individual purchasing from an unregistered supplier does not trigger Section 9(4); the transaction simply falls outside the GST net at that level. The moment the recipient crosses the registration threshold, they become liable for Section 9(4) RCM on their notified category purchases from unregistered sources.

## IGST Act Equivalents
{law_reference: "Section 5(3), 5(4) IGST Act, 2017; Section 7(4) IGST Act, 2017"}

**Section 5(3) IGST — Inter-State RCM on Notified Supplies**
Section 5(3) of the IGST Act is the precise equivalent of Section 9(3) CGST for inter-state supplies. Where the supply of notified goods or services crosses state boundaries (or where the place of supply is in a different state from the location of the supplier), IGST applies rather than CGST+SGST, and the RCM liability falls on the recipient under Section 5(3). The same notifications issued under CGST Section 9(3) are mirrored for IGST purposes through coordinated notifications under the IGST Act. In practice, a single notification event (e.g., adding a new service category to RCM) is implemented by parallel notifications under both Acts simultaneously.

**Section 5(4) IGST — Inter-State RCM from Unregistered Suppliers**
Section 5(4) mirrors Section 9(4) for inter-state supplies from unregistered persons. The same legislative history (suspension, amendment, current narrow scope) applies.

**Import of Services — The Critical IGST Application:**
Under Section 7(4) read with Section 2(6) of the IGST Act, import of services is deemed to be an inter-state supply. Consequently, when a person in India (registered or, critically, even unregistered persons) receives services from a foreign supplier for use in the course of business, IGST applies under Section 5(3) on reverse charge basis. Importantly, even an unregistered person in India who imports services for business purposes is required to pay IGST under RCM — this is one of the limited cases where RCM applies to an unregistered recipient. Such persons must take a limited registration for paying IGST on imported services.

**Key Practical Differences between CGST RCM and IGST RCM:**
- For intra-state supplies: CGST (at half rate) + SGST (at half rate) are paid; both go into respective cash ledgers
- For inter-state supplies: IGST (at full rate) is paid; goes into the IGST cash ledger
- For imports of services: IGST only, at full rate; self-invoice and payment voucher in INR at the applicable exchange rate on the date of supply
- ITC of IGST paid on imports is fully available for cross-utilisation against IGST, CGST, and SGST output liabilities in that sequence per Section 49 CGST Act

## Rules 3 to 5 of CGST Rules, 2017
{law_reference: "Rules 3, 4, 5 CGST Rules, 2017"}

### Rule 3 — Supplies Subject to RCM

**Text of Rule 3:** Rule 3 provides that the categories of supply of goods or services or both as specified in the Schedule to these rules or as may be notified by the Government under Section 9(3) shall be subject to payment of tax on reverse charge basis.

**Annotation:** Rule 3 is the rule-level operationalisation of Section 9(3). It incorporates by reference the notifications issued under Section 9(3), creating a seamless statutory chain: Act → Notification → Rule. Rule 3 does not independently list categories; it draws from the notification framework. Practitioners must read Rule 3 in conjunction with the current Section 9(3) notification (as amended up to the relevant date) to determine the operative list. The Rule also provides that where a notification is issued under Section 9(3) after the commencement of the Rules, such notification-specified categories are automatically covered by Rule 3 without requiring an amendment to the Rule itself.

### Rule 4 — Exemptions and Exclusions

**Text of Rule 4:** Rule 4 provides that the provisions of Rule 3 shall not apply to any supply of goods or services or both which is exempt from tax under Section 11 of the CGST Act. Further, supplies made by composition taxpayers (who opt for the composition scheme under Section 10) are covered under the composition scheme's own rules and are not subject to the normal RCM under Section 9(3) in their capacity as suppliers.

**Annotation and Key Exclusions:**

- **Exempt Supplies:** If the supply itself is exempt from GST by virtue of a notification under Section 11, no RCM can arise because there is no taxable supply. Example: raw silk is exempt; a manufacturer purchasing raw silk from an unregistered villager does not trigger RCM on that supply.

- **Composition Dealer as Supplier:** When a registered composition dealer supplies goods or services, the recipient does not pay RCM on such supplies. The composition dealer pays their own composition levy. However, if the composition dealer is the recipient (not the supplier) of an RCM supply, they must pay the RCM but cannot claim ITC (see Section 10(4)).

- **Threshold-Based Relief:** Under the amended Section 9(4) framework, the government may specify threshold values below which Section 9(4) RCM does not apply. Practitioners must verify the current threshold (if any) for the specific category from the operative notification.

### Rule 5 — Payment Timing and Recovery

**Text of Rule 5:** Rule 5 provides for the time of supply for reverse charge supplies and the mechanism for recovery from the supplier in case the recipient fails to pay.

**Time of Supply for RCM Under Section 12(3) (Goods) and Section 13(3) (Services):**

For goods under RCM, time of supply is the earliest of:
- Date of receipt of goods
- Date of payment (as entered in the books of account of the recipient or the date of debit in the bank account, whichever is earlier)
- Date immediately following thirty days from the date of invoice issued by the supplier

For services under RCM, time of supply is the earliest of:
- Date of payment as entered in the books of account of the recipient or the date of debit in the bank account, whichever is earlier
- Date immediately following sixty days from the date of invoice issued by the supplier

**Import of Services — Additional Triggers:** For import of services, if neither of the above dates can be determined, the time of supply is the date of entry in the books of account of the recipient. This provision addresses the practical reality that foreign invoices may be delayed, lost, or issued on dates that do not align with Indian accounting periods.

**Recovery Mechanism:** Where a recipient fails to pay the RCM, the tax officer may initiate recovery proceedings against the recipient (not the supplier) under Sections 73/74/79 of the CGST Act. The supplier in an RCM transaction has no liability to pay the tax and cannot be compelled to do so by the officer — the liability is exclusively on the recipient. Interest under Section 50 accrues from the due date of payment (i.e., from the tax period in which the time of supply falls) at 18% per annum.

## Documentation Rules Applicable to RCM
{law_reference: "Section 31(3)(f) CGST Act, 2017; Rule 46 CGST Rules, 2017; Rule 52 CGST Rules, 2017; Rule 50 CGST Rules, 2017; Section 36 CGST Act, 2017"}

**Self-Invoice — Rule 46 Read with Section 31(3)(f):**
When a registered person makes a purchase from an unregistered supplier (Section 9(4) scenarios), they are required to issue a **self-invoice** in respect of that inward supply. The self-invoice serves as the tax invoice for the purpose of the inward supply. Mandatory fields on the self-invoice include:
- Serial number unique for the financial year (in series maintained separately for self-invoices)
- Date of issue
- Name, address, and GSTIN of the recipient (i.e., the issuer of the self-invoice)
- Name and address of the unregistered supplier (GSTIN not applicable)
- HSN code (for goods) or SAC (for services)
- Description and quantity of goods or services
- Taxable value
- Applicable tax rates and amounts (CGST, SGST, or IGST as applicable)
- Place of supply
- Indication that the invoice is issued on account of reverse charge under Section 9(4)

The self-invoice must be issued on or before the date of receipt of goods or within 30 days of the date of supply of services. The time of supply rules under Section 12(3)/13(3) may be affected if the self-invoice date falls before the invoice from the supplier (if any). Self-invoices are not required for Section 9(3) RCM where the supplier issues their own invoice (the forward invoice from, say, a GTA or an advocate does not need to be replaced by a self-invoice — the recipient simply converts that invoice's liability into RCM in their books).

**Payment Voucher — Rule 52:**
A **payment voucher** must be issued by the recipient at the time of making payment to the supplier in respect of any supply covered by RCM (whether under Section 9(3) or 9(4)). The payment voucher contains:
- Serial number unique to the financial year
- Date
- Name and address of the recipient (GSTIN of the registered recipient)
- Name and address of the supplier
- Nature and description of the payment
- Amount paid and tax amount thereon

**Receipt Voucher — Rule 50:** Where an advance is paid to an unregistered supplier (Section 9(4) scenario), a receipt voucher is required to be issued by the recipient at the time of advance payment. The receipt voucher triggers the time of supply at the date of payment.

**Document Retention:** All RCM-related documents (self-invoices, payment vouchers, receipt vouchers, supplier invoices) must be retained for 72 months (six years) from the due date of filing of the annual return for the year to which they relate, under Section 36 of the CGST Act. The six-year period applies uniformly; for pending audit, appeal, or litigation, retention must continue until the matter is finally concluded.

## Comparison: Forward Charge vs Reverse Charge

| Parameter | Forward Charge | Reverse Charge |
|-----------|----------------|----------------|
| Who collects GST from the customer | Supplier | Not applicable (recipient pays directly) |
| Who deposits tax with the government | Supplier | Recipient |
| Invoice issuer | Supplier issues Tax Invoice | Supplier issues own invoice; Recipient additionally issues Self-Invoice (for unregistered supplier cases) |
| ITC availability timing | On receipt of goods/services AND receipt of invoice AND tax paid by supplier | Only after actual payment of RCM tax to the government (Section 16(2)) |
| Medium of payment of tax to government | Can use ITC balance or cash ledger | Exclusively cash ledger; ITC balance cannot be used for RCM tax payment (second proviso to Section 49(4)) |
| GSTR-3B reporting of tax liability | Table 3.1(a) for outward taxable supplies; 3.1(b) for zero-rated; 3.1(c) for nil-rated | Table 3.1(d): "Inward supplies liable to reverse charge" |
| ITC reporting in GSTR-3B | Table 4A(1) or 4A(5) depending on source | Table 4A(3): "Inward supplies liable to reverse charge" |
| GST registration impact | Supplier must be registered if turnover exceeds threshold | Recipient must be registered; RCM registration may be required for imports even if turnover below threshold |
| Interest on delayed tax payment | Section 50; from due date of return | Section 50; from due date of the tax period in which time of supply falls |
| Audit trail document | Supplier's tax invoice | Self-invoice (Rule 46) + Payment Voucher (Rule 52) |
| Place of supply determination | Normal rules under IGST Act | Normal rules apply; for import of services, place of supply is location of recipient (Section 13 IGST Act) |
| Liability to comply with return | Supplier liable for GSTR-1 reporting | Recipient reports in GSTR-3B; no GSTR-1 entry for RCM (recipient's GSTR-1 is not affected) |

## Section 31(3)(f) — Self-Invoice Obligation
{law_reference: "Section 31(3)(f) CGST Act, 2017; Rule 46 CGST Rules, 2017; CBIC Circular No. 26/26/2017-GST dated 29.12.2017"}

Section 31(3)(f) of the CGST Act provides:
> "A registered person shall, in the case of supplies in respect of which tax is paid or is liable to be paid on reverse charge basis under sub-section (3) or sub-section (4) of section 9, issue an invoice in respect of goods or services or both received by him from the supplier who is not registered."

**Scope:** The obligation under Section 31(3)(f) applies specifically when the supplier is unregistered (i.e., Section 9(4) scenarios). For Section 9(3) RCM supplies where the supplier is a registered entity (e.g., a GTA company, a law firm), the supplier issues their own invoice and Section 31(3)(f) does not require an additional self-invoice from the recipient. However, where a GTA or other Section 9(3) supplier is unregistered (which can happen if their turnover is below the threshold and they have not voluntarily registered), both Section 9(3) and Section 31(3)(f) apply simultaneously.

**Serial Numbering:** CBIC Circular No. 26/26/2017-GST clarified that self-invoices may be serially numbered separately from outward supply invoices. A separate series for self-invoices (e.g., "SI/2025-26/001" etc.) is recommended to avoid confusion. The series must be unique within a financial year and within a GSTIN.

**Time Limit for Issuance:** The self-invoice must be issued within 30 days of the date of supply (for services) or on or before receipt (for goods). Delayed issuance does not eliminate the liability but can affect the determination of time of supply and may attract penalties under Section 125 (general penalty, up to ₹25,000) for document non-compliance.

**Digital Signature:** Self-invoices do not require a digital signature unless the issuer is required to maintain electronic records under the Information Technology Act. For most businesses, a signed/stamped physical self-invoice or an electronically generated self-invoice from accounting software is sufficient.

**Where Supplier Issues Invoice:** Where a registered supplier under Section 9(3) (e.g., an advocate) issues their own invoice to the recipient, the recipient uses that invoice as the basis for RCM compliance. The self-invoice obligation under Rule 46 is superimposed only when the supplier has not issued any invoice (typically the unregistered supplier case). In practice, some businesses issue self-invoices even for Section 9(3) registered suppliers to maintain a uniform document series — this is not prohibited but is not legally required.
