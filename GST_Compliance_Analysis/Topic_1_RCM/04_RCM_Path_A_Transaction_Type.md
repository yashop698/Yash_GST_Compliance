## How to Use Path A

Path A organises the entire Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) universe by the **nature of the supply or transaction** encountered in practice. When a practitioner receives a bill from an overseas software vendor, hires a goods transport agency, pays a retainer to a law firm, or settles a security agency invoice, the immediate question is: *does RCM apply to this transaction?* Path A answers that question first, then points to the relevant section, notification, and compliance obligation.

Path A is therefore the **practitioner's entry point** — structured around the real-world encounter with a supply, rather than the structure of the statute. Academic readers and litigators who wish to work from first principles of the statute — tracing which Rule, which Section, and which Notification governs a transaction — should use **Path B** (File 05), which is the companion to this document.

**How to navigate Path A:**

1. Identify the nature of the supply (goods/services/both) and whether the supplier is located inside or outside India.
2. Identify whether the supplier is registered under GST.
3. Identify the category of the supply (GTA, legal, director services, import, etc.).
4. Locate the matching category in this document (Categories 1 through 5).
5. Within that category, read the applicability conditions, then check the Compliance Requirements sub-section.
6. Cross-reference the Comprehensive Matrix at the end of this file to confirm the applicable notification and rate.

**Governing legal framework at a glance:**

| Source of RCM | Governing Provision | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Specified services (domestic) | Section 9(3) CGST Act | Notification 13/2017-CT(Rate) and amendments |
| Supplies from unregistered suppliers | Section 9(4) CGST Act | Notification 7/2019-CT(Rate) and amendments |
| Import of services | Section 5(3) IGST Act | Read with Section 7(1)(b) CGST Act |
| Import of goods | Section 5(1) IGST Act | Read with Customs Act, 1962 |
| Maharashtra intra-state RCM | Section 9(3)/(4) Maharashtra SGST Act, 2017 | Mirrors CGST notifications **[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]** |

## Category 1: Supplies from Unregistered Suppliers
{law_reference: "Section 9(4) CGST Act, 2017; Notification No. 7/2019-CT(Rate) dated 29-Mar-2019; Notification No. 4/2019-IGST(Rate); Maharashtra Notification No. 7/2019-State Tax (Rate) [MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]"}

### Scope and Applicability

**Section 9(4) of the CGST Act, 2017** provides that the Central Government may, on the recommendation of the Council, notify certain categories of supplies of goods or services or both received from an **unregistered supplier** by a specified class of registered persons, who shall pay the tax on a reverse charge basis. The SGST law of each state contains a mirror provision (Section 9(4) Maharashtra SGST Act, 2017 **[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]**).

**Historical background — critical context:**

The original Section 9(4) as enacted in 2017 was a blanket provision: *every* registered person who procured *any* supply from *any* unregistered supplier was liable to pay GST on RCM for that purchase. This was extremely onerous in practice — a manufacturer buying stationery worth ₹500 from a local unregistered shop was technically liable to pay GST on RCM. The provision was suspended repeatedly by notifications granting blanket exemptions (Notification No. 8/2017-CT(Rate) and subsequent extensions) until it was fundamentally amended by the **Finance Act (No. 2), 2018**, effective 1 February 2019.

**Current position as of May 2026:**

From 1 February 2019 onwards, Section 9(4) no longer applies to all purchases from unregistered suppliers. The Central Government now issues **category-specific notifications** identifying particular categories of supplies (by goods/services classification) and particular classes of registered recipients for whom RCM under Section 9(4) is triggered. Only those notified combinations attract RCM; all other purchases from unregistered suppliers do not attract GST on RCM (though the input tax credit chain is broken, which is a business cost).

**Notification No. 7/2019-CT(Rate) dated 29 March 2019** is the primary notification operationalising Section 9(4) in the post-amendment regime. It has been amended by multiple subsequent notifications. The equivalent IGST notification is Notification No. 4/2019-IGST(Rate). **[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]** Maharashtra has issued a mirror state-level notification.

**Practical significance:** In the post-2019 regime, many small purchases from unregistered vendors do not attract RCM. However, for the specific categories notified (detailed below), the obligation is strict and non-compliance attracts interest at 18% per annum under Section 50 CGST Act plus penalties under Section 74.

### Who is "Unregistered"

A supplier is **unregistered** for the purposes of Section 9(4) if they:

1. **Do not hold a valid GSTIN** — either because they have not applied, their registration is cancelled, their registration is suspended, or they were never required to register.

2. **Fall below the aggregate turnover threshold:** Under Section 22 of the CGST Act, a supplier is required to obtain GST registration only when their aggregate turnover in a financial year exceeds:
   - **₹40 lakh** (for suppliers of goods) — as enhanced from ₹20 lakh by Notification No. 10/2019-CT dated 7 March 2019 for most states.
   - **₹20 lakh** (for suppliers of services, and for goods suppliers in states that did not opt for the enhanced threshold).
   - **₹10 lakh** (for suppliers in special category states under Article 279A of the Constitution: Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland, Tripura). Note that Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, Meghalaya, and Assam now have ₹20 lakh threshold following various council decisions.

   **Maharashtra** follows the general ₹40 lakh threshold for goods and ₹20 lakh for services. **[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]**

3. **Voluntarily registered suppliers are NOT unregistered:** A supplier who has obtained voluntary registration under Section 25(3) of the CGST Act (even if their turnover is below the threshold) is a registered person. Purchases from them do not attract Section 9(4) RCM.

4. **Composition dealers are registered persons:** A supplier registered under the Composition Scheme (Section 10) is a registered person and cannot collect GST from their buyers. However, they are registered; therefore Section 9(4) does not apply to the buyer. The buyer simply loses the ITC chain since the composition dealer collects no tax. This is NOT an RCM situation.

5. **Exempt suppliers vs unregistered suppliers:** A supplier making only exempt supplies may not be required to register (Section 23(1)(a)). If they are not registered, they are an unregistered supplier for this purpose.

**Key test:** At the time of the supply, does the supplier hold a valid, non-suspended, non-cancelled GSTIN? If yes → registered. If no → unregistered; check whether the specific supply falls in the notified categories under Section 9(4).

### What Supplies are Currently Covered

As of May 2026, the following categories of supplies from unregistered suppliers attract RCM under Section 9(4) read with Notification No. 7/2019-CT(Rate) and its amendments. Note that this is distinct from Section 9(3) — Section 9(3) applies regardless of registration status of the supplier; Section 9(4) specifically applies when the supplier is *unregistered*:

**Category A — Services specified in Notification 7/2019-CT(Rate):**

1. **Services by way of renting of any motor vehicle designed to carry passengers** where the cost of fuel is included in the consideration charged, provided by an unregistered person to a corporate body — at the applicable rate on RCM basis.

2. **Services of an author, music composer, photographer, poet, or other artist by way of transfer or permitting the use or enjoyment of a copyright** (covered under Section 13(1)(a) of the Copyright Act, 1957) relating to original literary, dramatic, musical or artistic works — where the author/artist is unregistered.

3. **Lending of securities** under SEBI's Securities Lending Scheme — covered in specific circumstances.

**Important note on Section 9(4) vs 9(3) overlap:** Several categories that might appear to be Section 9(4) scenarios are actually covered by Section 9(3) notifications regardless of registration status. For example, GTA services are notified under Section 9(3) — the GTA liability applies whether the GTA is registered or not (so the consignor/consignee pays RCM regardless).

**Category B — Goods specified separately:**

The GST Council and government have been cautious about specifying goods categories under the post-2019 Section 9(4) framework. As of May 2026, the primary emphasis of Section 9(4) notifications remains on the service categories above.

**What is NOT covered (and was covered historically under the blanket 2017 provision):**
- Stationery from local unregistered shops — not covered (no RCM).
- Cleaning supplies from unregistered vendors — not covered.
- Office maintenance by small local contractors — not covered unless they render a notified service.
- Food/catering from unregistered caterers below threshold — not covered.

**Critical point for practice:** Even when Section 9(4) does not mandate RCM, the practical cost is that the registered buyer bears the tax burden embedded in the price without being able to claim ITC. This cost should be factored into vendor evaluation — a registered vendor charging a higher price may be more economical when the ITC benefit is accounted for.

### Compliance Requirements

When Section 9(4) RCM is triggered, the **registered recipient** (the buyer) must:

**Step 1 — Self-Invoice:**
Issue a **self-invoice** under Section 31(3)(f) of the CGST Act read with Rule 46 of the CGST Rules. The self-invoice must be issued on or before the date of payment or date of accounting in the books, whichever is earlier. The self-invoice must contain all mandatory fields (detailed in Path B, Rule 46 section).

**Step 2 — Payment Voucher:**
Issue a **payment voucher** under Rule 52 of the CGST Rules when making the payment to the unregistered supplier. This documents the outward payment.

**Step 3 — Tax Payment:**
Pay the RCM GST through the **Electronic Cash Ledger only.** This is a critical and frequently misunderstood requirement — unlike regular GST outward tax liability (which can be set off against ITC in the Electronic Credit Ledger), RCM liability **must be paid in cash**. This is because the ITC credit generated from the RCM payment will flow into the Electronic Credit Ledger only *after* the cash payment is made; you cannot simultaneously use credit that is yet to be generated.

**Time of supply for payment:** The time of supply for RCM under Section 9(4) is the date of payment to the supplier or 60 days from the date of invoice/bill/challan of the supplier, whichever is earlier. Where the supplier does not issue any document, the time of supply is the date of payment or 60 days from the date of supply.

**Step 4 — GSTR-3B Reporting:**
In **GSTR-3B (Table 3.1(d)):** Report the aggregate value of inward supplies on which RCM is payable. The tax amount is reported here and paid in cash.

In **GSTR-3B (Table 4):** The ITC generated from the RCM payment is availed in the *same tax period or a later period* — it **cannot** be claimed in the period before the cash payment is made.

In **GSTR-2B:** The self-invoice raised by the taxpayer appears in their own GSTR-2B as auto-populated credit (from their GSTR-1 self-supply entries).

**Step 5 — Annual Return:**
In GSTR-9, the RCM supplies from unregistered persons must be disclosed in **Table 4G** (inward supplies on which tax is paid on RCM basis, from unregistered persons).

**[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC] Maharashtra-specific reporting:**
Maharashtra SGST follows the same GSTR-3B and GSTR-9 structure but the state tax component of RCM (SGST) must be reported and paid separately to the Maharashtra GST administration. When filing returns on the GST portal, the CGST and SGST components are segregated automatically. Maharashtra's GST department may issue audit show-cause notices specifically examining Section 9(4) compliance, particularly in sectors like construction, hospitality, and real estate where unregistered vendors are prevalent.

### Examples

**Example 1 — Cleaning services from unregistered individual:**

> **Facts:** ABC Pvt. Ltd., a registered company in Pune (Maharashtra), hires Ramesh (an individual, annual income ₹3 lakh, unregistered) for office cleaning at ₹8,000 per month.
>
> **RCM applicability check:** Cleaning services by an unregistered individual. As of May 2026, cleaning/housekeeping services are not specifically notified under Notification 7/2019-CT(Rate) as a Section 9(4) category. Therefore, **RCM does NOT apply.**
>
> **Practical consequence:** ABC Pvt. Ltd. pays ₹8,000 to Ramesh. No GST is collected; no RCM is payable. The ₹8,000 is a cost without any ITC. If ABC had hired a registered cleaning agency charging ₹9,440 (₹8,000 + 18% GST = ₹1,440), the net cost after ITC would be ₹8,000. The unregistered option saves ₹0 in this case — pricing is identical in net terms if full ITC is available.

**Example 2 — Repair work by local unregistered contractor:**

> **Facts:** Maharashtra Textiles Ltd. (registered, Mumbai) hires a local mason/contractor, Suresh, (unregistered, turnover ₹15 lakh p.a.) to carry out repairs to the factory building at ₹1,50,000.
>
> **RCM applicability check:** Works contract services — the supply is a composite supply of labour and materials for repair/maintenance. Works contract services to a registered person by an unregistered contractor: Is this notified under Section 9(4)? Post the 2019 amendment, works contract for repair/maintenance by unregistered contractors is generally **not specifically notified** for RCM under Section 9(4). Therefore, **RCM does not apply** under Section 9(4).
>
> **Practical consequence:** However, if the works contract relates to *construction of immovable property intended for own use* (capital goods / building), ITC is blocked under Section 17(5)(c)/(d) even if RCM were applicable. The contractor is unregistered so no GST is charged in the invoice; no RCM; and the cost is a pure expense.
>
> **[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]** Maharashtra GST auditors scrutinise construction-related payments to unregistered contractors closely. If a batch of payments to multiple small unregistered contractors cumulatively suggests avoidance of registration, the GST authorities may issue notices under Section 74 alleging the contractors ought to have been registered and should have charged GST, leading to demand on the contractor (not RCM on the recipient in this scenario).

**Example 3 — Printing services from unregistered vendor:**

> **Facts:** Navi Mumbai-based Pharma Ltd. (registered) procures printed packaging material from a small local printing press (unregistered, annual turnover ₹12 lakh) at ₹25,000 per month.
>
> **RCM applicability check:** Supply of printed packaging material — this is a supply of goods (printed matter) by an unregistered supplier. Goods-based Section 9(4) categories are narrow under the post-2019 regime. Printing services involving job-work (where the customer supplies the material and the printer only provides labour/skill) might be classified as services. However, if the printer supplies the finished printed goods (supplying both material and printing), it is a composite supply or goods supply. As of May 2026, this specific category is **not notified** for RCM under Section 9(4).
>
> **Outcome:** No RCM. The pharma company bears the cost without ITC. The pragmatic advice is to either require the vendor to register voluntarily, or switch to a registered vendor.

## Category 2: Import of Services
{law_reference: "Section 7(1)(b) CGST Act, 2017; Section 2(11) IGST Act, 2017; Section 13 IGST Act, 2017; Section 5(3) IGST Act, 2017; Rule 3 CGST Rules, 2017"}

### Deemed Supply: Import of Services

**Section 7(1)(b) of the CGST Act, 2017** extends the definition of "supply" to include the **import of services for a consideration** even if it is made in the course or furtherance of *business*. This provision, combined with **Section 7(1)(a)**, means that a recipient in India who receives services from a supplier located outside India is considered to be making/receiving a taxable supply — even though the supplier is outside the Indian GST jurisdiction.

The underlying philosophy is that the **economic consumption** of services happens in India; therefore, Indian GST should apply. Since the foreign supplier cannot practically be brought into the Indian GST compliance net for most transactions, the **recipient in India** is made the taxpayer under the RCM mechanism.

**Section 2(11) of the IGST Act, 2017** defines "import of services" as:
> *"the supply of any service, where—*
> *(i) the supplier of service is located outside India;*
> *(ii) the recipient of service is located in India; and*
> *(iii) the place of supply of service is in India."*

All three conditions must be simultaneously satisfied for a supply to qualify as "import of services." This is an important limitation — if the place of supply (determined under Section 13 IGST Act) falls outside India, it is NOT an import of services and Indian GST does not apply.

**Import of services for personal use:** Under Section 7(1)(b), the import of services for *personal consumption* (not in the course of business) is also a taxable supply. However, the RCM applies only when it is imported for business. If a private individual imports services for personal use, technically it is a supply but practically no RCM mechanism exists since the individual recipient may not be registered. This creates a compliance gap for non-business recipients.

### Who Pays the Tax

**Section 5(3) of the IGST Act, 2017** read with **Rule 3 of the CGST Rules** and the applicable IGST notification establishes that for import of services, the **recipient of services located in India** is liable to pay IGST on a reverse charge basis.

The foreign supplier does not collect or remit Indian GST. The Indian recipient:
- Determines the value of the import (generally the consideration paid in foreign currency, converted to INR at the RBI reference rate on the date of supply).
- Determines the applicable GST rate (same rate as if the service were supplied domestically).
- Issues a **self-invoice** under Section 31(3)(f) read with Rule 46.
- Pays **IGST only** (no CGST + SGST since import of services is an inter-state supply, treated as an intra-India cross-border transaction).
- Claims ITC on the IGST paid, subject to conditions.

**Why IGST only?** Import of services is treated as an **inter-state supply** under Section 7(4) of the IGST Act (supply of services where the supplier is outside India and recipient is in India). Therefore, **IGST** applies at the applicable rate. There is no CGST + SGST split for import of services.

**[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]** For a recipient registered in Maharashtra, the IGST paid on import of services is credited to the Maharashtra GSTIN. The IGST can be used to offset CGST, SGST (Maharashtra), or IGST outward liability in that order. Maharashtra-based multinational company subsidiaries frequently deal with large volumes of import of services from parent entities, and this is a key area of scrutiny in Maharashtra GST audits.

### Place of Supply Rules

**Section 13 of the IGST Act, 2017** governs the place of supply of services where either the supplier or the recipient is outside India.

**General rule — Section 13(2):** The place of supply shall be the **location of the recipient of services**. Since the recipient is in India, the place of supply is India — satisfying condition (iii) of the import definition. This confirms IGST applicability.

**Specific overrides — Sections 13(3) to 13(13):** For certain service categories, Section 13 prescribes specific place of supply rules that may deviate from the general rule:

| Service Category | Place of Supply | Section 13 |
|---|---|---|
| Services directly related to immovable property | Location of the immovable property | 13(4) |
| Restaurant/catering/personal grooming services | Location where services are performed | 13(5) |
| Training/apparel/research services | Location where services are performed | 13(5) |
| Services related to admission to events | Location where event is held | 13(5) |
| Transportation of goods (excluding courier) | Location where services are performed | 13(9) |
| Passenger transportation | Location where passenger embarks | 13(10) |
| Services on board a conveyance | First scheduled point of departure | 13(11) |
| Online Information Database Access and Retrieval (OIDAR) | Location of recipient | 13(12) |
| Residual (all other services) | Location of recipient | 13(2) |

For most business services imported by Indian companies (consulting, software, IT services, legal, finance), Section 13(2) applies — **place of supply = location of recipient in India** — confirming IGST applicability.

**Critical edge case:** If a foreign company sends its employee to India to perform services at a specific site, and the place of supply under Section 13(5) is determined to be the location in India where services are performed, the result is the same (IGST on import of services). However, if the Indian engagement requires the employee to perform part-work overseas, the apportionment of value between India-sited and overseas-sited work may become relevant to determine the IGST base.

### OIDAR Services
{law_reference: "Section 2(17) IGST Act, 2017; Rule 4 of the IGST (Procedure for furnishing Returns and Payment of Tax by OIDAR Service Providers) Rules; Circular No. 204/16/2023-GST"}

**Definition — Section 2(17) IGST Act, 2017:**
"Online information and database access or retrieval services" means services whose delivery is mediated by information technology over the internet or an electronic network and the nature of which renders their supply essentially automated and involving minimal human intervention, and impossible to ensure in the absence of information technology, including:
- Advertising on the internet.
- Provision of cloud services.
- Provision of e-books, movies, music, software, and other intangibles through telecommunication networks or internet.
- Providing data or information retrievable or otherwise, to any person in electronic form through a computer network.
- Online supplies of digital content (movies, TV shows, music, etc.).
- Digital data storage.
- Online gaming.

**Special significance of OIDAR:**

For business-to-business (B2B) transactions, OIDAR services from foreign providers are treated like any other import of services — the Indian registered recipient pays IGST on RCM.

However, **Circular 204/16/2023-GST** (and earlier related circulars) provides clarity that foreign OIDAR service providers supplying to non-business recipients in India (B2C) are required to **register in India** on a simplified basis and pay IGST on a forward charge. This is the Indian equivalent of the EU VAT MOSS regime.

**Post-1 October 2023 amendment:** The Finance Act 2023 amended the IGST Act to bring unregistered Indian recipients of OIDAR services within the tax net. An Indian company receiving, say, Netflix business subscriptions, or GitHub Enterprise from a foreign provider, pays RCM IGST. Indian individuals receiving services directly from OIDAR providers should technically be covered by the provider's Indian registration/remittance obligation.

**[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]** Maharashtra businesses (IT companies in Pune/Hyderabad corridor areas, financial services in Mumbai) are the largest consumers of OIDAR services. The Maharashtra GST department's audit teams have, in recent assessment cycles (2023-2025), identified ITC mismatches arising from OIDAR import of services not being reported in GSTR-3B Table 3.1(d), leading to demand notices. Proper GSTR-3B reporting and self-invoice issuance is essential.

### Examples

**Example 1 — Software subscription from US company:**

> **Facts:** Pune-based IT company (registered GSTIN: 27XXXXX) subscribes to Microsoft Azure cloud services from Microsoft Corporation, USA, at USD 5,000 per month. RBI reference rate on invoice date: ₹84 per USD. Invoice value: ₹4,20,000.
>
> **Classification:** Import of OIDAR services (cloud services). Place of supply: Location of recipient (Pune, Maharashtra) per Section 13(2)/(12). IGST applicable.
>
> **RCM process:** The IT company issues a self-invoice for ₹4,20,000 + IGST @ 18% = ₹75,600. Total: ₹4,95,600. IGST of ₹75,600 paid in cash through Electronic Cash Ledger. ITC of ₹75,600 (IGST) claimed in the same or subsequent period in GSTR-3B Table 4. Net tax cost = nil if full ITC is available.

**Example 2 — Management consulting from UK firm:**

> **Facts:** Mumbai-based Conglomerate Holdings Ltd. (registered) engages McKinsey & Company UK for strategic advisory services at GBP 1,00,000. RBI rate: ₹107 per GBP. Value: ₹1,07,00,000.
>
> **Classification:** Import of services (management consulting). Place of supply: Location of recipient (Mumbai, Maharashtra) per Section 13(2). IGST @ 18%.
>
> **RCM process:** Self-invoice for ₹1,07,00,000. IGST = ₹19,26,000. Paid in cash. ITC claimed. The company should ensure the foreign contract/Statement of Work (SOW) is retained as supporting documentation for the self-invoice. If the payment is through banking channels (SWIFT), the FEMA compliance (RBI/AD bank reporting) should also be in order.

**Example 3 — Cloud infrastructure services from Singapore:**

> **Facts:** Bengaluru branch of a Maharashtra-registered e-commerce company uses Amazon Web Services (AWS) Singapore for server hosting at ₹8,00,000 per month.
>
> **Classification:** OIDAR (cloud computing). Section 13(12) — place of supply = location of recipient. IGST @ 18%.
>
> **RCM process:** Self-invoice monthly. IGST = ₹1,44,000 per month. Pay in cash. Claim ITC. Note: If the company uses AWS through an Indian reseller (AWS India Pvt. Ltd.), the Indian entity charges GST on a forward charge — no RCM in that case.

### Self-Invoice and Compliance

**Issuance of self-invoice:** The recipient must issue the self-invoice under Rule 46 containing:
- A sequential serial number.
- Date of the self-invoice.
- Name, address, and GSTIN of the recipient (the Indian company).
- Name and address of the foreign supplier (no GSTIN for foreign supplier).
- Description of services.
- Value in INR (foreign currency converted at RBI reference rate as on the date of supply or date of invoice of the foreign supplier).
- Applicable IGST rate and amount.
- Signature of the authorised signatory of the recipient.

**Time of supply for import of services — Section 13(3) of the CGST Act (for RCM):**
The time of supply shall be the earliest of:
- Date of payment (as per books of account or date of debit in bank account).
- Date immediately following **60 days** from the date of invoice by the foreign supplier.

Where neither of the above can be ascertained, the date of entry in the books of the recipient is the time of supply.

**ITC on import of services:** ITC of IGST paid on import of services is eligible under Section 16, subject to:
- The recipient is a registered person.
- The import is for business purposes.
- The IGST has actually been paid in cash (cannot use ITC to pay RCM, as discussed above).
- The self-invoice has been issued.
- The supply is not used for blocked credits under Section 17(5).
- The ITC is claimed within the time limit (Section 16(4) — earlier of 30 November following the year-end or date of filing of annual return for the relevant year).

## Category 3: Import of Goods
{law_reference: "Section 5(1) IGST Act, 2017; Customs Act, 1962; Section 3(7) and 3(9) Customs Tariff Act, 1975; Section 20 IGST Act, 2017"}

### Customs + IGST Interaction

The import of goods into India is governed by **Chapter V of the IGST Act, 2017** read with the **Customs Act, 1962** and the **Customs Tariff Act, 1975**. Technically, import of goods is **not RCM** in the traditional sense — it is instead a levy of IGST at the customs frontier, collected by the Customs department, under the authority of Section 5(1) of the IGST Act read with Section 3(7) of the Customs Tariff Act.

However, it is included in this Path A document because:
1. It is frequently confused with import of services RCM.
2. The **effect on the importer** (paying tax at the border, then claiming ITC) is functionally similar to RCM.
3. Many practitioners treat the **Bill of Entry** as the equivalent of a self-invoice for ITC purposes.

**The legal mechanism:**

Under **Section 5(1) of the IGST Act**, IGST is levied on all inter-state supplies of goods or services. Under **Section 7(2) IGST Act**, supply of goods imported into India is treated as an inter-state supply.

**Section 3(7) of the Customs Tariff Act** provides that IGST shall be levied on imported goods at the rate applicable to like goods supplied domestically. The taxable value is the **customs transaction value** (under Section 14 of the Customs Act) plus basic customs duty (BCD) plus any other duties.

**Calculation structure:**
```
CIF Value (in INR at exchange rate)
+ Basic Customs Duty (BCD)
+ Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) = 10% of BCD
= Assessable Value for IGST
× IGST Rate (same as domestic GST rate for the goods)
= IGST payable
```

**Section 3(9)** provides for GST Compensation Cess on imported goods where applicable (e.g., luxury cars, tobacco, aerated drinks — subject to cess under the GST Compensation to States Act, 2017).

The Customs department collects BCD, SWS, and IGST. The importer pays all of these upfront at the port of entry, using the **Bill of Entry** as the tax document.

### How ITC Flows on Import of Goods

**Rule 36(1)(b) of the CGST Rules, 2017** provides that ITC on import of goods shall be available on the basis of the **Bill of Entry**. No tax invoice from the foreign supplier is needed for ITC. The Bill of Entry itself is a valid document for ITC claim.

**Conditions for ITC on import of goods:**
1. The goods are used in the course or furtherance of business.
2. The goods are not covered by the blocked credit provisions of Section 17(5).
3. The Bill of Entry reflects the correct IGST amount paid.
4. The ITC is claimed within the time limit under Section 16(4).
5. The GSTIN of the importer on the Bill of Entry must match the GSTIN under which ITC is claimed.

**GSTR-2B and Bill of Entry:** The IGST paid at customs is auto-populated in **GSTR-2B** of the importer from ICEGATE (Indian Customs EDI System) data fed to the GST portal. From May 2021, this auto-population has been functional, and reconciliation between GSTR-2B and the actual Bill of Entry is a key ITC compliance task.

**Multiple GST registrations and import:** Where a company has multiple GST registrations (e.g., head office in Maharashtra, branches in other states), the GSTIN on the Bill of Entry determines which registration can claim the ITC. If goods are imported for a Delhi branch but the Bill of Entry shows the Maharashtra GSTIN, the Maharashtra entity must transfer the ITC via the inter-state ITC transfer mechanism (input service distributor / ISD route or direct allocation).

**[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]** Mumbai (JNPT — Jawaharlal Nehru Port Trust) and Mumbai Air Cargo Complex are among India's largest ports of entry. Maharashtra-registered importers process a large volume of Bills of Entry through these ports. The Maharashtra GST department, in coordination with the Customs department, monitors cases where IGST on import is paid but ITC is not reflected in GSTR-3B, indicating possible mismatches requiring reconciliation.

### Distinction from Import of Services

| Parameter | Import of Goods | Import of Services |
|---|---|---|
| Governing Law | IGST Act + Customs Act + Customs Tariff Act | IGST Act + CGST Act |
| Collection Authority | Customs Department (at port/airport) | GST recipient (self-assessment) |
| Tax Payment Method | At customs frontier; using TR-6 challan / ICEGATE payment | Self-payment via Electronic Cash Ledger in GSTR-3B |
| Document for ITC | Bill of Entry | Self-Invoice (Rule 46 / Section 31(3)(f)) |
| Time of Tax Payment | Before clearance of goods from customs | Date of payment or 60 days from invoice |
| Is it technically RCM? | No — it is a border levy | Yes — it is RCM under Section 5(3) IGST Act |
| ITC Availability | Yes, IGST paid on imports | Yes, IGST paid on RCM |
| GSTR-3B Reporting | Table 4A(1) (ITC — imports) | Table 3.1(d) (RCM liability) + Table 4A(2) (RCM ITC) |
| GST Rate Basis | Domestic equivalent rate + BCD + SWS adjustments | Domestic equivalent rate |

## Category 4: Notified Services under Section 9(3)
{law_reference: "Section 9(3) CGST Act, 2017; Section 5(3) IGST Act, 2017; Notification No. 13/2017-CT(Rate) dated 28-Jun-2017 as amended up to May 2026; Notification No. 10/2017-IGST(Rate)"}

**Section 9(3) of the CGST Act, 2017** empowers the Central Government, on the recommendation of the GST Council, to notify certain categories of supply of goods or services on which the tax shall be paid by the recipient of such supply on reverse charge. This is the backbone of the RCM framework for specified services, and unlike Section 9(4), it applies **regardless of whether the supplier is registered or unregistered**.

**Notification No. 13/2017-CT(Rate) dated 28 June 2017** (amended multiple times) is the primary notification specifying the categories of services under Section 9(3). The equivalent IGST notification is **Notification No. 10/2017-IGST(Rate)**, and each state has issued a mirror notification for SGST. **[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]** Maharashtra's Section 9(3) SGST Notification mirrors the CGST notification.

The categories below represent the complete current list as of May 2026.

### 4A: Goods Transport Agency (GTA) Services
{law_reference: "Notification No. 13/2017-CT(Rate) as amended; Notification No. 11/2017-CT(Rate); Circular No. 164/20/2021 dated 27-Oct-2021; Circular No. 136/6/2020 dated 3-Apr-2020; Press Release dated 18-May-2022 (GST Council 47th meeting recommendations); Notification No. 3/2022-CT(Rate) dated 13-Jul-2022"}

**Definition of GTA — what makes a transporter a GTA:**

A **Goods Transport Agency (GTA)** is defined in the GST law (Explanation to Notification 13/2017) as any person who provides service in relation to transport of goods by road and issues a **consignment note**, by whatever name called. The issue of a consignment note is the critical distinguishing criterion.

A transporter who merely transports goods but does NOT issue a consignment note is **not a GTA** — they are providing transport services but not as a GTA. In such cases, no RCM applies under the GTA entry.

**What is a consignment note?** A consignment note is a document issued by the GTA acknowledging receipt of goods for transportation, stating the details of the consignor, consignee, origin, destination, and freight charges. It creates a contract of carriage. The lorry receipt (LR), bilty, or freight memo qualifies as a consignment note for this purpose.

**Who pays RCM on GTA services:**

Under the original Notification 13/2017-CT(Rate), RCM on GTA services applies when the **recipient** is any of the following:

1. Any factory registered under or governed by the Factories Act, 1948.
2. Any Society registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860, or under any other law for the time being in force in any part of India.
3. Any Co-operative Society established by or under any law.
4. Any person registered under the CGST Act or IGST Act or UTGST Act or any SGST Act.
5. Any body corporate established, by or under any law.
6. Any partnership firm whether registered or not under any law including association of persons.
7. Any Casual Taxable Person.

**Important:** If the recipient is **none of the above** (e.g., an individual receiving goods for personal use, an unregistered individual trader below threshold), RCM does not apply and no GST is payable on the GTA's services by default (subject to forward charge option).

**Rate structure and forward charge option (post-July 2022 amendment):**

The **47th GST Council Meeting (May 2022)** and consequent **Notification No. 3/2022-CT(Rate) dated 13 July 2022** brought a significant change to the GTA regime:

| Option | Rate | ITC to GTA | Forward/Reverse Charge | Who Pays Tax |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Option 1: GTA pays on Forward Charge | 12% of freight | GTA can claim ITC | Forward Charge | GTA |
| Option 2: GTA pays on Forward Charge | 5% of freight | GTA cannot claim ITC | Forward Charge | GTA |
| Option 3: Recipient pays on Reverse Charge | 5% of freight | Recipient claims ITC | Reverse Charge (RCM) | Recipient |

**Annual opt-in for forward charge:** A GTA wishing to pay on forward charge for a financial year must file a declaration on the GST portal by **15 March** of the preceding financial year. If no declaration is filed, the default is RCM (recipient pays 5%). If the GTA files the declaration, recipients are informed via the GST portal and do NOT pay RCM for that year.

**Consignor/Consignee Liability Matrix:**

Under the original Notification 13/2017, both the consignor and the consignee were liable for RCM. However, **Circular No. 136/6/2020** clarified that where both consignor and consignee are in the notified class:
- The person **actually paying freight** is liable to pay RCM.
- If the contract specifies "freight to be paid by consignee," the consignee pays RCM.
- If "freight paid" is marked on the lorry receipt (freight pre-paid by consignor), the consignor pays RCM.

**Composite supply by GTA — Circular 136/6/2020:**
Where a GTA provides additional services along with transportation (e.g., loading/unloading, transit insurance, packing/unpacking), the entire supply is a **composite supply** with the principal supply being transport. The entire composite supply is taxed at the GTA rate (5%/12%) and the RCM/forward charge rules apply to the entire value.

**Practical example — Manufacturing company and unregistered GTA:**

> **Facts:** XYZ Automotive Ltd. (registered, Pune, Maharashtra **[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]**) hires Ramkrishna Transport (a small GTA, registered) to transport auto components from Pune to Chennai. Freight: ₹50,000. Ramkrishna Transport has NOT opted for forward charge for FY 2025-26.
>
> **RCM applicability:** XYZ Automotive Ltd. is a "body corporate" and a "person registered under CGST Act" — both listed categories. GTA issues a consignment note. Default regime = RCM.
>
> **Tax computation:** RCM = 5% on ₹50,000 = ₹2,500 (CGST ₹1,250 + SGST/IGST as applicable based on inter-state/intra-state movement). For Pune to Chennai (inter-state): IGST @ 5% = ₹2,500. Paid by XYZ Automotive in cash through Electronic Cash Ledger. ITC of ₹2,500 (IGST) claimed in GSTR-3B.

### 4B: Legal Services by Advocates
{law_reference: "Notification No. 13/2017-CT(Rate) Entry No. 2; Circular No. 177/09/2022 dated 3-Aug-2022; Circular No. 211/5/2024 dated 26-Jun-2024"}

**Coverage of the notification:**

Entry 2 of Notification 13/2017-CT(Rate) covers:
> *"Services supplied by an individual advocate including a senior advocate by way of legal services, directly or indirectly, to any business entity located in the taxable territory."*

And:
> *"Services supplied by a firm of advocates including a limited liability partnership of advocates by way of legal services, directly or indirectly, to any business entity located in the taxable territory."*

**Scope of "legal services":** Legal services means any service provided in relation to advice, consultancy, or assistance in any branch of law, in any manner, and includes representational services before any court, tribunal, or authority.

**Individual advocate vs partnership firm:**

*Individual advocate:* The individual advocate (whether junior or senior) is the *supplier* of the service. Even senior advocates (designated as such under the Advocates Act) — whose fees can run into lakhs per appearance — are covered. The individual advocate does NOT charge GST to the business client; instead the client pays RCM.

*Partnership firm of advocates / LLP of advocates:* Similarly covered. A law firm (whether a traditional partnership or an LLP of advocates) providing legal services to a business entity triggers RCM on the business entity client.

**Business entity — definition:** The RCM applies only when the recipient is a **"business entity."** This term is defined in the notification's explanation as "any person carrying out any activity relating to industry, commerce, or any other business or profession." This is broader than "body corporate" and includes sole proprietors, partnerships, Hindu Undivided Families, and trusts engaged in business.

**Exclusion — senior advocate to junior advocate:**
Where a senior advocate provides advisory/chamber services to a junior advocate (not a business entity), RCM does not apply. The junior advocate is receiving the service in a professional capacity, not as a business entity.

**Exclusion — advocate to private individual:**
If an individual (not carrying on business) hires an advocate for a personal matter (property dispute, matrimonial case, personal injury claim), the recipient is not a business entity. No RCM. The individual bears no GST obligation.

**Circular No. 177/09/2022 — clarification:**
This circular clarified that legal services by an individual advocate to a business entity (even if provided through a law firm) attract RCM. It also addressed that where a corporate legal department hires an individual advocate for arbitration, the company pays RCM on the entire fee including any out-of-pocket expenses included in the advocate's invoice.

**Circular No. 211/5/2024:**
This circular (dated 26 June 2024) further clarified that even where an advocate provides services to a government entity or a public sector undertaking (which are "business entities" engaged in commercial activities), RCM applies. Pure government departments and constitutional bodies that are not "business entities" under any commercial activity interpretation are excluded.

**[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC] Maharashtra interpretation:**
Maharashtra's GST department has historically taken a strict view on legal service RCM. In several audit reports for Maharashtra-based companies (2022-2024 cycle), the Maharashtra SGST department identified cases where companies paid retainers to small law firms without properly paying RCM (arguing the firm was small and unregistered). Post-Circular 177/09/2022, the position is clear: size or registration status of the law firm is irrelevant; RCM applies whenever a business entity receives legal services.

**Applicable rate:** CGST @ 9% + SGST @ 9% = 18% (for intra-state), or IGST @ 18% (for inter-state) on the advocate's fee. RCM paid by the business entity recipient.

**Practical example:**

> **Facts:** Mumbai-based ABC Infrastructure Pvt. Ltd. engages Senior Advocate Shri R.K. Sharma for representation in the Bombay High Court for a tax matter. Fee: ₹5,00,000.
>
> **RCM:** ABC Infrastructure Pvt. Ltd. pays RCM: CGST @ 9% = ₹45,000 + Maharashtra SGST @ 9% = ₹45,000 = Total RCM = ₹90,000. Paid in cash. Self-invoice issued by ABC. ITC of ₹90,000 claimed (if eligible — note that legal services for litigation may qualify as ITC where it relates to taxable business). **[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]**

### 4C: Services by Directors to Companies
{law_reference: "Notification No. 13/2017-CT(Rate) Entry No. 6; Circular No. 177/09/2022 dated 3-Aug-2022; Schedule III Entry 1 CGST Act; Explanation to Notification 13/2017"}

**Scope of the entry:**

Entry 6 of Notification 13/2017-CT(Rate):
> *"Services supplied by a director of a company or a body corporate to the said company or the body corporate."*

This RCM entry covers services rendered by a **director** (as defined under the Companies Act, 2013) to the company of which they are a director.

**Critical distinction — employment vs professional services:**

**Schedule III, Entry 1 of the CGST Act** excludes from the definition of supply: *"Services by an employee to the employer in the course of or in relation to his employment."* This means the salary paid to a whole-time director (who is an employee of the company) is **NOT a supply of services** and does NOT attract GST — neither forward charge nor RCM.

The RCM entry (Notification 13/2017) applies only to services that are **NOT in the nature of employment services.** The critical question is: what is the nature of the relationship?

**Category-wise analysis:**

| Director Type | Nature of Payment | GST Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Whole-time/Executive Director (employee) | Salary/perquisites under employment contract | NOT a supply; no GST; Schedule III exclusion |
| Whole-time Director — professional fee | Professional fee for specific advisory services beyond employment scope | RCM applies; rate 18% |
| Non-executive Director | Director's remuneration/commission | RCM applies; rate 18% |
| Independent Director | Sitting fees + commission | RCM applies; rate 18% |
| Director providing specific professional services | Consultancy, technical advisory etc. | RCM applies; rate 18% |

**Circular No. 177/09/2022 — detailed clarification:**
This crucial circular resolved a long-standing controversy:
- Where a director is both an employee (draws salary) AND provides separate professional services (draws a professional fee), the salary component is excluded from GST (Schedule III) but the professional fee component attracts RCM.
- The test is whether there exists an employer-employee relationship: if there is a service agreement or contract of service (not contract of employment), RCM applies.
- Where a company pays "remuneration" to a director under the Companies Act (MCA Schedule V), the classification as salary vs professional fee requires careful analysis of the underlying contractual arrangement.

**Sitting fees:** Sitting fees paid to independent directors for attending board/committee meetings are a classic RCM situation. The independent director renders board advisory services; the company pays RCM.

**Practical Example — Independent Director:**

> **Facts:** Reliance Chemicals Pvt. Ltd. (registered, Maharashtra **[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]**) pays ₹50,000 per board meeting to Independent Director Mr. A. Kumar (who is not otherwise employed by the company). 12 meetings per year = ₹6,00,000 annually.
>
> **RCM computation:**
> - Annual payment: ₹6,00,000
> - GST @ 18% (RCM): ₹1,08,000
> - CGST: ₹54,000 | Maharashtra SGST: ₹54,000
> - Paid by Reliance Chemicals (the company) on RCM basis.
> - Self-invoice issued per meeting or monthly consolidated.
> - ITC claimed by Reliance Chemicals (if the company has taxable outputs — note that if the company makes only taxable supplies, full ITC is available).

**[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]** Maharashtra's company secretarial and GST audit ecosystem frequently flags non-compliance with director-service RCM in annual audits. The amount paid to independent directors must appear in GSTR-3B Table 3.1(d) each month the meeting fee is paid, and the self-invoice must be maintained.

### 4D: Renting of Motor Vehicles
{law_reference: "Notification No. 29/2018-CT(Rate) dated 31-Dec-2018; Notification No. 22/2019-CT(Rate) dated 30-Sep-2019; Circular No. 164/20/2021 dated 27-Oct-2021"}

**Scope of the notification:**

Entry in Notification 22/2019-CT(Rate) (amending Notification 13/2017):
> *"Services by way of renting of a motor vehicle provided to a body corporate, by a person who is not a body corporate, to a body corporate — at 5% GST on RCM basis (where fuel cost is part of the consideration)."*

**Key parameters:**

1. **Type of vehicle:** Motor vehicle designed to carry passengers — including cars, SUVs, taxis, minibuses (up to 13 passengers). Does NOT cover goods vehicles (trucks, lorries — those fall under GTA). Does NOT cover buses or vehicles with seating capacity above 13 (different rate/treatment).

2. **Supplier is NOT a body corporate:** The RCM applies when the supplier (the person who rents out the vehicle) is an individual, a proprietorship, a partnership, or any other entity that is **not a body corporate.** If the supplier IS a body corporate (e.g., a fleet management company like Meru Cabs Pvt. Ltd. renting vehicles to another company), the forward charge applies — the supplier company charges GST at 12% on forward charge.

3. **Recipient IS a body corporate:** The RCM entry applies only when the recipient is a body corporate. A body corporate includes any company incorporated under the Companies Act, an LLP, a banking institution, an insurance company, etc.

4. **Fuel included in consideration:** The RCM entry at 5% applies where fuel cost is part of the consideration. Where the vehicle is rented without fuel (dry hire), different rate/treatment may apply.

**Summary matrix:**

| Supplier Type | Recipient Type | GST Treatment | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Individual / Non-body corporate | Body Corporate | RCM — Recipient pays | 5% IGST or 2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST |
| Body Corporate | Body Corporate | Forward Charge — Supplier charges | 12% (with ITC) |
| Individual / Non-body corporate | Non-body corporate entity | Forward Charge by supplier | 5% (if unregistered, RCM under 9(4) if applicable) |

**Circular No. 164/20/2021 — cab aggregators:**
This circular clarified the treatment of cab aggregator platforms (Ola, Uber) and their driver-partners:
- Cab aggregators are Electronic Commerce Operators (ECO). Under Section 9(5) CGST Act (not Section 9(3)), cab aggregators are liable to pay GST on services provided through their platform. This is a separate provision from the motor vehicle renting RCM.
- The motor vehicle renting RCM under Notification 13/2017 / Notification 22/2019 applies to direct renting arrangements (company hiring a car owner directly), NOT to cab aggregator platform transactions.

**Practical Example:**

> **Facts:** TechCorp Ltd. (a body corporate registered in Maharashtra **[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]**) hires Raju (an individual car owner, unregistered) to provide a sedan for employee transport at ₹30,000 per month (including fuel).
>
> **RCM analysis:** Supplier (Raju) is an individual — not a body corporate. Recipient (TechCorp Ltd.) is a body corporate. Vehicle carries passengers. Fuel included. RCM applies.
>
> **Tax computation:** ₹30,000 × 5% = ₹1,500 GST (RCM). Intra-Maharashtra: CGST ₹750 + SGST (Maharashtra) ₹750.
>
> **TechCorp's compliance:** Issue self-invoice for ₹30,000. Pay ₹1,500 in cash (CGST ₹750 to central, SGST ₹750 to Maharashtra). Claim ITC of ₹1,500 (subject to Section 17(5) — note that motor vehicle renting for employee transport may be blocked ITC under Section 17(5)(b) unless it is for further supply of transportation services or falls under the permitted exceptions for cab services).

### 4E: Security Services
{law_reference: "Notification No. 29/2018-CT(Rate) dated 31-Dec-2018 (adding Entry 14 to Notification 13/2017-CT(Rate)); Circular No. 164/20/2021"}

**Scope:**

Entry 14 of Notification 13/2017-CT(Rate) (as inserted by Notification 29/2018):
> *"Security services (services provided by way of supply of security personnel) provided to a registered person, by any person other than a body corporate."*

However, important **exceptions** (no RCM) apply:
- Recipient is a **body corporate** and supplier is also a **body corporate** → Forward charge (body corporate supplier charges GST at 18%).
- Recipient is a **government entity** (Central/State Government, local authority) → No RCM; government entities do not pay GST on security personnel services received.
- Recipient is an establishment of a government entity.
- Recipient is a registered person who is registered solely for the purpose of deducting TDS under Section 51 (not actually making taxable supplies).

**In practice — security services RCM matrix:**

| Supplier | Recipient | RCM? |
|---|---|---|
| Individual security agency (non-body corporate) | Any registered person (company, firm, LLP) | Yes — RCM @ 18% |
| Body corporate security company (e.g., G4S Secure Solutions India Pvt. Ltd.) | Any registered person | No — Forward Charge @ 18% (supplier charges GST) |
| Any security agency | Central/State Government department | No — Exempt/government exclusion |
| Any security agency | Registered PSU (body corporate) | Forward Charge by PSU if supplier is also body corporate; RCM if supplier is non-body corporate |

**Practical Example:**

> **Facts:** Videocon Industries Ltd. (registered, Maharashtra **[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]**) hires Suraksha Security (a proprietorship firm, GST registered) to provide 10 security guards for its factory at ₹1,50,000 per month.
>
> **RCM applicability:** Supplier (Suraksha Security) is a proprietorship — NOT a body corporate. Recipient (Videocon Industries) is a registered body corporate. Recipient is NOT a government entity. RCM applies under Entry 14.
>
> **Tax:** ₹1,50,000 × 18% = ₹27,000 GST on RCM. (CGST ₹13,500 + SGST Maharashtra ₹13,500). Self-invoice issued by Videocon Industries Ltd. Paid in cash. ITC claimed.

### 4F: Sponsorship Services
{law_reference: "Notification No. 13/2017-CT(Rate) Entry No. 5"}

**Scope:**

Entry 5 of Notification 13/2017-CT(Rate):
> *"Services by way of sponsorship to a body corporate or partnership firm."*

The RCM applies when:
- A person provides **sponsorship services** to a body corporate or partnership firm.
- The body corporate or partnership firm (the **sponsored entity**) pays the GST on RCM.

Wait — the structure is: the *sponsor* pays money to the event organiser/sporting entity/association etc. to get branding rights. The *event organiser* provides sponsorship services to the *sponsor*. The **sponsor** (if a body corporate or partnership firm) pays RCM.

**Practical mechanics:**
- Event company / sports team / association approaches a corporate for sponsorship.
- Corporate (body corporate) agrees to pay, say, ₹10,00,000 as sponsorship fee.
- The event company provides services (brand display, naming rights, hospitality) in return.
- The event company issues an invoice to the corporate.
- The corporate (sponsor who is a body corporate/partnership firm) pays RCM on the ₹10,00,000.

**Rate:** 18% GST (RCM by the sponsor recipient if body corporate/partnership firm).

**Exclusion:** If the sponsor is an individual or HUF (not a body corporate or firm), no RCM applies. The event company would need to charge GST on forward charge.

**Sports sponsorship specificity:** Corporate sponsorships of the Indian Premier League (IPL), ISL (Indian Super League), Kabaddi leagues, etc. — all major corporate sponsorships — attract RCM where the corporate sponsor is a body corporate or partnership firm.

**Practical Example:**

> **Facts:** Maharashtra Seamless Ltd. (a company registered in Maharashtra **[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]**) sponsors the Maharashtra Ranji Trophy cricket tournament at a fee of ₹25,00,000 paid to the Maharashtra Cricket Association.
>
> **RCM:** Maharashtra Seamless Ltd. (body corporate sponsor) pays RCM. GST @ 18% = ₹4,50,000. (CGST ₹2,25,000 + Maharashtra SGST ₹2,25,000, assuming intra-state). Self-invoice issued by Maharashtra Seamless Ltd. ITC claimed (subject to Section 17(5) — sponsorship of events may be business promotion; ITC generally available if it relates to the company's taxable business activities).

### 4G: Insurance Agent Services
{law_reference: "Notification No. 13/2017-CT(Rate) Entry No. 7"}

**Scope:**

Entry 7 of Notification 13/2017-CT(Rate):
> *"Services provided or agreed to be provided by an insurance agent to any person carrying on insurance business."*

**Mechanics:**
- Insurance agents (individual or corporate agents) source insurance policies and earn commissions from insurance companies.
- The insurance agent provides a service (lead generation, customer acquisition, policy servicing) to the **insurance company** (insurer).
- The **insurer** (insurance company) is the recipient of the insurance agent's service.
- The insurer pays GST on RCM on the commission/fee paid to the insurance agent.

This RCM entry was introduced to address the practical reality that insurance agents — particularly individual agents — are often small taxpayers who may not be registered or may be of large numbers making compliance difficult if each agent were required to charge GST on forward charge.

**Rate:** 18% on the commission/fee paid to the insurance agent.

**Applicability:** Applies regardless of whether the insurance agent is registered or unregistered. Even if the agent is a large corporate insurance marketing intermediary (company), the insurer pays RCM on the commission.

**Practical Example:**

> **Facts:** New India Assurance Company Ltd. pays ₹5,00,000 in commission to individual agent Suresh Mehta (unregistered, Mumbai) for sourcing general insurance policies.
>
> **RCM:** New India Assurance pays RCM: 18% × ₹5,00,000 = ₹90,000 GST. Self-invoice by New India Assurance. ITC claimed by New India Assurance (subject to any restriction on ITC for insurance companies based on their exempt/taxable output ratio).

### 4H: Recovery Agent Services
{law_reference: "Notification No. 13/2017-CT(Rate) Entry No. 10; Circular No. 11/11/2017-GST"}

**Scope:**

Entry 10 of Notification 13/2017-CT(Rate):
> *"Services provided or agreed to be provided by a recovery agent to a banking company or a financial institution or a non-banking financial company."*

**Mechanics:**
- Banks, NBFCs, housing finance companies (HFCs), and other financial institutions use recovery agents to recover dues from loan defaulters.
- The recovery agent provides services (field visits, negotiations, recovery activities) to the **lender** (bank/NBFC/financial institution).
- The lender pays GST on RCM on the fee/commission paid to the recovery agent.

**Rate:** 18% GST (RCM by the lender).

**Scope of "recovery agent":** Any person engaged for recovery of unpaid dues on behalf of a banking company, financial institution, or NBFC. This includes both individuals and corporate recovery agencies.

**Circular 11/11/2017-GST:** This circular provided early clarifications on the treatment of various banking and financial services, including recovery services.

**[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]** Mumbai is the financial capital of India, with the highest concentration of bank branches, NBFC headquarters, and financial institutions. This RCM entry is particularly significant for Maharashtra-registered financial entities. The Maharashtra GST department closely monitors recovery agency payments in the NBFC sector.

**Practical Example:**

> **Facts:** HDFC Bank Ltd. (registered) pays ₹2,00,000 per month to ABC Recovery Services (a proprietorship, registered) for recovery operations on NPA accounts in Maharashtra.
>
> **RCM:** HDFC Bank pays RCM: 18% × ₹2,00,000 = ₹36,000 GST per month. (CGST ₹18,000 + Maharashtra SGST ₹18,000 for intra-state; IGST ₹36,000 if cross-state recovery operations).

### 4I: Author/Music Composer/Artist Royalties
{law_reference: "Notification No. 13/2017-CT(Rate) Entry No. 8; Notification No. 15/2017-CT(Rate) dated 30-Jun-2017 (forward charge option); Section 13(1)(a) Copyright Act, 1957"}

**Scope:**

Entry 8 of Notification 13/2017-CT(Rate):
> *"Services provided or agreed to be provided by an author, music composer, photographer, poet, or other artist by way of transfer or permitting the use or enjoyment of a copyright covered under clause (a) of sub-section (1) of section 13 of the Copyright Act, 1957 relating to original literary, dramatic, musical or artistic works to a publisher, music company, producer, or the like."*

**What is covered:**
- Transfer of copyright (assignment) or licence of copyright by an author, composer, photographer, poet, or other creator.
- The work must be an **original literary, dramatic, musical, or artistic work** under Section 13(1)(a) of the Copyright Act.
- The service is provided to a publisher, music company, film producer, or similar commercial exploiter of the copyright.

**Who pays RCM:**
The **publisher / music company / producer** (recipient) pays GST on RCM on the royalty or advance against royalty paid to the author/artist.

**Forward charge option — Notification 15/2017-CT(Rate):**
An author/artist who is **registered under GST** may opt to pay GST on **forward charge** instead of the RCM applying. If the author opts for forward charge, they collect GST from the publisher/producer and remit it. This benefits authors who prefer to claim ITC on their expenses and manage their own GST compliance.

To exercise the forward charge option, the author must be registered and must indicate on their invoice that they are paying GST on forward charge. In practice, most individual authors are unregistered or prefer RCM (as it places the compliance burden on the publisher).

**Rate:** 12% GST (CGST 6% + SGST 6% intra-state, or IGST 12% inter-state) per Notification 11/2017-CT(Rate) (services rate notification).

**Practical Example:**

> **Facts:** Penguin Random House India (publisher, registered) pays ₹10,00,000 as an advance royalty to author Ms. P. Sharma (unregistered individual) for the rights to her debut novel.
>
> **RCM:** Penguin pays RCM: 12% × ₹10,00,000 = ₹1,20,000. (CGST ₹60,000 + SGST ₹60,000 if intra-state in Maharashtra). Self-invoice by Penguin. ITC claimed by Penguin.

### 4J: Services from Government/Local Authority
{law_reference: "Notification No. 13/2017-CT(Rate) Entry No. 5 and 6 of original notification; Explanation in notification defining 'government entity' and 'business entity'; Circular No. 119/38/2019-GST"}

**Scope:**

The original Notification 13/2017-CT(Rate) includes:

*Entry (b) — Services provided by Government/Local Authority to a business entity:*
> *"Taxable services provided or agreed to be provided by the Central Government, State Government, Union Territory, or local authority to a business entity, other than specified exempt services."*

**Excluded services (forward charge or exempt):**
- Services by way of any activity in relation to any function entrusted to a municipality under Article 243W of the Constitution.
- Services by way of any activity in relation to any function entrusted to a Panchayat under Article 243G.
- Services by way of renting of immovable property (covered separately — see Category 5).
- Postal services.
- Services by a postal department.
- Transport of passengers by railways.
- Transport of goods by railways.
- Services provided to individuals/households.

**What IS covered (government services attracting RCM on business entity recipient):**
- Spectrum/frequency allocation fees.
- Mining lease fees.
- License fees for commercial activities (license to carry on a regulated industry).
- Rights to use natural resources (forest, water, sand, minerals).
- Airport/port development fees paid by commercial operators.
- Government services rendered to businesses in their regulatory capacity.

**Who pays RCM:** The **business entity** (any person carrying on industry, commerce, or any business or profession) that receives the government/local authority service pays GST on RCM.

**Rate:** Applicable rate for the service (varies by service type — generally 18%).

**Circular 119/38/2019-GST:** This circular clarified the distinction between services by government as part of sovereign functions vs. commercial activities, and the scope of the business entity RCM.

**[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC] Maharashtra Local Authorities and MIDC:**
Maharashtra Industrial Development Corporation (MIDC) is a statutory body. Services provided by MIDC to industrial units (lease of plots, water supply, infrastructure services in MIDC estates) are subject to GST, and RCM may apply where the provision is by MIDC (government/local authority equivalent) to a business entity. Maharashtra-based businesses in MIDC areas frequently deal with MIDC charges and must evaluate each charge for RCM applicability. The **Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC)** and other Maharashtra municipal corporations providing services to business entities (advertisement hoardings, licences) also trigger this entry.

**Practical Example:**

> **Facts:** Star Manufacturing Pvt. Ltd. (registered, Pune) receives a 30-year lease from MIDC for an industrial plot. Annual lease premium: ₹5,00,000. MIDC does not separately charge GST.
>
> **RCM:** Star Manufacturing pays RCM: 18% × ₹5,00,000 = ₹90,000 per year. (Maharashtra SGST component: ₹45,000 + CGST ₹45,000). Self-invoice annual or monthly as per payment terms. ITC claimed (subject to Section 17(5) if the land/building relates to blocked ITC).

### 4K: Additional Notified Categories (as of May 2026)

The GST Council and Central Government have periodically added new categories to Notification 13/2017-CT(Rate). The following additional categories have been notified and are in effect as of May 2026:

**4K.1 — Services by way of renting of residential dwelling to a registered person:**
{law_reference: "Notification No. 5/2022-CT(Rate) dated 13-Jul-2022 (effective 18-Jul-2022)"}

With effect from **18 July 2022**, GST @ 18% applies under RCM on:
> *"Services by way of renting of residential dwelling to a registered person."*

Before July 2022, renting of residential dwellings was fully exempt under GST (Entry 12 of Notification 12/2017-CT(Rate)). Post July 2022, the exemption continues for rentals to **unregistered** tenants (i.e., an individual renting a flat for personal residence). However, if the **tenant is a registered person** (e.g., a company rents a flat for accommodating its employees, or a proprietor uses a residential property for business and is registered under GST), then RCM applies at 18%.

**Conditions for applicability:**
- Property: Residential dwelling (apartment, house, flat, bungalow designed for residential use).
- Recipient: Registered person under GST (the tenant company or individual registered for GST).
- The landlord is the supplier; RCM is paid by the tenant.

**Exemption preserved for:** Renting by registered person landlord to unregistered tenant for personal residence use.

**[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]** In Mumbai's expensive real estate market, it is common for companies to lease residential flats for visiting executives, top management, and employee housing. Post-18 July 2022, all such corporate rentals of residential property attract RCM at 18%. Maharashtra's large number of registered businesses renting residential flats make this a high-volume RCM category. Maharashtra GST audit teams have been particularly active in identifying non-compliance in this category (FY 2022-23 and 2023-24 audit cycles).

**Practical Example:**

> **Facts:** Infosys BPM Ltd. (registered, Mumbai **[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]**) rents a 3-BHK apartment at Bandra, Mumbai from Mr. Patel (individual, unregistered landlord) at ₹1,20,000 per month for accommodation of its visiting global executives.
>
> **RCM:** Tenant (Infosys BPM Ltd.) is a registered person. The property is a residential dwelling. RCM applies: 18% × ₹1,20,000 = ₹21,600 per month. (CGST ₹10,800 + Maharashtra SGST ₹10,800). Self-invoice issued by Infosys BPM Ltd. ITC availability: Note — ITC on guest house / accommodation expenses for employees may be restricted under Section 17(5)(b) (blocked credit for food, beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services, and similar expenses — check if accommodation for employees falls under "club membership fees, life insurance premium" blocking). This is a nuanced ITC eligibility issue requiring case-by-case analysis.

**4K.2 — Services by Overseeing Committee Members to RBI:**
{law_reference: "Notification No. 33/2017-CT(Rate)"}

Services provided by members of Overseeing Committee (constituted by the Reserve Bank of India) to the RBI are subject to RCM. The RBI pays RCM on fees paid to these members.

**4K.3 — Services of Business Facilitators / Business Correspondents:**
{law_reference: "Notification No. 17/2017-CT(Rate)"}

Services provided by a business facilitator or business correspondent to a banking company, micro-finance institution, or national bank for agricultural purposes — RCM applicable on the banking company/institution.

**4K.4 — Services by Agricultural Produce Grading/Packing:**
Certain agricultural supply chain services to registered recipients may attract RCM provisions under specific notifications issued in 2024-2025. Practitioners should verify the latest notification position for their specific sector.

## Category 5: Government/Local Authority as Supplier
{law_reference: "Notification No. 13/2017-CT(Rate) and 12/2017-CT(Rate); Section 2(53) CGST Act, 2017; Article 366(12) Constitution of India; Circular No. 119/38/2019-GST"}

### Government Entity as Supplier Concept

**Section 2(53) of the CGST Act, 2017** defines "Government" as the Central Government. State Governments are separately identified. "Local authority" is defined in Section 2(69) to include municipal committees, panchayats, cantonment boards, port trusts, and similar bodies.

When a government entity (Central/State Government, local authority) makes a supply of goods or services to a business, the GST treatment depends on the nature of the activity:

**Classification of government activities:**

1. **Sovereign/Constitutional functions** — cannot be supplied commercially; not within GST scope. Example: issuing passports, adjudicating disputes, legislative activities, police services.

2. **Non-business public services** — postal services for letters (basic postal), transport of passengers/goods (railways in basic class) — may be exempt from GST or specifically zero-rated.

3. **Commercial/business services rendered to businesses** — these are taxable and may attract either forward charge (where the government entity charges GST) or RCM (where the business recipient pays).

The GST Council policy is to minimise administrative burden on government departments for GST compliance. Hence, where government provides a service to a business entity, RCM is the preferred mechanism — the business entity (registered recipient) pays the tax.

### Non-Business Government Activities: Not GST

Services that are purely in the exercise of statutory/regulatory powers (not involving a commercial element) are outside the scope of supply. Examples:
- Fines and penalties imposed by government — not a supply.
- Court fees — not a supply.
- Taxes, levies, duties — not supplies in themselves.
- Regulatory approvals granted as a matter of right under public law (e.g., building permits where the fee is a regulatory charge) — debatable, and fact-specific.

### Business Activities: Forward or Reverse Charge

Where government engages in business activities (renting out government property, operating airports/ports commercially, providing water/electricity commercially), GST applies:
- If the government entity is registered and charges GST on its invoice: forward charge, no RCM for recipient.
- If the government entity provides a taxable service to a business entity but does not charge GST (or is not required to register): RCM on the business entity recipient.

### Renting of Immovable Property by Government

The renting of any immovable property by the **Central Government, State Government, or Union Territory** to a **business entity** is a taxable supply. The **business entity tenant** pays RCM.

**[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC] Maharashtra Government properties:**

A common scenario in Maharashtra is where industrial/commercial land owned by the State Government of Maharashtra (or MIDC, CIDCO, MMRDA, SRA, and similar Maharashtra statutory authorities) is given on lease or licence to private businesses. The lease premium, annual rent, or maintenance charges paid by the business to these authorities may attract RCM where:
- The authority is providing a "service" (not merely a sovereign function).
- The recipient is a registered business entity.

Maharashtra industries dealing with MIDC plot leases, MMRDA commercial complex rentals, or SRA (Slum Rehabilitation Authority) commercial premises should evaluate each payment for RCM applicability. MIDC has been clarified (by Maharashtra AAR and subsequent circulars) to be a "government entity" for GST purposes, and its rental/lease income from business entities attracts GST where not specifically exempt.

**Practical Example:**

> **Facts:** Omega Hotels Ltd. (registered, Maharashtra **[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]**) occupies a prime commercial property in South Mumbai owned by the Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC). Annual licence fee: ₹80,00,000.
>
> **RCM applicability:** BMC is a local authority. Renting of immovable property by local authority to business entity: RCM on business entity tenant.
>
> **Tax:** ₹80,00,000 × 18% = ₹14,40,000. (CGST ₹7,20,000 + Maharashtra SGST ₹7,20,000). Paid by Omega Hotels Ltd. on RCM basis. Self-invoice issued. ITC claimed (hotel services are taxable at 12%/18%; ITC on property related costs generally available).
