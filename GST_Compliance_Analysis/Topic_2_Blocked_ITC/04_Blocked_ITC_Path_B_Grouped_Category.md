## How to Use Path B

Path B organises the blocked ITC provisions not in statutory order (as Path A does) but by **practical category** — the way expenses actually arise in a business. A practitioner who receives an invoice for a gym membership, a catering bill, or a renovation cost can go directly to the relevant Group and find whether ITC is blocked, what the exception conditions are, and what documentation is required.

**Path B is not a substitute for the statute.** The underlying law is always Section 17(5) of the CGST Act, 2017. Path B is a practitioner-oriented navigation tool. For the corresponding statutory clause, see the **Path A vs Path B Mapping Table** at the end of this file.

**Who should use Path B:**
- CA/CS/CMA professionals advising clients on invoice-level ITC eligibility
- Accounts payable teams reviewing invoices before posting to Electronic Credit Ledger
- Internal auditors conducting ITC compliance reviews
- Tax consultants preparing departmental audit responses

**Guiding Principle:** The fundamental question at every step is — *Is this inward supply being received by the business to make taxable outward supplies, or is it for personal use, personal benefit, or a purpose excluded by statute?* Section 17(5) is a list of absolute exclusions. If the supply falls within the list and no exception applies, the block is complete — there is no partial allowance, no proportionate claim, and no business purpose defence.

## Group 1: Personal Consumption Items

{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(b)(i), 17(5)(g) CGST Act, 2017"}
{nature_of_block: "Absolute — no exception unless mandated by law"}
{gst_form_reporting: "GSTR-3B Table 4D(2)"}

### The Common Thread

All items in Group 1 share one characteristic: the ultimate beneficiary is an individual — an employee, director, or proprietor — and the supply serves **personal consumption** rather than the making of outward taxable supplies. The CGST Act recognises that a business entity could easily re-route personal expenditures as business expenses to claim ITC, which is why Section 17(5) explicitly blocks these categories.

### 1.1 Personal Grooming, Beauty Treatment, and Salon Services

**Statutory Provision:** Section 17(5)(b)(i) — ITC is blocked on beauty treatment, health services, cosmetic and plastic surgery.

**Scope of the block:**
- Company-paid salon visits for directors, partners, or employees: **ITC blocked**
- Corporate wellness packages including facials, massages, and grooming sessions: **ITC blocked**
- Aesthetic dental procedures (veneers, cosmetic whitening) paid by employer: **ITC blocked**
- Hair transplant, cosmetic plastic surgery funded by company: **ITC blocked**
- Dermatological skin care treatments: **ITC blocked**

**What is NOT blocked:**
- Medical treatment for illness or work-related injury — these fall under a different analysis (see Factories Act discussion below)
- Corrective dental work necessitated by an accident at workplace: potentially not a "cosmetic" procedure

**Decision Test:** *Is the procedure for enhancing appearance (personal) or for treating a medically necessary condition (potentially business-related)?* The line is not always bright, but the default is that beauty and grooming treatments are personal.

**Examples:**
- Company pays ₹15,000 (plus 18% GST = ₹2,700) for a spa treatment for a director after a client meeting: **₹2,700 ITC blocked**
- Company pays ₹8,000 (plus GST) for a uniform purchase for factory workers: **ITC NOT blocked** (business necessity; not personal consumption)
- Company pays ₹25,000 for a corporate wellness subscription including yoga, meditation, and beauty treatments packaged together: **ITC blocked** on the entire package; if separately invoiced and yoga/meditation can be carved out, analyse separately

### 1.2 Health Services for Personal Use

**Statutory Provision:** Section 17(5)(b)(i) — ITC blocked on "health services."

**The critical distinction** is between:

| Nature of Health Service | ITC Status | Basis |
|--------------------------|------------|-------|
| Personal medical treatment funded by employer (voluntary) | **Blocked** | 17(5)(b)(i) |
| Mandatory health check under Factories Act (Section 41-B, 41-C) | **Available** | Obligatory under law |
| Group Mediclaim insurance premium (voluntary) | **Blocked** | 17(5)(b)(iii) unless mandatory |
| OPD reimbursements for personal illness | **Blocked** | Personal consumption |
| Ambulance service for employee injured in factory accident | **Needs analysis** | May be business-related if taxable supply made |

**Mandatory vs Voluntary — The Governing Principle:**
The CBIC position, reinforced by Circular No. 172/04/2022-GST dated 6 July 2022, is that where an employer is **obligated by law** to provide a facility, the ITC on that facility is available. This is because the expenditure is not voluntary personal consumption — it is a statutory cost of running the business. Therefore:

- **Mandatory** under a specific statute = ITC potentially available (subject to the supply also being taxable)
- **Voluntary** by employer policy = ITC blocked as personal consumption

### 1.3 Leave Travel Allowance / Leave Travel Concession (LTA/LTC)

**Statutory Provision:** Section 17(5)(g) — ITC is blocked on goods or services used for personal consumption.

**Nature of LTA:** LTA is a component of employee CTC that reimburses an employee for personal vacation travel. Although it is part of employment, the underlying consumption is personal — the employee is travelling for leisure, not for the business.

**ITC Position:**
- Air tickets purchased for employee's family vacation (LTA reimbursement): **ITC blocked**
- Rail tickets for LTC: **ITC blocked**
- Hotel accommodation during personal vacation: **ITC blocked**

**Contrast with business travel:**
- Air tickets for employee travelling to attend a client meeting: **ITC NOT blocked** (directly attributable to business; not personal consumption under 17(5)(g))
- Hotel accommodation during business trip: **ITC NOT blocked**

**Documentation to distinguish:**
- Travel request / approval forms
- Purpose of travel stated in travel expense claim
- Whether travel is to a business destination or a personal destination
- Meeting schedules, client visit reports corroborating business travel

### 1.4 Employee Welfare — The Boundary

The distinction between **employee welfare** (ITC eligible if obligatory/directly business-related) and **personal consumption** (ITC blocked) is one of the most litigated areas under Section 17(5).

| Facility | ITC Status | Reasoning |
|----------|------------|-----------|
| On-site crèche (mandatory under Maternity Benefit Act for ≥50 employees) | **Available** | Obligatory under law |
| Club membership for senior executive | **Blocked** | Personal benefit; see Group 4 |
| Residential accommodation for expat employee (business requirement) | **Needs analysis** | If contractual obligation for business deployment, may be eligible |
| Annual medical check-up (mandatory under Factories Act) | **Available** | Statutory obligation |
| Annual medical check-up (voluntary employer initiative) | **Blocked** | Personal consumption |
| Employee birthday party catering | **Blocked** | Personal consumption; entertainment |
| Safety gear (helmets, gloves, safety shoes) | **NOT blocked** | Not in 17(5); directly for taxable supply |

## Group 2: Vehicle-Related Supplies

{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(a), 17(5)(aa), 17(5)(b)(i) partially, CGST Act, 2017"}
{nature_of_block: "Conditional — exceptions exist for specified business uses"}
{gst_form_reporting: "GSTR-3B Table 4D(2); related services linked to the blocked vehicle"}
{key_document: "Registration Certificate (RC book) for seating capacity verification"}

### 2.1 Motor Vehicles (≤13 Passenger Capacity) — The Core Block

**Statutory Provision:** Section 17(5)(a) — ITC is blocked on motor vehicles for transportation of persons having approved seating capacity of **not more than thirteen persons** (including the driver).

**What is blocked:**
1. **Purchase or acquisition** of such motor vehicles: CGST + SGST or IGST paid on purchase price
2. **Hiring, lease, or rental:** GST paid on hire charges or lease rentals
3. **Insurance:** GST on insurance premium for such vehicles
4. **Repairs and maintenance:** GST on servicing, repair parts, tyres, and related services
5. **Accessories and modifications:** GST on accessories fitted to such vehicles

**The Seating Capacity Test — As Per RC Book:**
- The determination of seating capacity is based on the **Registration Certificate (RC book)** issued by the Regional Transport Office (RTO)
- Actual use is irrelevant — a 13-seat vehicle used by only 2 persons is still a ≤13-seat vehicle for this test
- A modified vehicle (seats removed to increase cargo space) is still classified by its RC book capacity

**Critical: What is a "Motor Vehicle for Transportation of Persons"?**
- Sedans, SUVs, hatchbacks: clearly covered
- Pickup trucks registered as goods carriages: **NOT covered** by 17(5)(a) — these are goods transport vehicles, not person transport vehicles
- Ambulances: covered by 17(5)(a) in terms of capacity, but the exception for passenger transport may apply

**Full coverage of GST paid:**
The block applies to CGST, SGST, and IGST paid on the motor vehicle or its related services. There is no distinction between state and central tax for purposes of the block.

### 2.2 When Vehicle ITC IS Available — Exceptions Under Section 17(5)(a)

Three categories of businesses can claim ITC on motor vehicles (≤13 seats) that would otherwise be blocked:

**Exception 1: Dealer / Trader in Motor Vehicles**
- A person in the business of **further supply of such motor vehicles** can claim ITC
- Applicability: automobile dealers (showrooms), used car dealers, vehicle auctioneers
- Rationale: the vehicles are stock-in-trade for the dealer; they are not used for personal transportation but for commercial resale
- Condition: the vehicle must be part of stock held for sale, not used for the dealer's own business transportation
- If a dealer also uses one of the vehicles for office commuting: that specific vehicle's ITC is blocked; only stock vehicles qualify

**Exception 2: Passenger Transport Business**
- Persons engaged in the business of **transportation of passengers**: ITC on their fleet is available
- Examples: taxi operators (app-based and traditional), tour operators with passenger vehicles, bus services registered under Motor Vehicles Act for passenger transport, employee shuttle bus services operated as a service
- Condition: the vehicle must be used for the passenger transport service that constitutes the taxable outward supply
- An IT company running a shuttle for its own employees (without separately charging for transport) may NOT qualify as being in the "business of transportation of passengers" — the IT services are the business; employee transport is ancillary

**Exception 3: Driving Schools / Training Institutes**
- Persons engaged in **imparting driving training** can claim ITC on vehicles used for training
- The vehicle must be actually used for training purposes, not for other business use

### 2.3 Services Related to Blocked Vehicles

A critical point: **if the motor vehicle itself is blocked, ALL services RELATED to that vehicle are also blocked.** Section 17(5)(a)(B) and (C) expressly extend the block to:
- General insurance for the vehicle
- Servicing, repair, and maintenance

This means:
- ₹50,000 car service bill for a company sedan: **ITC blocked entirely**
- ₹30,000 insurance premium for the company car: **ITC blocked**
- If the car is used by a taxi operator (exception applies): **ITC available** on insurance and repairs too

### 2.4 Vessels and Aircraft

**Statutory Provision:** Section 17(5)(aa) — ITC is blocked on vessels and aircraft.

**Scope:**
- Corporate yacht used for client entertainment: **ITC blocked** (purchase + running costs + crew)
- Private jet used for executive travel: **ITC blocked**
- Corporate helicopter used for site inspection: **Needs analysis** — if the site inspection is for business purposes and the helicopter is a business tool, there is an argument; however, the statutory bar under 17(5)(aa) is broad

**Exceptions under Section 17(5)(aa):**
- Vessels used for **transportation of goods**: ITC available (goods transport is specifically excepted)
- Aircraft used for **transportation of goods**: ITC available
- **Transportation of passengers**: ITC available for commercial passenger carriers
- **Imparting training on navigation of vessels / flying of aircraft**: ITC available for maritime academies, flying schools

**Practical Implication:**
- Shipping company operating cargo vessels: **ITC available** on vessel acquisition and maintenance
- Airline operating commercial flights: **ITC available** on aircraft (subject to other conditions)
- Corporate charter aircraft used exclusively for executive travel: **ITC blocked**

### 2.5 Practical Examples — Vehicle ITC Determination

| Situation | Seating Capacity | ITC Status | Reason |
|-----------|-----------------|------------|--------|
| Company car for CEO (sedan, 5 seats) | 5 | **Blocked** | ≤13 seats; no exception |
| Delivery van (RC: goods carriage, 2 seats) | 2 | **NOT blocked** | Goods carriage, not person transport |
| Company bus (20 seats) for employee transport | 20 | **NOT blocked** | >13 seats; outside 17(5)(a) |
| Taxi operator's fleet car (5 seats) | 5 | **Available** | Passenger transport business exception |
| Car dealer's showroom stock (10 cars) | Various | **Available** | Further supply exception |
| Ambulance (10 seats) | 10 | **Needs analysis** | ≤13 seats; but if patient transport is taxable, exception may apply |
| Insurance on CEO's company car | N/A (related service) | **Blocked** | Related to blocked vehicle |
| Tyre replacement for delivery van (goods carriage) | N/A | **Available** | Base vehicle not blocked |
| Driving school's training car (5 seats) | 5 | **Available** | Driving training exception |
| Corporate yacht (10-berth) | N/A | **Blocked** | Section 17(5)(aa) |

## Group 3: Food, Beverages, and Personal Care

{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(b)(i) CGST Act, 2017"}
{nature_of_block: "Conditional — same category outward supply exception; mandatory canteen exception"}
{cbic_circular: "Circular No. 172/04/2022-GST dated 6 July 2022"}
{gst_form_reporting: "GSTR-3B Table 4D(2)"}

### 3.1 General Rule — Food and Beverages

**Statutory Provision:** Section 17(5)(b)(i) — ITC is blocked on food and beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services, cosmetic and plastic surgery.

**Blocked in virtually all business contexts:**
- **Office pantry:** Tea, coffee, cold drinks, biscuits, snacks purchased for office use: **ITC blocked**
- **Client entertainment at restaurant:** Business lunch or dinner for clients: **ITC blocked** (even though it has a business purpose, the nature of supply is food/beverage which falls within 17(5)(b)(i))
- **Outdoor catering for office parties:** New Year party, Diwali celebration, team outing: **ITC blocked**
- **Corporate gift hampers with food items:** Food-based gifts: **ITC blocked** under both 17(5)(b)(i) and 17(5)(h)
- **Employee meals during overtime:** Meals arranged for employees working late: **ITC blocked**
- **Working lunch during training programmes:** Meals provided during in-house training: **ITC blocked** (the food is consumed personally; the training itself may be eligible)

### 3.2 The Same Category Exception

**When food ITC IS available:**
Section 17(5)(b)(i) contains a proviso: ITC on food/beverages is available when **the category of inward and outward supply is the same** — i.e., the business MAKES food/beverage supplies as its taxable outward supply.

| Business Type | Inward Supply | Outward Supply | ITC Status |
|---------------|--------------|----------------|------------|
| Restaurant | Food ingredients | Taxable food supply | **Available** |
| Hotel with restaurant | Food for restaurant | Food & beverage service | **Available** for restaurant portion |
| Caterer | Ingredients + labour | Outdoor catering service | **Available** |
| Food manufacturer | Raw materials (spices, grains) | Taxable food products | **Available** |
| IT company buying food for pantry | Pantry supplies | IT services | **Blocked** — different category |
| Manufacturing company buying food for canteen | Canteen supplies | Manufactured goods | **Blocked** — different category (unless mandatory canteen, see below) |

**Important limitation:** The "same category" exception applies to the **core business** outward supply, not to incidental food purchases. A hotel that provides food to its own staff (not to guests) cannot claim ITC on that staff food under the same-category exception — because the outward supply is to guests, not to staff.

### 3.3 Mandatory Canteen Under the Factories Act — The CBIC Position

**Legal basis:** Section 46 of the Factories Act, 1948 requires factories employing **250 or more workers** to provide a canteen.

**CBIC Circular No. 172/04/2022-GST dated 6 July 2022 (Key ruling):**
The Circular clarifies that:
- Where an employer is **legally obligated** to provide a facility to its employees, the ITC on that facility is NOT blocked under Section 17(5)(b)(i) — because the expenditure is a statutory compliance cost, not voluntary personal consumption
- This applies to the **mandatory canteen** under the Factories Act: factories with ≥250 workers that are required to maintain a canteen **CAN claim ITC** on canteen services and food supplies

**Conditions for claiming ITC on mandatory canteen:**
1. Worker count must be ≥250 (as per factory records / muster rolls)
2. The canteen must be maintained by the factory (not outsourced to an exempt-supply caterer)
3. Document the statutory obligation: cite Factories Act Section 46 in the compliance file
4. If the canteen is contracted out: ITC on contractor's invoice is available (since the factory's obligation is to provide, and outsourcing is one method)
5. Where employees are charged for meals: the amount collected from employees may constitute a taxable supply (and output tax must be paid on those collections)

**Below 250 workers:**
- Voluntary canteen maintained by employer: **ITC blocked** — no statutory obligation; purely welfare
- If a factory voluntarily maintains a canteen with <250 workers: **ITC blocked**

**[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]:** Maharashtra factories employing ≥250 workers must comply with the Factories Act, 1948 (a central statute applicable nationwide). Maharashtra Labour Welfare Fund Act may impose additional welfare obligations. If a specific Maharashtra state notification or circular mandates a facility, that facility's GST may also qualify for the same exception. Maharashtra SGST officers have, in scrutiny proceedings, required factories to produce worker count evidence and floor-level compliance records when claiming canteen ITC.

**Documentation checklist for mandatory canteen ITC:**
- Muster roll / attendance records showing worker count ≥250
- Factory licence / inspection report confirming canteen obligation
- Canteen operator's GST-registered invoices (if outsourced)
- Amount charged to employees (for output tax computation)
- Written legal opinion or advance ruling if amounts are material

### 3.4 Outdoor Catering

**Always blocked for most businesses:**
- Office event catering: **blocked**
- Catering at annual general meetings: **blocked**
- Catering at product launch events: **blocked** (even though business-related, the catering itself is blocked)

**Exception — caterers themselves:**
A catering business that hires sub-caterers or purchases food supplies for outdoor catering services: **ITC available** (same category — outward supply is outdoor catering; inward supply is food/sub-catering for the same service).

## Group 4: Club Memberships and Fitness Centres

{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(b)(ii) CGST Act, 2017"}
{nature_of_block: "Absolute — no exceptions"}
{gst_form_reporting: "GSTR-3B Table 4D(2)"}

### 4.1 The Absolute Block — No Exceptions Exist

**Statutory Provision:** Section 17(5)(b)(ii) — ITC is blocked on membership of a **club, health and fitness centre**.

**Critical point:** Unlike Section 17(5)(a) (vehicles) or 17(5)(b)(i) (food — same category exception), Section 17(5)(b)(ii) has **no exceptions whatsoever.** There is no "same category" proviso, no "further supply" exception, and no "mandatory under law" carve-out applicable to club/fitness memberships.

**What is absolutely blocked:**
- **Golf club memberships:** Annual subscription, entrance fee, green fees when part of a membership package
- **Social clubs:** Rotary Club, Lions Club, business networking clubs — membership fees with GST
- **Business clubs:** Chambers of commerce with facilities (where GST is charged on membership)
- **Gymkhana and sports clubs:** Mumbai Gymkhana, Cricket Club of India membership
- **Gym and health fitness centres:** Individual or corporate gym memberships
- **Yoga centres, pilates studios, fitness chains:** Corporate membership packages
- **Swimming clubs:** Pool membership for employees
- **Sports facility memberships:** Tennis club, squash court memberships

### 4.2 The "Business Networking" Argument — Rejected

A common argument by taxpayers is: *"We took the club membership for business networking purposes — our sales team meets clients at the club; it is for business."*

**This argument does not succeed under Section 17(5)(b)(ii).**

The statute does not ask about purpose. It asks about the nature of the supply. If the inward supply is a "membership of a club," Section 17(5)(b)(ii) blocks it — regardless of the business purpose for which the membership is used.

**AAR and court positions:** Multiple AAR rulings have upheld the absolute block on club memberships even when the taxpayer demonstrated business use. The statutory language admits no purpose-based exceptions.

### 4.3 Corporate Club Membership — Revenue Impact

Many companies in India include club membership as part of the cost-to-company (CTC) for senior executives. The tax impact is:

- **Input GST on club membership:** Blocked — company cannot recover this GST
- **Perquisite value for income tax:** Taxable as perquisite in employee's hands under Income Tax Act
- **Effective cost:** The GST blocked (typically 18% on membership fee) becomes an additional cost to the company

**Example:**
- Golf club entrance fee: ₹5,00,000 + 18% GST = ₹5,90,000
- Annual subscription: ₹1,50,000 + 18% GST = ₹1,77,000
- Total ITC blocked: ₹90,000 + ₹27,000 = ₹1,17,000 per executive per year
- For a company with 10 senior executives with golf memberships: ₹11,70,000 ITC lost per year

### 4.4 Fitness Centre Memberships — Scope

**Blocked regardless of:**
- Whether the fitness centre is on-premises (company's own gym) or external
- Whether the membership is individual or corporate
- Whether health improvement leads to productivity improvement (a non-statutory consideration)

**Note on on-premises gym:** If a company sets up its own gym within office premises:
- **Equipment purchase (treadmill, weights):** ITC on equipment — **blocked** under Section 17(5)(b)(ii) (fitness centre equipment)
- Some practitioners argue that equipment for the company's own facility is not a "membership of a fitness centre" — this is a litigation risk area; conservative approach is to block
- CBIC has not specifically clarified on internally operated fitness facilities

## Group 5: Entertainment and Employee Benefits

{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(b)(iii), 17(5)(b)(iv) CGST Act, 2017"}
{nature_of_block: "Conditional — exceptions for government notified mandatory benefits"}
{gst_form_reporting: "GSTR-3B Table 4D(2)"}

### 5.1 Rent-a-Cab

**Statutory Provision:** Section 17(5)(b)(iii) — ITC is blocked on **rent-a-cab**.

**Scope of the block:**
- Hiring a cab or vehicle from a cab operator to ferry employees to and from workplace: **ITC blocked**
- Company car hire for employee daily use (not for goods transport): **ITC blocked**
- App-based cab services (Ola, Uber) for employee official travel: **Needs analysis** — if for personal travel of employee, blocked; if for official client visit, arguably NOT personal consumption and not "rent-a-cab" as a benefit

**The Exception — Government Notified Mandatory Benefits:**
Section 17(5)(b) proviso: ITC is available on rent-a-cab if:
1. The government has notified this as a mandatory employee benefit by a competent authority; OR
2. The employer is providing the service as the same category of outward supply (i.e., the employer is in the cab/transport business)

**Notification No. 14/2018-CT (Rate):** This notification specified conditions under which transport of employees is a taxable supply. However, the cross-reference between the notification and ITC eligibility requires careful analysis. The general position is:

- If the employer is **legally obligated** by a government notification or statute to provide transportation: ITC is available
- If the employer voluntarily provides cab service to employees as a perk: ITC is **blocked**

**[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]:** The Maharashtra Shops and Establishments Act and certain state government orders may mandate cab services for night-shift employees in specific industries (IT/ITES). If the employer is obligated under such state regulation, the mandatory benefit exception may apply for Maharashtra. Documentary evidence of the regulatory obligation is essential.

### 5.2 Life Insurance — Group Term Life Policies

**Statutory Provision:** Section 17(5)(b)(iii) — ITC blocked on life insurance.

**Scope:**
- Group term life insurance premium paid by employer for employees: **ITC blocked** unless mandatory under law
- Keyman insurance policy: **ITC blocked** (personal benefit to the individual, even if the company is beneficiary)
- Credit life insurance on company borrowings: **needs analysis** — if the insurance is on the life of a key person, may be blocked; if it is a purely financial/commercial product, different analysis

**Exception:**
- Life insurance mandated by a government notification or statute: ITC available
- Example: If a specific government circular mandates group life insurance for workers in hazardous industries, and the employer complies, the ITC on that insurance would be eligible

**Practical reality:** Most group term life insurance programmes in India are voluntary employer initiatives. **ITC is blocked** for the vast majority of corporate life insurance premiums.

### 5.3 Health Insurance — Group Mediclaim Policies

**Statutory Provision:** Section 17(5)(b)(iii) — ITC blocked on health insurance.

**Scope:**
- Group mediclaim / health insurance premium for employees and their families: **ITC blocked** unless mandatory under law
- Top-up health insurance covers for employees: **ITC blocked**
- COVID hospitalisation insurance bought voluntarily: **ITC blocked**

**Exception — Mandatory Health Insurance:**
Where a statute or government notification mandates health insurance:
- Employees' State Insurance (ESI) contributions: Note — ESI is not GST-exempt; ESI contributions are a statutory deduction from wages. There is no GST on ESI contributions, so this is not a GST-ITC issue
- If a specific sectoral regulation mandates private health insurance (e.g., for employees in certain hazardous zones), and the employer purchases commercial health insurance to comply: ITC on those premiums may be eligible

**Companies providing health insurance as outward supply:**
- An insurance company paying insurance claim processing costs is in the insurance business. Their analysis is different: they may be able to claim ITC on health insurance premiums they pay as re-insurance in the course of their taxable supply of insurance services.

### 5.4 Leave Travel Allowance — Benefits-in-Kind

**Statutory Provision:** Section 17(5)(b)(iv) — ITC is blocked on travel benefits extended to employees for vacation such as leave or home travel concession.

**Scope:**
- Air/rail tickets reimbursed to employees for personal vacation (LTA): **ITC blocked**
- Hotel accommodation reimbursed for holiday travel: **ITC blocked**
- Travel agent charges for booking employee vacation travel: **ITC blocked**

**Distinction:**
- **LTA (vacation):** Blocked under 17(5)(b)(iv)
- **Official travel (business):** NOT blocked — directly attributable to business; the employee is travelling to make taxable supplies happen

**Documentation requirement:** Maintain travel request forms distinguishing official travel from LTA clearly. LTA reimbursements should flow through a separate LTA ledger to facilitate ITC segregation.

## Group 6: Construction and Works Contract

{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(c), 17(5)(d) CGST Act, 2017 as amended by Finance Act 2021"}
{nature_of_block: "Conditional — plant and machinery exception; further supply exception"}
{finance_act_2021: "Retrospective amendment effective from 1 July 2017"}
{gst_form_reporting: "GSTR-3B Table 4D(2)"}

### 6.1 Works Contract Services for Own Immovable Property

**Statutory Provision:** Section 17(5)(c) — ITC is blocked on **works contract services when supplied for construction of an immovable property (other than plant and machinery)**, except where the registered person is in the business of further supply of works contract services.

**"Works Contract" Defined:** Section 2(119) CGST Act — a contract for building, construction, fabrication, completion, erection, installation, fitting out, improvement, modification, repair, maintenance, renovation, alteration, or commissioning of any immovable property where both goods and services are involved.

**What is blocked:**
- Civil construction service for building a factory: **ITC blocked**
- Interior design and furnishing works (capitalised): **ITC blocked**
- Renovation and reconstruction of office premises (if capitalised): **ITC blocked**
- Erection of a boundary wall: **ITC blocked**
- Laying of drainage and sewerage for own premises: **ITC blocked**

**The "Further Supply" Exception:**
A construction company that provides works contract services as its outward supply:
- Main contractor receiving sub-contractor's works contract service: **ITC available** (the main contractor's outward supply is also works contract — "further supply" of the same)
- A builder building flats for sale to homebuyers: receiving construction sub-contract: **ITC available** (selling flats under construction is a taxable supply; the construction cost ITC chain is intact)

### 6.2 Goods and Services for Own-Account Construction

**Statutory Provision:** Section 17(5)(d) — ITC is blocked on **goods or services or both received by a taxable person for construction of an immovable property on his own account**, even if the goods/services are used in course or furtherance of business.

**What is blocked:**
- Cement, steel, bricks, sand purchased for building own office: **ITC blocked**
- Labour charges paid for own building construction: **ITC blocked**
- Architect fees for designing own building: **ITC blocked**
- Consultancy for own construction project: **ITC blocked**
- Electrical fittings permanently attached to own building (fixtures): **ITC blocked**

**The Plant and Machinery Exception:**
Section 17(5)(d) proviso: ITC IS available for goods/services used for construction of **plant and machinery**.

"Plant and Machinery" is defined in the Explanation to Section 17 of the CGST Act:
> "Plant and machinery" means apparatus, equipment and machinery fixed to earth by foundation or structural support that are used for making outward supply of goods or services or both and includes such foundation and structural support but excludes land, building or any other civil structures; telecommunication towers; and pipelines laid outside the factory premises.

**Practical application of the plant and machinery exception:**

| Item | Classification | ITC Status |
|------|---------------|------------|
| Effluent treatment plant (ETP) | Plant and machinery | **Available** |
| Manufacturing equipment bolted to factory floor | Plant and machinery | **Available** |
| Factory building that houses the equipment | Civil structure / building | **Blocked** |
| Foundation for machinery | Part of plant and machinery | **Available** |
| Telecom tower erected on own premises | Excluded from P&M definition | **Blocked** |
| Pipeline inside factory premises | Plant and machinery | **Available** |
| Pipeline outside factory premises | Excluded from P&M definition | **Blocked** |
| HVAC system for manufacturing process | Plant and machinery (if process-critical) | **Needs analysis** |
| HVAC system for office comfort | Civil building amenity | **Blocked** |

### 6.3 The Capitalisation Test — Finance Act 2021 (Critical)

**The Most Significant Amendment to Section 17(5):**

The Finance Act 2021 inserted the phrase "including where such construction is used in course or furtherance of business" in Section 17(5)(d). This was given **retrospective effect from 1 July 2017**.

**Before Finance Act 2021:** There was a school of thought that if the construction was used for business purposes (e.g., a factory), the construction cost ITC should be eligible because it was "in course or furtherance of business."

**After Finance Act 2021:** This argument is statutorily eliminated. Even if the construction is for business use (factory, warehouse), ITC on construction goods/services is blocked if it is "own account" construction.

**The Capitalisation Test — Repairs and Renovation:**

This is the key practical distinction for repairs/renovation:

| Nature of Expenditure | Accounting Treatment | ITC Status |
|-----------------------|---------------------|------------|
| Major renovation (new false ceiling, new flooring) | **Capitalised** as Building improvement | **Blocked** under 17(5)(c)/(d) |
| Routine repair (painting, minor plumbing) | **Expensed** to P&L (repairs & maintenance) | **NOT blocked** under 17(5) |
| Air-conditioning unit (portable, not fixed) | **Capitalised** as equipment | **Available** — it is movable equipment, not immovable property construction |
| Air-conditioning ducting permanently embedded in building | **Capitalised** as building component | **Blocked** |

**Governing Principle:** The block under 17(5)(c)/(d) applies to construction of **immovable property**. If a repair or renovation results in the addition being **capitalised as part of the immovable structure**, it is blocked. If it is **expensed as a revenue item** (because it merely restores the asset to its original condition), it is not blocked under 17(5) — though it could still be blocked if it is for a blocked vehicle under Group 2.

**Warning — Retrospective Interest Liability:**
Companies that availed ITC on renovation/refurbishment costs between July 2017 and the Finance Act 2021 amendment, where those costs were capitalised, are technically required to reverse that ITC with 18% per annum interest. This is a significant contingent liability for companies with large office renovation history.

**[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]:** Maharashtra SGST officers have specifically flagged the capitalisation test during GST scrutiny of manufacturing companies. Companies in the MIDC (Maharashtra Industrial Development Corporation) estates that undertook factory expansions between 2017 and 2021 may face queries. The Maharashtra State Tax Department has included this issue in its audit guidelines. Taxpayers should review their fixed asset register for any construction ITC availed during this period.

## Group 7: Regime-Based Blocks

{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(e), 17(5)(f), 17(5)(i) CGST Act, 2017"}
{nature_of_block: "Absolute — no exceptions"}
{gst_form_reporting: "Not applicable — these taxpayers have different filing regimes"}

### 7.1 Composition Scheme Dealers

**Statutory Provision:** Section 17(5)(e) — ITC is blocked for persons paying tax under the Composition Scheme (Section 10 CGST Act).

**Scope:**
- A manufacturer, trader, or restaurant operator under the Composition Scheme: **cannot claim any ITC** on any inward supply
- The Composition Scheme is a simplified, concessional tax regime where tax is paid at a lower rate (0.5% to 6% of turnover) in lieu of regular GST compliance
- The trade-off is that no ITC is available

**Why the block exists:** The Composition Scheme is designed for small businesses where the compliance burden of tracking ITC and filing returns is considered too heavy. The lower tax rate compensates for the inability to claim ITC.

**Consequences:**
- All GST paid on purchases becomes a cost (part of expense or inventory cost)
- No Electronic Credit Ledger for Composition dealers
- GST paid on capital goods (machinery, vehicles) is also a cost — cannot be claimed

**Transition:** When a Composition dealer opts out and registers as a regular taxpayer, ITC on closing stock of inputs and capital goods is available as per Rule 40 of CGST Rules (with proportionate reduction for the period capital goods were used under Composition).

### 7.2 Non-Resident Taxable Persons (NRTP)

**Statutory Provision:** Section 17(5)(f) — ITC for Non-Resident Taxable Persons is restricted to GST paid on **imported goods** only.

**Who is an NRTP:** A person who occasionally undertakes taxable supplies in India but does not have a fixed place of business in India (Section 2(77) CGST Act). Examples: foreign performers, foreign exhibitors at Indian trade shows.

**ITC position:**
- ITC on imported goods brought for the taxable event in India: **Available**
- ITC on goods/services procured within India: **Blocked**

**Registration requirement:** NRTPs must register before making taxable supplies in India and deposit an advance tax estimate.

### 7.3 Tax Paid Under Enforcement Provisions

**Statutory Provision:** Section 17(5)(i) — ITC is blocked on tax paid under Sections 74 (fraud/evasion), 129 (detention/seizure), and 130 (confiscation).

**What this means:**
- Section 74: If GST is demanded and paid after detection of willful suppression/fraud: **ITC blocked**
- Section 129: GST paid to release detained goods in transit: **No ITC** on that detained consignment's tax
- Section 130: Tax arising from confiscation proceedings: **No ITC**

**Rationale:** Tax paid under enforcement is a penalty-linked payment. Allowing ITC on such tax would substantially reduce the deterrent effect of enforcement.

**Compliance implication:** Companies that pay Section 74 demands (even under protest) and issue self-invoices — do NOT claim ITC on those payments. The block is absolute.

## Group 8: Lost, Stolen, Destroyed Goods, Gifts, and Samples

{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(h) CGST Act, 2017"}
{nature_of_block: "Absolute at time of event — reversal of previously availed ITC required"}
{reversal_mechanism: "GSTR-3B Table 4B(2)"}

### 8.1 Goods Lost, Stolen, Destroyed, Written Off, or Disposed Without Consideration

**Statutory Provision:** Section 17(5)(h) — ITC is blocked on goods **lost, stolen, destroyed, written off, or disposed of by way of gift or free samples**.

**Important timing note:** At the time of purchasing these goods, ITC may have been correctly availed (assuming the goods were intended for taxable supply). The block under 17(5)(h) operates at the time of the loss/destruction event — requiring **reversal** of the ITC already claimed.

**Scenarios and ITC implications:**

**Warehouse fire:**
- Goods worth ₹10,00,000 destroyed in fire; CGST+SGST paid at time of purchase = ₹1,80,000
- ITC of ₹1,80,000 was availed at time of purchase
- On write-off of these goods: **reverse ₹1,80,000** via GSTR-3B Table 4B(2)
- Insurance proceeds received (if any): do NOT reduce the reversal obligation; the ITC reversal is on the destroyed goods regardless of insurance recovery

**Flood damage:**
- Goods in transit damaged by flood: ITC reversal required on the specifically identifiable goods
- Identify invoices; compute CGST/SGST/IGST paid; reverse that amount

**How to compute the reversal amount:**
- Step 1: Identify the specific invoices for the goods that were destroyed/lost
- Step 2: Extract the CGST, SGST, and IGST paid on those invoices
- Step 3: If the goods were inputs used in work-in-progress (WIP): compute the input portion consumed and reverse proportionately
- Step 4: Report in GSTR-3B Table 4B(2) for the month of write-off
- Step 5: Retain evidence: FIR (for theft), fire report/insurance claim (for fire), stock count records (for write-off)

**Theft:**
- Goods stolen from warehouse or transit: **ITC reversal required**
- File FIR as supporting documentation for the loss
- Reversal computed on the basis of purchase invoices

**Write-off (Obsolete/Expired Goods):**
- Old inventory written off as obsolete: **ITC reversal required**
- Expired pharmaceutical products disposed: **ITC reversal required**
- Goods destroyed for quality control (sub-standard): **ITC reversal required**

### 8.2 Gifts and Free Samples

**The Gift Block:**
When goods are disposed of **by way of gift** — without consideration — the ITC on those goods must be reversed. This applies at the time of gifting.

**Corporate gifts:**
- Diwali gift hampers to customers and distributors: **ITC reversal required** at time of distribution
- Festival gifts to employees: **ITC reversal required**
- New year gifts to vendors: **ITC reversal required**
- Note: monetary gifts (cash) have no GST implications; ITC reversal applies to goods with GST content

**Pharmaceutical free samples to doctors:**
- Pharma companies distributing drug samples to medical professionals: **ITC reversal required**
- This was confirmed by multiple AARs and by the practice circulars of pharmaceutical industry associations post-GST
- The quantum is significant: pharma companies distribute large volumes of samples annually

**Promotional material — embedded in taxable supply:**
If a promotional item is **bundled as a composite supply** with a taxable product (i.e., it is part of the sale price and does not constitute a separate free disposition), the ITC analysis changes:
- "Buy one get one free" (BOGO): The free item is not a "gift" in the GST sense — it is part of a composite transaction with consideration (the price paid for the first item)
- Promotional packs where the extra item is included in MRP: ITC on the extra item may be available if it is part of the taxable supply
- However: if the free item is over and above a concluded sale (an additional gift to the buyer), it is a gift and ITC should be reversed

**Free samples with no consideration at all:**
- Sample packets sent to potential new customers: **ITC reversal required** — no consideration is received
- Testing samples to existing customers for QC purposes: **ITC reversal required**

### 8.3 Documentary Requirements for Group 8 Reversals

| Event | Documents Required |
|-------|-------------------|
| Fire/flood destruction | Insurance claim, fire department report, surveyor report, stock count pre-loss |
| Theft | FIR with police, internal incident report, security logs |
| Write-off (obsolescence) | Stock audit report, expiry documentation, write-off approval (board resolution for large amounts) |
| Gifts | Gift register (recipient name, date, item, GST value), corporate gift policy documentation |
| Pharma samples | Sample dispatch register, MR (medical representative) distribution records |
