## Section 17(5) — Complete Annotated Text

### Opening Non-Obstante Clause
{law_reference: "Section 17(5) opening — CGST Act, 2017"}
{topic: "Non-Obstante Clause — Section 17(5)"}

**Statutory Text:** "Notwithstanding anything contained in sub-section (1) of section 16 and sub-section (1) of section 18, input tax credit shall not be available in respect of the following, namely:—"

**Annotation:** The words "notwithstanding anything contained in sub-section (1) of section 16 and sub-section (1) of section 18" constitute a non-obstante clause of the highest legal significance. Section 16(1) is the primary ITC entitlement provision — it gives every registered person the right to take credit of input tax charged on supply of goods or services used in the course or furtherance of business. Section 18(1) grants ITC upon registration or on going from exempt to taxable supply status. The non-obstante clause means that even where these primary entitlement provisions would otherwise grant ITC, Section 17(5) overrides them categorically for the listed supplies. No amount of business-use argumentation, commercial purpose, or integration with taxable output will open up the credit if the supply falls squarely within a Section 17(5) category (absent a statutory exception).

This non-obstante formulation was upheld by the Bombay High Court in *Zee Entertainment Enterprises Ltd. v. Union of India* (2022) where the court affirmed that Section 17(5) operates as a complete override and taxpayers cannot use Section 16(1) as a counter-argument once the Section 17(5) block applies. The court further held that the non-obstante clause extends to the entire Section 17(5) and is not limited to sub-clause (a) or any particular clause.

### Section 17(5)(a) — Motor Vehicles for Transportation of Persons
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(a) CGST Act, 2017 as amended by Finance Act 2018 and CGST (Amendment) Act 2018"}
{topic: "Section 17(5)(a) — Motor Vehicles"}

**Statutory Text:**
"(a) motor vehicles for transportation of persons having approved seating capacity of not more than thirteen persons (including the driver), except when they are used for making the following taxable supplies, namely:—
(A) further supply of such motor vehicles; or
(B) transportation of passengers; or
(C) imparting training on driving such motor vehicles;"

**Companion Provision — General Insurance, Servicing and Maintenance:**
"Provided also that the input tax credit in respect of the general insurance services, services of repair and maintenance in so far as they relate to a motor vehicle, vessel or aircraft referred to in clause (a) or clause (aa), shall also not be available, except when—
(a) the motor vehicle, vessel or aircraft referred to in clause (a) or clause (aa) is used for the purposes specified therein;"

**Annotation:**

**Scope:** Clause (a) blocks ITC on motor vehicles that are designed and approved (under the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 and the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989) for carrying persons and have an "approved seating capacity" of ≤13 persons including the driver. This covers: cars (sedans, hatchbacks), SUVs with up to 7 seats, MUVs, vans with up to 13 seats, and jeeps. It does not cover:
- Motor vehicles designed exclusively for goods transportation (trucks, tempos, lorries) — these are not "for transportation of persons"
- Buses and coaches with approved seating capacity of 14 or more persons — these exceed the 13-person threshold
- Two-wheelers and three-wheelers (the provision is specifically for "motor vehicles" as defined under Motor Vehicles Act — though two-wheelers and three-wheelers are motor vehicles, they are not "for transportation of persons having approved seating capacity" in the same sense; the practical view from advance rulings is that two-wheelers are not covered by 17(5)(a), though some authorities disagree)

**The "Approved Seating Capacity" Test:** The relevant figure is the seating capacity as approved in the Registration Certificate (RC book) issued by the Regional Transport Office (RTO). The actual number of seats occupied in practice, or the number of seats physically present, is not the test. If the RC book shows 7 seats and the company installs only 5 seats for executive comfort, the vehicle still has an approved seating capacity of 7 — the ITC block applies. If the RC book shows 14 seats, ITC is not blocked under 17(5)(a) even if only 10 passengers routinely use the vehicle.

**Exception (A) — Further Supply (Automobile Dealers):** Automobile dealers and distributors who purchase vehicles as stock-in-trade for onward sale: full ITC available. The vehicle is not being "used" by the dealer; it is inventory. The output tax on the eventual sale absorbs the input tax — this is the core ITC principle at work.

**Exception (B) — Transportation of Passengers:** Taxi operators, cab aggregators, radio taxi services, passenger bus operators (with ≤13-seater fleet), school bus operators (if charging fees constituting a taxable supply of transportation service): ITC available on their fleet. However, this exception requires that the transportation of passengers is itself a taxable supply — the business must be charging GST on the transportation service. A charitable organisation providing free transport has no taxable output and cannot claim this exception.

**Exception (C) — Driving Training Schools:** Motor driving schools imparting training on operating motor vehicles: ITC available on training vehicles. The training itself is a taxable service (GST at 18%), and the vehicle is the core input for generating that taxable output.

**Companion Block — Insurance, Servicing, Maintenance:** Where the principal vehicle ITC is blocked (i.e., none of the exceptions apply), ITC is also blocked on:
- Motor vehicle insurance (both general insurance and comprehensive)
- Vehicle servicing, periodic maintenance, Annual Maintenance Contracts (AMC)
- Repair and replacement of parts (tyres, batteries, clutch plates)
- Car washing and detailing services

This companion block prevents a back-door ITC claim where the vehicle purchase ITC is blocked but the taxpayer attempts to claim ITC on running costs. The logic: if the vehicle itself does not qualify for ITC, its operating costs equally do not qualify.

**Common Practical Scenarios:**

| Scenario | ITC Status |
|---|---|
| Company purchases sedan for MD's business travel | Blocked — ≤13 seats, not for further supply/transport service/training |
| Automobile dealer purchases cars as stock | Available — exception (A): further supply |
| Taxi company purchases fleet of 7-seater cabs | Available — exception (B): transportation of passengers |
| Driving school purchases training cars | Available — exception (C): imparting driving training |
| Company insures its company car used by CFO | Blocked — companion block; principal vehicle is blocked |
| Delivery company purchases goods carrier truck | Not blocked — truck is for transportation of goods, not persons; 17(5)(a) does not apply |
| Company purchases 14-seater mini-bus for employee transport | Not blocked under 17(5)(a) — exceeds 13-seat threshold; may be blocked under 17(5)(b)(iii) for rent-a-cab if applicable |

### Section 17(5)(aa) — Vessels and Aircraft
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(aa) CGST Act, 2017 inserted by CGST (Amendment) Act 2018"}
{topic: "Section 17(5)(aa) — Vessels and Aircraft"}

**Statutory Text:**
"(aa) vessels and aircraft except when they are used—
(i) for making the following taxable supplies, namely:—
(A) further supply of such vessels or aircraft; or
(B) transportation of passengers; or
(C) imparting training on navigating such vessels; or
(D) imparting training on flying such aircraft;
(ii) for transportation of goods;"

**Annotation:**

**Scope:** This clause extends the motor vehicle block to waterborne and airborne conveyances. "Vessels" covers ships, boats, barges, ferries, yachts. "Aircraft" covers aeroplanes, helicopters, gliders. There is no seating capacity threshold for vessels and aircraft — the block is categorical regardless of size.

**Exception (i)(A) — Further Supply:** Vessel/aircraft dealers and lessors selling or transferring ownership of these assets: ITC available.

**Exception (i)(B) — Transportation of Passengers:** Airlines providing commercial passenger air services; ferry operators; cruise ship operators: ITC available on their fleet. The passenger transportation must be a taxable supply.

**Exception (i)(C) and (D) — Training:** Maritime training institutes (navigation training); flying schools and pilot training academies: ITC on the training vessels/aircraft is available.

**Exception (ii) — Transportation of Goods:** Cargo shipping companies; air freight operators; inland waterway cargo services: ITC available. This is a significant exception — cargo shipping is a major business, and blocking ITC on cargo vessels would be economically damaging and contrary to the ITC-follows-output-tax principle (cargo transportation is fully taxable).

**Common Practical Scenarios:**

| Scenario | ITC Status |
|---|---|
| Corporation maintains a yacht for client entertainment | Blocked — personal/entertainment use, none of the exceptions apply |
| Airline purchases aircraft for commercial passenger service | Available — exception (i)(B): transportation of passengers |
| Shipping company purchases cargo vessel | Available — exception (ii): transportation of goods |
| Flying school purchases trainer aircraft | Available — exception (i)(D): imparting training on flying |
| Real-estate company charters private helicopter for site visits | Blocked — not for further supply, not for transport of passengers as taxable service, not for training |

### Section 17(5)(b) — Specified Services
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(b) CGST Act, 2017"}
{topic: "Section 17(5)(b) — Food, Entertainment, Insurance, Membership, LTA"}

**Statutory Text (clause (b)(i)):**
"(b) the following supply of goods or services or both—
(i) food and beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services, cosmetic and plastic surgery, leasing, renting or hiring of motor vehicles, vessels or aircraft referred to in clause (a) or clause (aa) except when used for the purposes specified therein, life insurance and health insurance:

Provided that the input tax credit in respect of such goods or services or both shall be available where an inward supply of goods or services or both of a particular category is used by a registered person for making an outward taxable supply of the same category of goods or services or both or as an element of a taxable composite or mixed supply;"

**Statutory Text (clause (b)(ii)):**
"(ii) membership of a club, health and fitness centre;"

**Statutory Text (clause (b)(iii)):**
"(iii) rent-a-cab, life insurance, health insurance except where—
(A) the Government notifies the services which are obligatory for an employer to provide to its employees under any law for the time being in force; or
(B) such inward supply of goods or services or both of a particular category is used by a registered person for making an outward taxable supply of the same category of goods or services or both;"

**Statutory Text (clause (b)(iv)):**
"(iv) travel benefits extended to employees on vacation such as leave or home travel concession;"

**Annotation — Clause (b)(i):**

**Food and Beverages:** The block covers all food and drink services procured in the course of business. Canteen services, office tea/coffee supplies, restaurant bills reimbursed to employees, working lunch expenses — all blocked. **Critical Exception — Factories Act Obligation:** Where the employer is obligated under Section 46 of the Factories Act, 1948 to provide a canteen (applies to factories with ≥250 workers), ITC on canteen services is available per CBIC Circular No. 172/04/2022-GST dated 06 July 2022. This is the government notification route under clause (iii)(A) applied by analogy to clause (b)(i). The key condition: the factory must have 250+ workers, and the canteen must be pursuant to the statutory obligation.

**[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]** Under the Maharashtra Factories Rules, 1963 (framed under the Factories Act, 1948), factories with ≥250 workers must maintain a canteen. Accordingly, the Circular 172/04/2022 ITC availability applies to qualifying Maharashtra factories. Maharashtra factories with fewer than 250 workers providing voluntary canteens: ITC blocked. Additionally, factories in Maharashtra's Special Economic Zones (SEZs) operating under the SEZ Act should confirm whether the Factories Act obligation applies to their specific zone — SEZ units may have separate welfare regulations.

**Outdoor Catering:** Client entertainment parties, product launch catering, annual day celebrations, business conclaves — ITC blocked. However, if a catering company provides its own catering services, the food it procures as raw material is used for "making outward taxable supply of the same category" — ITC available per the proviso.

**Beauty Treatment, Health Services, Cosmetic and Plastic Surgery:** Employer-paid spa treatments, beauty services for employees, cosmetic procedures (even if for customer-facing roles like flight attendants or brand ambassadors) — blocked. Health services in the general sense (physiotherapy camps, annual health check-up packages) — blocked. **Exception:** A hospital providing health services (HSN 9993) can claim ITC on medicines, equipment, and health service inputs — the "same category" proviso applies.

**Leasing, Renting, or Hiring of Motor Vehicles/Vessels/Aircraft:** If the underlying vehicle/vessel/aircraft is covered under clause (a) or (aa) and none of the exceptions in those clauses apply, then the ITC on the lease/rent/hire of such vehicle/vessel/aircraft is also blocked. This prevents the blocked-vehicle ITC from being accessed indirectly by renting rather than owning the vehicle.

**Life Insurance and Health Insurance (under (b)(i)):** This clause (b)(i) reference to "life insurance and health insurance" — cross-read with clause (b)(iii) which contains the exceptions — means the block on life and health insurance in (b)(i) is subject to the same exceptions as (b)(iii).

**Annotation — Clause (b)(ii):**

**Club and Fitness Centre Memberships:** This is one of the most unambiguous blocked items — there are no exceptions. Club membership fees (social clubs, golf clubs, professional clubs, Gymkhana), health club subscriptions (gym memberships, fitness centres), yoga centre subscriptions — all fully blocked. Even where the club membership is a condition of employment or a professional requirement (e.g., a senior advocate whose firm pays for bar association membership), ITC is blocked. The provision applies to "membership" fees specifically; standalone use of a facility may require separate analysis.

**Annotation — Clause (b)(iii):**

**Rent-a-Cab:** Hiring motor vehicles for employee transportation (the classic "cab for employees" scenario) — ITC blocked. A company hiring a fleet of cabs from a cab operator to transport employees to and from office: ITC on the cab hire charge blocked.

**Exception (A) — Government-Notified Mandatory Employer Obligation:** Where the Government has notified specific services as obligatory for employers to provide to employees under any law, ITC on those services is available. As of May 2026, the key notified categories are:
- Canteen services where mandated by Factories Act (Circular 172/04/2022)
- No specific notification has been issued making cab services or life insurance mandatory for all employers

**Exception (B) — Same Category Outward Supply:** A cab aggregator (e.g., a company running an employee transportation services business, or an outsourced transportation vendor itself) uses rent-a-cab services to make outward taxable supply of rent-a-cab services: ITC available.

**Health Insurance Exception:** Group health insurance for employees: ITC blocked unless:
- Government notifies it as mandatory under any law (as of May 2026, no broad notification exists; however, IRDAI's 2020 regulations require certain employers in certain sectors to provide health insurance — whether this qualifies as "any law" is a grey area under active litigation)
- Or the employer is in the business of providing health insurance (same category)

**Annotation — Clause (b)(iv):**

**Leave Travel Allowance / Home Travel Concession:** Air tickets, train tickets, hotel bookings for employee personal vacations — ITC blocked. This applies even where LTA/LTC is a contractual component of the employee's compensation package. The block is triggered because the travel is for "vacation" (personal consumption), not business. Contrast: business travel tickets booked by the company for employees on official duty — this does not fall under "travel benefits on vacation" and is not blocked under 17(5)(b)(iv); however, it may be blocked under 17(5)(g) if it is personal in nature.

### Section 17(5)(c) — Works Contract Services for Immovable Property
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(c) CGST Act, 2017 as amended by Finance Act 2021 (retrospective from 01.07.2017)"}
{topic: "Section 17(5)(c) — Works Contract for Immovable Property"}

**Statutory Text:**
"(c) works contract services when supplied for construction of an immovable property (other than plant and machinery) except where it is an input service for further supply of works contract service;"

**Explanation (applicable to both (c) and (d)):**
"Explanation.— For the purposes of clauses (c) and (d), the expression 'construction' includes re-construction, renovation, additions or alterations or repairs, to the extent of capitalisation, to the said immovable property;"

**Annotation:**

**"Works Contract" — Definition:** Section 2(119) of the CGST Act defines "works contract" as a contract for building, construction, fabrication, completion, erection, installation, fitting out, improvement, modification, repair, maintenance, renovation, alteration or commissioning of any immovable property wherein transfer of property in goods (whether as goods or in some other form) is involved in the execution of such contract. Works contract is thus a composite supply of both goods (materials) and services (labour). The GST rate on works contract services for construction of immovable property (other than government works) is 18%.

**Scope of Block:** When a registered person receives works contract services for the purpose of constructing an immovable property (building, structure, road, bridge, dam, factory building, warehouse), and that immovable property is not "plant and machinery," the ITC on the works contract service is blocked. This applies to:
- Office building construction
- Warehouse construction
- Factory building (the building shell, not the machinery inside)
- Commercial complex construction
- Hotel construction
- Hospital building construction

**The Finance Act 2021 Retrospective Amendment — "To the Extent of Capitalisation":**

Prior to the Finance Act 2021 amendment, the Explanation to Section 17(5)(c)/(d) did not exist. The original statutory text merely said "construction of an immovable property." Many taxpayers interpreted this to mean only fresh construction — not renovation, not repair. They claimed ITC on renovation and repair works contract services on the basis that "renovation is not construction."

The Finance Act 2021 inserted the Explanation, operative from 01 July 2017 (retrospective), clarifying that "construction" includes reconstruction, renovation, additions, alterations, and repairs — but only "to the extent of capitalisation." This critical qualifier means:

- If renovation/repair expenditure is **capitalised** in the books (debited to capital work-in-progress or fixed assets account): ITC blocked (it is "construction to the extent of capitalisation")
- If renovation/repair expenditure is treated as **revenue expenditure** (debited to P&L as repair and maintenance expense): ITC NOT blocked by the Explanation (the Explanation only blocks capitalised amounts)

**Practical Application of the Capitalisation Test:**

| Nature of Work | Accounting Treatment | ITC on Works Contract |
|---|---|---|
| New building construction | Capitalised (CWIP → Fixed Asset) | Blocked |
| Office floor renovation (capitalised as leasehold improvement) | Capitalised | Blocked |
| Structural repair to factory building (capitalised) | Capitalised | Blocked |
| Painting of office (charged to P&L) | Revenue expense | NOT blocked by Explanation; may still be blocked as personal/non-business if applicable |
| Annual maintenance of building (charged to P&L) | Revenue expense | NOT blocked by Explanation |
| Addition of new wing to factory building (capitalised) | Capitalised | Blocked |

**Exception — Further Supply of Works Contract:** A sub-contractor receiving works contract services from the main contractor, to be used as input for the sub-contractor's outward supply of works contract services to the main contractor, can claim ITC. This is the classic sub-contracting exception: Main Contractor awards sub-contract → Sub-Contractor provides works contract back → Main Contractor can also claim ITC on sub-contract works contract (since this is input for main contractor's outward works contract service). The ITC chain works across the construction supply chain at the works-contract-service level, just not at the end-user level.

**Common Practical Scenarios:**

| Scenario | ITC Status |
|---|---|
| IT company engages civil contractor to build new office campus | Blocked — construction of immovable property |
| Real estate developer building flats for sale | ITC available on works contract if building is under construction and being sold (developer is providing works contract as outward supply); complex analysis required |
| Factory owner engages contractor to build new production hall | Blocked — immovable property (factory building is not plant and machinery) |
| Main contractor receives civil work sub-contract | Available — further supply of works contract exception applies |
| Company renovates office floor and capitalises as leasehold improvement | Blocked — capitalised renovation falls within the Explanation |
| Company repaints office annually, charged to P&L | Not blocked by 17(5)(c) — revenue expense, Explanation does not apply; but check if any other block applies |

### Section 17(5)(d) — Goods/Services for Own Account Immovable Property Construction
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(d) CGST Act, 2017 as amended by Finance Act 2021"}
{topic: "Section 17(5)(d) — Own Account Construction"}

**Statutory Text:**
"(d) goods or services or both received by a taxable person for construction of an immovable property (other than plant or machinery) on his own account including when such goods or services or both are used in the course or furtherance of business."

**Annotation:**

**Scope:** Clause (d) addresses the scenario where the registered person does not engage a works contractor (which would be clause (c)), but instead directly procures goods and/or services and undertakes construction on its own account. For example:
- A company directly purchases cement, steel, bricks, and contracts labour separately (not as a works contract composite) for building construction
- An infrastructure company purchasing construction materials directly and deploying its own workforce for construction

**The Key Phrase — "On His Own Account":** The block applies when the registered person constructs the immovable property for its own use (retention), not for sale. Real estate developers who construct properties primarily for sale face a different analytical framework (where construction activity is a taxable supply of works contract service, and ITC may be partially available). Clause (d) targets the end-user who constructs for self-use.

**"Including When Used in Course or Furtherance of Business":** This phrase is highly significant — it removes the "business use" defence entirely. A taxpayer cannot argue "the factory building I constructed is 100% for business use, so ITC should be available." Parliament has explicitly stated that even when the construction is for business purposes, the ITC on goods and services used in that construction is blocked. This is the core of the principle that civil structures (buildings, factories) do not generate ITC on their construction inputs, regardless of business nexus.

**Distinction from Clause (c):**
- Clause (c): The registered person **receives a works contract service** from a contractor (composite supply of goods + services)
- Clause (d): The registered person **directly procures goods and/or services** separately and performs construction on own account

Both are blocked. Together, clauses (c) and (d) ensure that no matter how the construction activity is structured — through a works contractor or on own account — the ITC on construction of immovable property (other than plant and machinery) is comprehensively blocked.

**Same Finance Act 2021 Explanation Applies:** The Explanation defining "construction" to include capitalised renovation/repair applies equally to clause (d). Therefore, goods and services directly purchased for a capitalised renovation are blocked under 17(5)(d) even if procured independently (not through a works contract).

**Plant and Machinery Exception:** If the goods or services are for construction of plant and machinery (as defined in the Explanation to Section 17), ITC is available. For instance, fabrication materials for a CNC machine or industrial furnace are not blocked even if the fabrication is on own account.

### Section 17(5)(e) — Composition Scheme Supplies
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(e) CGST Act, 2017; Section 10 CGST Act, 2017"}
{topic: "Section 17(5)(e) — Composition Scheme"}

**Statutory Text:**
"(e) goods or services or both on which tax has been paid under section 10;"

**Annotation:**

Section 10 of the CGST Act allows eligible registered persons (manufacturers, traders, restaurant service providers below specified turnover thresholds) to opt for the composition scheme, paying tax at a flat rate (1%, 5%, or 6% depending on category) on turnover instead of the standard GST rate. The fundamental trade-off of the composition scheme is:
- **Benefit:** Simple flat-rate tax compliance; no requirement to maintain detailed input/output records
- **Cost:** No ITC available on any input

Section 17(5)(e) codifies this cost by making it an absolute bar under the blocked ITC provision. A composition dealer cannot claim ITC on any goods or services purchased for its business. Furthermore, a composition dealer cannot charge GST to its customers (it can only collect the composition tax as a lump sum). This makes the composition scheme suitable only for small businesses with predominantly end-consumer clientele.

**Turnover Limits (as of May 2026):**
- Composition scheme available if aggregate annual turnover ≤ ₹1.5 crore (₹75 lakh for certain states — not Maharashtra)
- For service providers: ₹50 lakh (with services not exceeding 10% of turnover)
- [MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC] Maharashtra follows the central ₹1.5 crore threshold; there is no separate Maharashtra-specific threshold reduction.

### Section 17(5)(f) — Non-Resident Taxable Persons
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(f) CGST Act, 2017; Section 27 CGST Act, 2017"}
{topic: "Section 17(5)(f) — Non-Resident Taxable Person"}

**Statutory Text:**
"(f) goods or services or both received by a non-resident taxable person except on goods imported by him;"

**Annotation:**

A Non-Resident Taxable Person (NRTP) is defined under Section 2(77) of the CGST Act as any person who occasionally undertakes transactions involving supply of goods or services or both, whether as principal or agent or in any other capacity, but who has no fixed place of business or residence in India. NRTPs are subject to a special registration regime under Section 27 — they must register at least five days before the first taxable supply and deposit estimated advance tax.

**Scope of Block:** An NRTP cannot claim ITC on goods or services received within India. This is because NRTPs are transient operators — they come to India for specific events (exhibitions, performances, conferences), complete their supplies, and leave. The GST framework treats them differently from regular taxable persons, and restricts their ITC entitlement to ensure that the tax collected from their Indian customers is not offset by credits on their Indian expenses.

**Exception — Imported Goods:** The NRTP can claim ITC on goods that it has imported into India. When goods are imported, Integrated GST (IGST) is levied under the Customs Tariff Act at the point of import. The NRTP is entitled to credit of this IGST on imported goods because the imported goods are physically brought in for use in making taxable supplies in India. This exception is narrow — it covers only imported goods, not services received in India.

### Section 17(5)(g) — Personal Consumption
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(g) CGST Act, 2017"}
{topic: "Section 17(5)(g) — Personal Consumption"}

**Statutory Text:**
"(g) goods or services or both used for personal consumption;"

**Annotation:**

**Scope:** This is the broadest and most open-ended of the Section 17(5) clauses. It blocks ITC on any goods or services used for personal consumption — by the proprietor, partners, directors, employees, family members, or any individual — where the use is personal in nature rather than for the business. The provision has no statutory definition of "personal consumption," leaving it to be determined by facts and circumstances.

**Principles for Identifying Personal Consumption:**
1. **Nature of the supply:** Inherently personal goods and services (food for personal meals, personal clothing, personal travel, personal healthcare beyond employment obligations) signal personal consumption
2. **Who benefits:** If the primary beneficiary of the supply is an individual for their personal enjoyment or welfare, it is personal consumption
3. **Business nexus:** Even if there is some tangential business connection (business dinner, team outing), if the dominant purpose is personal enjoyment, it is personal consumption
4. **Employment obligation:** Goods and services provided to employees as part of a statutory or contractual obligation forming part of CTC (and thus already subject to employer's accounting and payroll systems) may escape the personal consumption block — but this is fact-specific

**Interaction with Other Clauses:** Section 17(5)(g) serves as a residual catch-all. If a supply is not blocked by clauses (a) through (f), but is consumed personally, clause (g) blocks the ITC. Conversely, many specific items that are personal in nature are already listed in clauses (a)–(f), making clause (g) redundant for those specific items but essential for items not listed elsewhere.

### Section 17(5)(h) — Lost, Stolen, Destroyed Goods; Gifts; Free Samples
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(h) CGST Act, 2017"}
{topic: "Section 17(5)(h) — Lost/Destroyed Goods and Gifts"}

**Statutory Text:**
"(h) goods lost, stolen, destroyed, written off or disposed of by way of gift or free samples; and"

**Annotation:**

**Scope:** This clause recognises that ITC is only justified when the goods flow through the supply chain and output tax is eventually paid on them. When goods exit the supply chain without output tax — through loss, theft, destruction, write-off, gifting, or free distribution — the ITC originally availed on those goods must be reversed. The clause effectively requires a reconciliation between inventory movement and ITC: every unit of input must either be consumed in making a taxable output or have its ITC reversed.

**Goods Lost (Fire, Flood, Theft):** When inventory is destroyed by fire, flood, or natural calamity, or stolen by employees or external parties, the ITC on the lost/stolen goods must be reversed. This reversal should be done in the tax period in which the loss is ascertained. Insurance claims received on the lost goods are a separate matter — the insurance payout itself may attract GST in certain circumstances, but the ITC reversal obligation under 17(5)(h) is independent of insurance recovery.

**Goods Written Off (Expired, Obsolete):** Expired medicines, obsolete components, perishable goods past their shelf life — when these are written off in the books of account, the ITC must be reversed in that period. The write-off in the accounts is the trigger.

**Gifts to Employees/Customers:** Gifts given to customers (Diwali gifts, business promotion gifts), gifts given to employees (non-cash benefits, achievement awards), gifts given to dealers or trade partners — ITC on the gifted goods must be reversed. Note: Schedule I Entry 2 deems "gift of goods made without consideration where input tax credit has been availed" as a supply (if value exceeds ₹50,000 per person per year). The taxpayer must:
- Either pay output GST on the deemed supply of gifts (Schedule I, Entry 2), in which case ITC need not be separately reversed (the ITC is absorbed by output tax on the deemed supply)
- Or reverse the ITC under Section 17(5)(h) if not treating the gift as a deemed supply

**Free Samples:** Pharmaceutical companies distributing free samples to doctors; FMCG companies distributing promotional samples — ITC on free samples must be reversed. This has been a significant compliance point for pharmaceutical companies following CBIC Circular No. 92/11/2019-GST.

**[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]** Maharashtra does not have any state-specific rules on ITC reversal for free samples. MGST mirrors the CGST provision identically. Maharashtra pharmaceutical companies distributing samples should comply with both CGST and MGST reversal requirements — reversal under CGST Rules and corresponding reversal under MGST rules for SGST credit.

### Section 17(5)(i) — Tax Paid Under Enforcement Proceedings
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(i) CGST Act, 2017; Sections 74, 129, 130 CGST Act, 2017"}
{topic: "Section 17(5)(i) — Enforcement Tax Payments"}

**Statutory Text:**
"(i) any tax paid in accordance with the provisions of sections 74, 129 and 130."

**Annotation:**

**Section 74 — Tax Paid Pursuant to Fraud/Suppression/Wilful Misstatement:** Section 74 authorises the tax officer to issue a show cause notice and demand unpaid tax in cases involving fraud, suppression of facts, or wilful misstatement. When a taxpayer pays tax pursuant to a Section 74 adjudication or settlement, that tax payment cannot generate ITC. This is a policy deterrent — if fraudulent claims could generate fresh ITC, the sanction effect of Section 74 would be nullified.

**Section 129 — Tax Paid for Release of Detained/Seized Goods:** When goods in transit are detained or seized under Section 129, the owner can secure their release by paying the applicable tax (and penalty). The tax paid for release of detained goods is blocked under Section 17(5)(i) — it cannot be claimed as ITC. This prevents tax-in-detention-release from being recycled back as ITC.

**Section 130 — Tax Paid Pursuant to Confiscation:** When goods are confiscated under Section 130 (for specified contraventions), and the owner pays the fine to secure the release of the confiscated goods, the tax component of that payment is blocked from ITC.

## Legislative History — Finance Act 2021 Retrospective Amendment
{law_reference: "Finance Act 2021; Section 17(5)(c) and (d) CGST Act, 2017"}
{amendment_date: "01-Jan-2022 notified; retrospective from 01-Jul-2017"}
{topic: "Finance Act 2021 — Retrospective Capitalisation Amendment"}

### The Pre-Amendment Position

From July 1, 2017 to January 31, 2022 (i.e., before the Finance Act 2021 notification), Section 17(5)(c) and (d) did not contain any Explanation. The statutory text blocked ITC on "works contract services when supplied for construction of an immovable property" and "goods or services received for construction of an immovable property on own account." The word "construction" was not statutorily defined for this purpose, though the definition of "works contract" in Section 2(119) included "renovation" and "repair" within the wider works contract definition.

Many taxpayers took the position that:
1. "Construction" in Section 17(5)(c)/(d) means fresh construction only — bringing a new structure into existence
2. Renovation, repair, and additions to existing structures are not "construction"
3. Therefore ITC on renovation/repair works contract services and goods is available

This interpretation gained significant support from several Advance Rulings (most notably AAR Maharashtra rulings in 2019-2020 period) and had reasonable textual basis.

### The Finance Act 2021 Amendment — Key Features

The Finance Act 2021 inserted the Explanation to Section 17(5) defining "construction" to include "re-construction, renovation, additions or alterations or repairs, to the extent of capitalisation." Key features:

1. **Retrospective from 01 July 2017:** The Explanation is expressly made applicable from the date of commencement of the CGST Act — meaning every taxpayer who claimed ITC on capitalised renovation from July 2017 onwards is exposed to demand + interest + penalty

2. **"To the Extent of Capitalisation" Qualifier:** The Explanation is not blanket — it brings renovation within "construction" only to the extent the renovation cost is capitalised in the books. Revenue-expensed repair/maintenance remains outside the block (at least under this Explanation)

3. **Impact on Outstanding ITC Claims:** Taxpayers who had availed ITC on capitalised renovation received demands for reversal with interest (at 18% per annum) computed from the date of wrong availment to the date of reversal — potentially 4-5 years of interest on substantial ITC amounts

### Litigation Challenges to Retrospectivity

The retrospective application has been challenged before various High Courts:

- **Gujarat High Court:** In *Safari Retreats Pvt. Ltd. v. Chief Commissioner of Central Goods and Services Tax* (2022), the court extensively discussed the scope of Section 17(5)(d) and referred the matter to a larger bench. The Supreme Court admitted the matter. The fundamental question: can Section 17(5)(d) block ITC on goods and services used in construction of immovable property that itself generates taxable rental income?

- **Supreme Court — Safari Retreats (Larger Bench):** The case is under hearing. If the Supreme Court holds that Section 17(5)(d) is unconstitutional or does not apply to immovable property used for making taxable supplies (such as rental), it could result in significant ITC relief for commercial real estate.

- **General Retrospectivity Challenge:** The power of Parliament to enact retrospective tax provisions is well-established (within constitutional limits); the retrospective amendment to Sections 17(5)(c)/(d) has been challenged primarily on the ground of legitimate expectation and that it imposes a burden on completed transactions. No High Court has struck down the retrospective amendment as of May 2026.

**Practical Compliance Response:** Taxpayers who claimed ITC on capitalised renovation between July 2017 and January 2022 should:
1. Review all renovation works contracts in the period and identify capitalised amounts
2. Quantify the ITC availed on such capitalised renovation
3. Consider voluntary reversal with interest to avoid penal consequences
4. Monitor Safari Retreats SC proceedings for potential relief

## Plant and Machinery Exception
{law_reference: "Explanation to Section 17 CGST Act, 2017"}
{topic: "Plant and Machinery — Definition and ITC Implications"}

### Statutory Definition

The Explanation to Section 17 of the CGST Act defines "plant and machinery" as:

"'plant and machinery' means apparatus, equipment, and machinery fixed to earth by foundation or structural support that are used for making outward supply of goods or services or both and includes such foundation and structural support but excludes—
(i) land, building or any other civil structures;
(ii) telecommunication towers;
(iii) pipelines laid outside the factory premises"

### Significance

The "plant and machinery" exception carves out a critically important category from the blocked ITC regime:
- Works contract services for construction of **plant and machinery** → ITC **available** (not blocked under 17(5)(c))
- Goods/services for own-account construction of **plant and machinery** → ITC **available** (not blocked under 17(5)(d))

This exception exists because plant and machinery, unlike civil buildings, is directly used in the production of taxable outputs. A CNC machine, a chemical reactor, an industrial furnace, a textile loom — these are inputs to the production process, and blocking ITC on their construction would violate the input-follows-output principle.

### Key Elements of the Definition

**"Fixed to earth by foundation or structural support":** The apparatus/machinery must be affixed to the earth — it cannot be merely placed on the floor. Mobile plant and equipment may not qualify. However, temporary or removable foundations can qualify if they are the means by which the equipment is fixed during operation.

**"Used for making outward supply of goods or services or both":** The plant and machinery must be directly involved in producing the taxable output. Equipment used solely for internal administration (air conditioning in office areas, CCTV in parking lots) may not qualify as "plant and machinery" for this exception.

**Exclusions:**
- Land and building: No matter how industrial the building, a factory building is not "plant and machinery" — it is a civil structure
- Telecommunication towers: Despite being functional equipment, telecom towers are expressly excluded — ITC on construction of telecom towers is blocked
- Pipelines outside factory: Pipelines within factory premises may qualify as plant; those outside do not

### Common Grey Areas

| Asset | Plant and Machinery? | ITC Status |
|---|---|---|
| CNC machining centre (bolted to factory floor) | Yes — fixed to earth, used in production | Available |
| Chemical storage tank (concrete-embedded) | Yes — fixed to earth, used in production/supply | Available |
| Industrial air compressor (bolted base) | Yes — fixed, used in production | Available |
| Factory building (RCC structure) | No — civil structure excluded | Blocked |
| Lift/elevator in factory building | Arguable — functionally equipment, but part of civil structure; advance ruling recommended | Grey area |
| Air conditioning plant for factory floor | Arguable — if directly for production process (e.g., clean room) it may qualify; if for comfort only, it does not | Fact-specific |
| Telecom tower | Expressly excluded by the definition | Blocked |
| Pipeline inside factory premises | Included in plant and machinery (not in exclusion list) | Available |
| Pipeline outside factory premises | Expressly excluded | Blocked |
| Wind turbine generator (affixed to foundation) | Yes — fixed to earth, used for generation of electricity (taxable supply) | Available |
| Solar panel mounting structure (ground-mounted, fixed) | Arguable — mounting structure is foundation/support for plant | Advance ruling recommended |

## Schedule III — Supplies Neither Supply of Goods nor Services
{law_reference: "Schedule III, CGST Act, 2017"}
{topic: "Schedule III — Interface with ITC"}

### What Schedule III Does

Schedule III of the CGST Act lists activities or transactions that shall be treated as neither a supply of goods nor a supply of services. These are outside the GST levy entirely — not taxed, not exempt, but simply not a "supply" for GST purposes. Current Schedule III entries include:
1. Services by an employee to the employer in the course of employment
2. Services by any court or tribunal
3. Functions performed by Members of Parliament, State Legislature, local authorities, etc.
4. Services of a funeral, burial, crematorium, or mortuary
5. Sale of land and sale of building after completion certificate
6. Actionable claims (other than lottery, betting, gambling)
7. Supply of goods from a place in non-taxable territory to another place in non-taxable territory (high seas sales after amendment)

### Schedule III vs Section 17(5) — Conceptual Difference

Schedule III and Section 17(5) operate at entirely different levels:

- **Schedule III:** The activity is not a "supply" at all — no GST applies, no obligation to pay tax, no registration required for this activity alone
- **Section 17(5):** The inward supply IS a supply and DOES attract GST, but the recipient cannot claim ITC on it

A taxpayer performing a Schedule III activity (e.g., a real estate developer who has completed and obtained a completion certificate for flats being sold) faces an ITC apportionment question, not a Section 17(5) blocking question. The completed flat sale is Schedule III (no supply = no output tax); the inputs used in making the completed flat must have their ITC reversed/apportioned under Rule 42/43 as if it were an "exempt supply."

**[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]** Maharashtra GST Act Schedule III is identical to CGST Act Schedule III. However, the Maharashtra Stamp Duty regime for immovable property has specific treatment for GST paid on construction — buyers of under-construction flats in Maharashtra who pay GST can factor it in computing their net cost. The Schedule III treatment of completed flat sales (no GST) means that the developer's ITC on construction inputs must be apportioned/reversed for the completed-flat portion of the project.

## Rule 42 and Rule 43 — Companion Rules to Section 17
{law_reference: "Rule 42, Rule 43 CGST Rules, 2017"}
{topic: "Rule 42 and Rule 43 — Interface with Section 17(5)"}

### Rule 42 — Apportionment of ITC for Inputs and Input Services

Rule 42 prescribes the method for computing the reversal of ITC on inputs (goods) and input services that are used for both taxable and exempt supplies. The formula-based approach considers:
- T1: Inputs exclusively used for taxable supply — full ITC
- T2: Inputs exclusively used for non-business/personal purposes — nil ITC
- T3: Inputs exclusively used for exempt supply — nil ITC
- T4 (= T - T1 - T2 - T3): Common inputs/input services used for both
- D1: Provisionally blocked from T4 = (Exempt Supply Value / Total Supply Value) × T4
- D2: Inputs used for non-business purposes (estimated by taxpayer)

**Rule 42 does NOT override Section 17(5):** The Rule 42 formula is applied to the eligible ITC pool. Section 17(5) blocked ITC never enters this formula — it is removed before the Rule 42 calculation begins. T2 in Rule 42 covers non-business/personal use that does not fall within Section 17(5) categories — Rule 42 and Section 17(5) are complementary, not alternative.

### Rule 43 — Apportionment of ITC for Capital Goods

Rule 43 prescribes the method for computing ITC on capital goods (as defined in Section 2(19) CGST Act — goods with useful life of more than one year, used for the purpose of business). Capital goods ITC is spread over a 60-month period (5 years), and the exempt-supply portion is reversed each month based on the exempt/total output ratio.

**Application to blocked capital goods:** Capital goods that are motor vehicles (≤13 seater) are blocked under Section 17(5)(a) — they do not enter the Rule 43 calculation. Capital goods used in construction of immovable property are blocked under 17(5)(c)/(d) — they do not enter Rule 43. Only eligible capital goods enter the Rule 43 apportionment.

### The Correct Processing Sequence

For each tax period's ITC computation:

```
Step 1: Identify total input tax from all inward supplies
        ↓
Step 2: Remove Section 17(5) blocked items completely
        (These are not ITC at all — they go to Table 4(D)(1) of GSTR-3B)
        ↓
Step 3: From remaining ITC, identify:
        - Exclusively taxable-use inputs → Full ITC (Table 4(A))
        - Exclusively exempt-use inputs → Zero ITC (Rule 42, T3)
        - Exclusively non-business inputs not in 17(5) → Zero ITC (Rule 42, T2)
        - Common inputs → Rule 42/43 apportionment (Table 4(D)(2))
        ↓
Step 4: Net ITC = Step 3 eligible ITC net of Rule 42/43 reversals
```

## Comparison: Section 17(5) vs Rule 42/43
{topic: "Comparison — Section 17(5) vs Rule 42/43"}

| Parameter | Section 17(5) — Blocked ITC | Rule 42/43 — Proportional Apportionment |
|---|---|---|
| **Legal basis** | Section 17(5) CGST Act, 2017 | Rule 42/43 CGST Rules, 2017 r/w Section 17(2) |
| **Nature of denial** | Absolute categorical block | Proportional reversal |
| **What triggers** | Type/category of inward supply | Exempt/non-taxable output in the same period |
| **Amount denied** | 100% of ITC on the blocked supply | Only the exempt-output-attributable fraction |
| **Fluctuates with output mix?** | No — once blocked, always blocked | Yes — varies month to month, year to year |
| **Reversal mechanism** | Should not be availed; if availed, reverse in 4(D)(1) | Periodic provisional reversal; annual finalisation |
| **Remedy available?** | Only if supply moves out of blocked category (e.g., vehicle dealer demonstrates further supply) | ITC recoverable if exempt output reduces |
| **GSTR-3B reporting** | Table 4(D)(1) — "As per Section 17(5)" | Table 4(D)(2) — "Others" (Rule 42/43 reversals) |
| **Sequencing** | First — applied before any apportionment | Second — applied to the pool remaining after blocking |
| **Retrospective exposure** | Yes for 17(5)(c)/(d) post-FA 2021 | No retrospective changes to Rule 42/43 formula |
| **Annual Return impact** | GSTR-9 Table 7 — ITC reversed and ineligible | GSTR-9 Table 7 — Rule 42/43 reversals separately |
| **Interest on wrong availment** | 18% per annum under Section 50 | 18% per annum under Section 50 |

**[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]** The Maharashtra State GST Act, 2017 ("MGST Act") contains provisions mirroring Section 17(5) CGST Act identically. The Maharashtra GST Rules, 2017 contain Rules 42 and 43 in identical form to the CGST Rules. Maharashtra taxpayers claiming both CGST ITC and SGST ITC must apply the blocking and apportionment analysis twice — once for CGST and once for SGST — and the result will be identical since both statutes are mirrors. There are no Maharashtra-specific divergences in the blocked ITC provisions or apportionment rules as of May 2026. Maharashtra SGST advance rulings (issued by the Maharashtra AAR and AAAR) on Section 17(5) are persuasive but not binding outside Maharashtra, and Maharashtra-based taxpayers should monitor Maharashtra AAR rulings specifically for the most jurisdictionally relevant precedents.
