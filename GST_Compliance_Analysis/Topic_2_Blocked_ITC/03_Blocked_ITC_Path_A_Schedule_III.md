## Section 17(5)(a) — Motor Vehicles for Transportation of Persons
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(a) CGST Act, 2017"}
{amendment_date: "01-Jul-2017"}

### Statutory Text
> ITC shall not be available in respect of: motor vehicles for transportation of persons having approved seating capacity of not more than thirteen persons (including the driver), except when they are used for making the following taxable supplies, namely:— (A) further supply of such motor vehicles; or (B) transportation of passengers; or (C) imparting training on driving such motor vehicles.

### Scope of Block
The block covers:
- **Purchase** of motor vehicles: the GST on purchase price is blocked
- **Leasing and renting** of such vehicles (cross-ref to Section 17(5)(b)(i))
- **Insurance, servicing, repair and maintenance** of such blocked vehicles
- The seating capacity test uses the **approved capacity per the RC book**, not actual passengers carried

### Seating Capacity — The 13-Person Rule

| Vehicle | Capacity | ITC Status |
|---------|----------|------------|
| Car/Sedan/SUV (≤7 seats) | ≤13 | BLOCKED |
| Mini-van/Innova (7-8 seats) | ≤13 | BLOCKED |
| Tempo Traveller (12 seats) | ≤13 | BLOCKED |
| Minibus (14 seats) | >13 | NOT BLOCKED |
| Bus/Coach (20+ seats) | >13 | NOT BLOCKED |
| Ambulance (if ≤13 seat) | ≤13 | Blocked UNLESS used for taxable transportation |

### Three Exceptions — When ITC IS Available

**Exception A: Further Supply (Vehicle Dealer)**
ITC on vehicle stock is available to a dealer whose business is selling motor vehicles. The vehicle must be held as stock-in-trade, not used as a demonstration/test-drive vehicle for prolonged personal use. Documentation: stock register showing the specific vehicle is inventory.

**Exception B: Transportation of Passengers**
Taxi operators, cab aggregator fleets, inter-city bus services (if seating capacity ≤13 but service is taxable passenger transport). The operator must charge GST on the passenger service (18% or applicable rate). If the transport service itself is exempt (e.g., reserved RCM categories), the exception may not apply cleanly.

**Exception C: Driving Training**
Registered driving schools imparting training on driving motor vehicles. The specific vehicle used for training qualifies; office cars of the driving school management do not.

### Practical Examples
- **Company car for CEO**: ₹15,00,000 + GST ₹2,70,000 → ITC **BLOCKED** (5-seat sedan, not dealer/transport/school)
- **Car dealership vehicle stock**: ₹20,00,000 + GST ₹3,60,000 → ITC **AVAILABLE** (further supply exception)
- **Taxi fleet vehicle (cab operator, 5-seater)**: GST ₹2,70,000 → ITC **AVAILABLE** (transportation of passengers exception)
- **Delivery vehicle (goods vehicle, 2-seat cab, large cargo area)**: Not covered by 17(5)(a) — this is a goods vehicle, not passenger vehicle

### Maintenance and Insurance of Blocked Vehicles
ITC on **general insurance, servicing, repair and maintenance** of Section 17(5)(a)-blocked vehicles is also blocked under Section 17(5)(b)(i) ("leasing, renting or hiring of motor vehicles... referred to in clause (a)"). The block extends across the life of the vehicle.

## Section 17(5)(aa) — Vessels and Aircraft
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(aa) CGST Act, 2017"}
{amendment_date: "01-Jan-2022 (restructured; concept existed from 2017)"}

### Statutory Text
> ITC shall not be available for vessels and aircraft except when they are used— (i) for making taxable supplies, namely: (A) further supply; (B) transportation of passengers; (C) training on navigating/flying; or (ii) for transportation of goods.

### Scope
Vessels: ships, boats, barges, dinghies used for non-business personal purposes. Aircraft: private jets, helicopters.

### Exceptions
- **Further supply**: Ship-building company's vessel inventory; aircraft dealer's fleet
- **Transportation of passengers**: Airline fleet; ferry services
- **Training**: Navigation/flying academies
- **Transportation of goods**: Shipping companies; cargo aircraft operators

### Practical Examples
- **Corporate yacht for client entertainment**: ITC **BLOCKED**
- **Private helicopter for MD's travel**: ITC **BLOCKED**
- **Airline's fleet (passenger service)**: ITC **AVAILABLE**
- **Shipping company's cargo vessel**: ITC **AVAILABLE**

## Section 17(5)(b)(i) — Food, Beverages, Personal Care, and Related Services
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(b)(i) CGST Act, 2017"}
{amendment_date: "01-Jul-2017"}

### Statutory Text
> ITC not available for: food and beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services, cosmetic and plastic surgery, leasing, renting or hiring of motor vehicles, vessels or aircraft referred to in clause (a) or clause (aa) except when used for the purposes specified therein, life insurance and health insurance.

**Provided** that ITC shall be available where an inward supply of a particular category is used by a registered person for making an outward taxable supply of the **same category** or as an element of a taxable composite or mixed supply.

### Categories Covered

| Category | Examples | Block? | Exception? |
|----------|---------|--------|-----------|
| Food and beverages | Office pantry, team lunches | YES | Restaurant using food for taxable supply |
| Outdoor catering | Company parties, client events | YES | Caterer providing taxable catering |
| Beauty treatment | Company-paid salon | YES | Beauty salon providing taxable services |
| Health services | Gym, medical check-ups (voluntary) | YES | Hospital providing taxable health services |
| Cosmetic/plastic surgery | Employer-paid cosmetic procedures | YES | Clinics providing taxable plastic surgery |
| Life insurance | Group term life for employees | YES | Insurance company (same category exception) |
| Health insurance | Group Mediclaim policy | YES | Insurance company (same category exception) |
| Renting of motor vehicle (≤13 seats) | Cab hire for employees | YES | If same category taxable outward supply |

### The Same-Category Exception in Detail
The exception applies when the inward supply category equals the outward supply category:
- A **restaurant** buying food ingredients → making taxable supply of food → ITC available
- A **beauty salon** buying beauty products → providing taxable beauty treatment → ITC available
- A **cab company** paying for vehicle hire → providing taxable cab service → ITC available
- An **IT company** buying food for employee pantry → providing software services → NO exception (different categories)

### Mandatory Employer Obligations (Government Notification)
Where a government notification specifies services as **obligatory for an employer to provide to its employees under any law**, ITC is available. Currently:
- **Canteen services** mandated by Factories Act 1948 (≥250 workers): ITC available per Circular 172/04/2022
- Uniform/safety equipment mandated under Mines Act, Factories Act: not food/beauty — check separate eligibility

## Section 17(5)(b)(ii) — Club and Fitness Centre Memberships
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(b)(ii) CGST Act, 2017"}

### Statutory Text
> ITC not available for: membership of a club, health and fitness centre.

### Scope — Absolute Block, No Exceptions
- Golf club membership: blocked
- Social/business club membership: blocked
- Gym/fitness centre subscription: blocked
- Sports facility membership: blocked
- Yoga/meditation centre membership: blocked

**No exceptions** exist for this category. Even if networking at the club is a business necessity, ITC is unavailable.

### **[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]**
Corporate club memberships (Willingdon Club, Cricket Club of India, etc.) are very common in Mumbai for senior executives. These are high-value items — annual memberships at premium Mumbai clubs range from ₹5 lakh to ₹50 lakh with GST. ITC on all of these is fully blocked. Maharashtra SGST auditors specifically look for this.

## Section 17(5)(b)(iii) and (iv) — Rent-a-Cab, Life Insurance, Health Insurance, LTA
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(b)(iii), (iv) CGST Act, 2017"}

### Section 17(5)(b)(iii) — Rent-a-Cab, Life Insurance, Health Insurance
Blocked **unless**:
- (A) Government notifies the service as obligatory for employers under any law
- (B) Same-category outward supply

Rent-a-cab for employee commute: blocked (unless cab service is the business).  
Life insurance/health insurance: blocked unless mandatory under law or same-category business.

### Section 17(5)(b)(iv) — Travel Benefits on Vacation (LTA/LTC)
> Travel benefits extended to employees on vacation such as leave or home travel concession.

Air/train tickets for personal vacation under LTA scheme: ITC on the ticket GST is blocked.  
Note: ITC on business travel (client visits, official meetings) is NOT blocked — this clause is specifically for personal vacation benefits.

## Section 17(5)(c) — Works Contract Services for Immovable Property
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(c) CGST Act, 2017 as amended by Finance Act 2021"}
{amendment_date: "01-Jan-2022 (effective retrospectively from 01-Jul-2017)"}

### Statutory Text (as amended)
> Works contract services when supplied for construction of an immovable property (other than plant and machinery) except where it is an input service for further supply of works contract service.
> 
> **Explanation**: For the purposes of clauses (c) and (d), the expression "construction" includes re-construction, renovation, additions or alterations or repairs, **to the extent of capitalisation**, to the said immovable property.

### The Finance Act 2021 Explanation — Critical Impact
The addition of the Explanation changed the landscape dramatically:
- **Pre-amendment understanding**: only new construction (not repairs) was blocked
- **Post-amendment (retrospective from 2017)**: any renovation/repair/alteration that is **capitalised in books** = "construction" = ITC blocked

**The Capitalisation Test**:
- Renovation capitalised in fixed assets → BLOCKED (it is "construction" per Explanation)
- Repairs and maintenance expensed to P&L → NOT blocked (not capitalised, not "construction")

### Exception — Further Supply of Works Contract
A sub-contractor who provides works contract services to a main contractor (for the main contractor's further supply of works contract services to the end client) can claim ITC. The main contractor can also claim ITC on sub-contractor invoices as an input for further taxable works contract supply.

The end client/building owner who receives works contract for constructing their own building: NO ITC (the exception does not apply to them — they are not making a further supply of works contract).

### Plant and Machinery Exception
Works contract for construction of plant and machinery (as defined in the Explanation to Section 17) is NOT blocked. ITC is available if the constructed asset qualifies as plant and machinery.

## Section 17(5)(d) — Goods/Services for Own-Account Construction of Immovable Property
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(d) CGST Act, 2017 as amended by Finance Act 2021"}
{amendment_date: "01-Jan-2022 (retrospective from 01-Jul-2017)"}

### Statutory Text
> Goods or services or both received by a taxable person for construction of an immovable property (other than plant or machinery) on his own account including when such goods or services or both are used in the course or furtherance of business.

**Explanation**: Same as Section 17(5)(c) — capitalised repairs/renovations are also "construction."

### Scope
This covers the **materials and services** (not works contract services — that is 17(5)(c)) received for constructing an immovable property:
- Cement, steel, bricks, tiles, paint purchased for own construction: ITC blocked
- Labour supply services for own construction: ITC blocked
- Architectural drawings: ITC blocked (for own account construction)
- MEP (mechanical, electrical, plumbing) services for own building: ITC blocked

### "On His Own Account" — Key Phrase
This blocks ITC only when the building is for **own use** (office, factory, residential). If building for **resale** (developer selling flats under GST): different position — developer's ITC on construction materials may be available since it's for making taxable outward supply (sale of under-construction flat).

### The Distinction Between 17(5)(c) and 17(5)(d)
| Feature | Section 17(5)(c) | Section 17(5)(d) |
|---------|-----------------|-----------------|
| What is blocked | Works contract SERVICE | Goods and other services |
| Typical scenario | Hiring a contractor for construction | Buying materials yourself |
| Exception | Further supply of works contract | Plant and machinery |
| Both subject to capitalisation test | Yes (Finance Act 2021) | Yes (Finance Act 2021) |

## Section 17(5)(e) — Tax Paid Under Section 10 (Composition)
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(e) CGST Act, 2017; Section 10 CGST Act, 2017"}

### Scope
Goods or services on which tax has been paid under Section 10 (Composition Scheme). Composition dealers pay tax at a flat rate (1%, 5%, or 6% depending on category) and **cannot claim any ITC**. This is the fundamental design of the composition scheme.

---

## Section 17(5)(f) — Non-Resident Taxable Persons
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(f) CGST Act, 2017"}

### Scope
A Non-Resident Taxable Person (NRTP) — a person occasionally making taxable supplies in India without a fixed place of business — cannot claim ITC on goods or services received in India, **except on goods imported by them**. NRTPs are required to pay advance IGST deposit before registration.

---

## Section 17(5)(g) — Personal Consumption
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(g) CGST Act, 2017"}

### Scope
Goods or services used for **personal consumption** by the proprietor, partners, directors, employees, or their families. "Personal consumption" has no statutory definition, which creates ongoing disputes (see [Shared/Disputed_Ambiguous_Areas.md]).

**Practical test**: Does the supply directly benefit a specific individual's personal life, or does it serve the business?
- Business necessity → eligible (subject to apportionment if mixed outputs)
- Personal benefit → blocked

## Section 17(5)(h) — Lost, Stolen, Destroyed Goods, Gifts, and Free Samples
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(h) CGST Act, 2017"}

### Scope
ITC must be **reversed** (if already claimed) when:
1. Goods are **lost** (theft, fire, flood, any loss)
2. Goods are **stolen**
3. Goods are **destroyed** (fire damage, expired goods, obsolescence write-off)
4. Goods are **written off** in books
5. Goods are **disposed of by way of gift** (diwali gifts, annual day gifts)
6. Goods are **given as free samples** (pharmaceutical samples to doctors, FMCG trial packs)

### Reversal Mechanics
When these events occur: reverse the ITC already claimed on those specific goods. If ITC was ₹50,000 on goods worth ₹2,77,778 that are now destroyed by fire: reverse ₹50,000 in the month of destruction.

---

## Section 17(5)(i) — Tax Paid Under Enforcement Sections
{law_reference: "Section 17(5)(i) CGST Act, 2017; Sections 74, 129, 130 CGST Act, 2017"}

### Scope
No ITC on tax paid under:
- **Section 74**: Tax demands arising from fraud, suppression, misrepresentation
- **Section 129**: Tax paid to obtain release of detained/seized goods
- **Section 130**: Tax paid for confiscated goods

These are penalty-related tax payments — allowing ITC on them would neutralize the punitive effect.
