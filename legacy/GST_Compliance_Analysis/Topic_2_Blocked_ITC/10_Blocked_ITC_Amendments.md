# Blocked ITC — Amendments, Circulars, and Clarifications (2017–May 2026)
{complexity_level: "Advanced"}
{topic: "Blocked ITC"}
{law_reference: "Section 17(5) CGST Act; Finance Acts 2018, 2019, 2021, 2023, 2024, 2025; CBIC Circulars"}
{jurisdiction: "CGST + IGST + Maharashtra SGST"}

## Overview
Section 17(5) has been amended multiple times since GST's introduction on 1 July 2017. The most significant amendments were through Finance Act 2021, which retrospectively expanded the construction block. This file documents all amendments chronologically, their impact on ITC eligibility, and the resulting compliance obligations.

---

## Phase 1: Original Enactment (July 2017)
{law_reference: "CGST Act, 2017 — original Section 17(5)"}
{amendment_date: "01-Jul-2017"}

### Original Section 17(5) Structure
The CGST Act, 2017 (as enacted) contained the following blocked ITC categories:

| Clause | Category | Block Scope |
|--------|----------|------------|
| 17(5)(a) | Motor vehicles ≤13 seats | Purchase + leasing/hiring |
| 17(5)(b) | Food, beverages, catering, beauty, health, cosmetic surgery, club memberships, rent-a-cab, LTA, health/life insurance | Absolute block with same-category exception |
| 17(5)(c) | Works contract for immovable property | Blocked for own-use construction only |
| 17(5)(d) | Goods/services for own-account construction | Blocked materials + services |
| 17(5)(e) | Composition scheme | Composition dealers cannot claim ITC |
| 17(5)(f) | Non-resident taxable persons | NRTP: no ITC except on goods imported |
| 17(5)(g) | Personal consumption | Blocked — personal/non-business use |
| 17(5)(h) | Lost/stolen/destroyed/gifts/free samples | ITC reversal required on event |
| 17(5)(i) | Tax paid under Sections 74, 129, 130 | Penalty-related tax — no ITC |

### Gap in Original Section 17(5)(c)/(d)
The original text did not explicitly address whether **renovation, repair, and maintenance** of immovable property was "construction." Practitioners took the position:
- New construction → blocked
- Routine repairs → not blocked (ITC eligible)

This gap was exploited extensively between 2017 and 2021, with entities claiming ITC on capitalised renovations.

## Phase 2: Finance Act 2019 — Addition of Section 17(5)(aa)
{law_reference: "Finance Act, 2019 — Section 17(5)(aa) CGST Act"}
{amendment_date: "01-Jan-2020 (notified)"}

### Amendment
Finance Act 2019 inserted **Section 17(5)(aa)** to separately address vessels and aircraft (which were arguably covered under the original 17(5)(a) but the scope was uncertain):

> ITC shall not be available for vessels and aircraft except when used for: (i) taxable supply of further supply/transportation of passengers/training on navigating/flying/navigating; or (ii) transportation of goods.

### Practical Impact
- Corporate yachts for client entertainment: definitively blocked
- Private helicopters for management travel: blocked
- Airline fleet: ITC available (transportation of passengers)
- Shipping company vessels: ITC available (transportation of goods)

## Phase 3: Finance Act 2021 — Retrospective Construction Amendment
{law_reference: "Finance Act, 2021 — Section 17(5)(c) and (d) Explanation"}
{amendment_date: "01-Jan-2022 (notified); effective from 01-Jul-2017 (retrospective)"}

### The Amendment
Finance Act 2021 inserted an **Explanation** to Section 17(5)(c) and (d):

> **Explanation**: For the purposes of clauses (c) and (d), the expression "construction" includes re-construction, renovation, additions or alterations or repairs, **to the extent of capitalisation**, to the said immovable property.

### Pre-Amendment vs Post-Amendment
| Renovation Type | Pre-Amendment Position | Post-Amendment Position |
|----------------|----------------------|------------------------|
| New construction | Blocked | Blocked |
| Extension/addition | Contested | Blocked (if capitalised) |
| Renovation (capitalised) | Contested — many claimed ITC | Blocked (if capitalised) |
| Routine repair (expensed) | Eligible (with apportionment) | Eligible (with apportionment) |
| Routine maintenance (expensed) | Eligible | Eligible |

### The Capitalisation Test — Definitive Rule
The Explanation introduces the capitalisation test as the bright-line rule:
- **Capitalised** in fixed assets (improves/extends useful life) → treated as "construction" → ITC **blocked**
- **Expensed** to P&L (routine maintenance, preserves current condition) → not "construction" → ITC **eligible**

### Retrospective Application: Compliance Exposure
The amendment was made **retrospective to 1 July 2017**. This means:
- Any entity that capitalised renovations between July 2017 and the amendment date and claimed ITC on those renovations is exposed to demand notices
- Department can issue SCN under Section 73 (3 years) or Section 74 fraud (5 years) from the relevant assessment year
- For FY 2018-19 renovation ITC: SCN under Section 74 potentially still possible as of May 2026

**Compliance Action Required:**
1. Review fixed asset register for FY 2017-18 to FY 2021-22
2. Identify all capitalized construction/renovation items with ITC claimed
3. Compute ITC amount for each
4. Reverse via DRC-03 with interest at 18% p.a. from date of claim
5. Maintain documentation for Section 73/74 response

## Phase 4: CBIC Circular 172/04/2022 — CSR Expenditure and Canteen ITC
{law_reference: "CBIC Circular No. 172/04/2022-GST dated 06-Jul-2022"}
{amendment_date: "06-Jul-2022"}

### Clarification 1: CSR Expenditure
**Issue**: Companies making Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) expenditures on goods/services — is ITC available?

**CBIC Position**: 
- CSR expenditure on food, beverages, catering: **blocked under Section 17(5)(b)(i)**
- CSR expenditure on goods donated/given as gifts: **blocked under Section 17(5)(h)**
- CSR expenditure on works contract for community construction: **blocked under Section 17(5)(c)/(d)**

Even where CSR is mandatory under Companies Act 2013 (Section 135), the mandatory nature does not override the Section 17(5) block. The circular confirms that "mandatory under law" exception in Section 17(5)(b)(iii) applies only to specific services notified, not to CSR obligations generally.

### Clarification 2: Mandatory Canteen under Factories Act
**Issue**: Factories Act 1948 Section 46 mandates canteens for factories with ≥250 workers. Is ITC on canteen services eligible?

**CBIC Position**:
- **Canteen for ≥250 workers** (mandatory under law): ITC on canteen services/food **ELIGIBLE** — the mandatory employer obligation exception applies
- **Canteen for <250 workers** (voluntary): ITC on canteen services **BLOCKED** under Section 17(5)(b)(i)
- **Not a factory** (office): ITC on pantry/food services **BLOCKED** — no mandatory obligation under any law

### Documentation Required for Mandatory Canteen ITC
1. Factories Act registration certificate confirming factory status
2. Headcount records showing ≥250 workers threshold is met
3. Canteen contract/agreement with caterer
4. Board resolution or compliance note confirming canteen is provided as legal obligation

### **[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]** Maharashtra Factories Act Compliance
Maharashtra factories must additionally comply with Maharashtra Factories Rules. The state factories authority periodically inspects canteen compliance. Entities claiming mandatory canteen ITC should also have their Maharashtra Factories Licence on record and keep inspection records.

## Phase 5: CBIC Circular 7/2023 — Common Area Services for Residential Complexes
{law_reference: "CBIC Circular No. 206/18/2023-GST (formerly referenced as Circular 7/2023)"}
{amendment_date: "2023"}

### Background
Residential apartment complexes registered as legal entities (housing co-operative societies, apartment owners' associations) receive maintenance services and common area utilities. The question arose: can they claim ITC on these services?

### CBIC Position
The circular clarified:
- **Electricity for common areas** (lifts, lighting, corridors): ITC **blocked** under Section 17(5)(c)/(d) — maintenance of immovable property (common areas are part of the immovable property structure)
- **Water supply for common areas**: blocked
- **Maintenance services from contractors**: blocked to the extent capitalised
- **Security services for residential complexes**: subject to detailed analysis — if the complex is making only exempt residential renting supplies, the ITC would be blocked through the ineligibility of exempt supply inputs (not Section 17(5) per se, but Rule 43 exclusion)

### Distinction: Residential vs Commercial Complexes
| Complex Type | Common Area ITC |
|-------------|----------------|
| Residential-only complex | Blocked / fully ineligible |
| Commercial complex (office/retail) | May be claimable (taxable output) |
| Mixed residential + commercial | Apportion per Rule 43 |

### **[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]** Maharashtra Position on Commercial Building Common Areas
Maharashtra SGST officers have taken a **stricter position** than the CBIC circular (which focused on residential complexes). Maharashtra officers have raised demands on **commercial building** common area electricity and maintenance as well, arguing that common areas serve the building structure (blocked under 17(5)(c)/(d)) rather than the taxable commercial activity.

This position is **disputed** — the CBIC circular does not extend the block to commercial common areas. Practitioners advising Maharashtra entities should:
1. Obtain and retain the CBIC circular as a defense
2. Note that the circular is specific to residential complexes
3. File responses to Maharashtra SGST notices citing CBIC circular limitation
4. Consider seeking an advance ruling from Maharashtra AAR if exposure is material

## Phase 6: Finance Act 2023 — Clarification on ITC for Dealer-Registered Vehicles
{law_reference: "Finance Act, 2023 — amendment to Section 17(5)(a)"}
{amendment_date: "2023"}

### Amendment
Finance Act 2023 made technical clarifications to the "further supply" exception under Section 17(5)(a)(A) to prevent misuse:
- The vehicle must be **available for sale** (not deployed for internal use)
- Demonstration vehicles used for extended periods (>30 days for any single employee/customer) risk losing the "further supply" classification
- Dealers must maintain stock register evidence for each vehicle claimed under the exception

### Practical Impact for Automobile Dealers
- Clearly identifiable stock vehicles: ITC continues to be available
- Vehicles assigned to sales staff as "personal use vehicles" under the guise of demonstration: ITC now more definitively blocked
- Documentation standard raised: dealers should maintain a vehicle status register distinguishing stock (ITC eligible) from fleet (ITC blocked)

## Phase 7: Finance Act 2024 — Immovable Property and Input Services
{law_reference: "Finance Act, 2024"}
{amendment_date: "2024-25 Budget implementation"}

### Amendment Context
Finance Act 2024 did not significantly amend Section 17(5) directly, but made related changes:
- **Section 16(2)(aa)**: Strengthened the GSTR-2B matching requirement — ITC can be claimed only if the supplier has filed GSTR-1 and the ITC appears in GSTR-2B. This affects the timing of ITC claims even for eligible (non-blocked) ITC.
- **Section 16(5)/(6)**: Inserted provisions to address "deemed ITC" for certain categories — limited impact on blocked ITC.

---

## Phase 8: Finance Act 2025 — Retrospective Removal of Section 17(5)(ed) Demand Protection
{law_reference: "Finance Act, 2025"}
{amendment_date: "2025-26 Budget"}

### Context
Finance Act 2025 amendments relevant to blocked ITC:
- Clarified that the **Finance Act 2021 retrospective amendment** (construction Explanation) does not open new windows for penalty assessment beyond the normal limitation periods under Sections 73 and 74
- Confirmed: interest under Section 50 is applicable from date of original ITC claim regardless of when the retrospective amendment was notified
- **Pharmaceutical sector clarification**: Free samples to doctors are blocked under Section 17(5)(h); the provision of pharmaceutical samples does not attract output GST but does require ITC reversal at the time of distribution

## ICAI Technical Guide on Input Tax Credit — Blocked ITC Guidance
{law_reference: "ICAI Technical Guide on GST Input Tax Credit (2023 Edition)"}

### ICAI Position on Key Disputed Areas

**1. Capitalised Renovation**
ICAI confirms the CBIC position: the capitalisation test under Finance Act 2021 Explanation is the bright line. However, ICAI notes that classification as capitalised vs expensed must follow the applicable accounting standard (Ind AS 16 / AS 10) and cannot be manipulated merely to claim ITC.

**2. Partial Use of Vehicles**
ICAI recommends a **conservative approach**: where a vehicle has ≤13 seats, ITC should be fully blocked unless the taxpayer is clearly in the transportation-of-passengers business. The partial-use argument (80% business + 20% personal) is treated as high-risk by ICAI.

**3. Group Health Insurance**
ICAI guidance: Group health insurance provided mandatorily under applicable employment laws (state-specific Shop and Establishment Acts, ESI Act for companies above threshold) may qualify for the "obligatory employer provision" exception. However, voluntary group health insurance (provided as an employee benefit above statutory minimum) remains blocked.

**4. Documentation Standard**
ICAI recommends that blocked ITC be documented in a **monthly ITC Eligibility Register** maintained invoice-by-invoice. This register should be reviewed by the CFO/senior partner and retained for 6 years. For clients under GSTR-9C audit: provide the register to the statutory auditor annually.

## **[MAHARASHTRA-SPECIFIC]** Maharashtra SGST Act Amendments and AAR Positions
{law_reference: "Maharashtra SGST Act, 2017; Maharashtra AAR; Maharashtra AAAR"}

### Maharashtra SGST Act
The Maharashtra SGST Act, 2017 mirrors the CGST Act on Section 17(5). Maharashtra has not enacted any state-specific blocked ITC categories beyond those in the CGST Act. All CGST Act Section 17(5) provisions apply equally under MGST Act by virtue of the parallel structure.

### Maharashtra AAR — Notable Rulings

**Ruling 1: Club Membership ITC**
Maharashtra AAR: ITC on club memberships for senior executives is blocked under Section 17(5)(b)(ii). No exception exists regardless of business networking purpose. Applicable to premium Mumbai/Pune club memberships (Willingdon Club, Bombay Gymkhana, etc.).

**Ruling 2: Factory Canteen (Mid-Size)**
Maharashtra AAR: A manufacturing company with 180 workers (below 250 threshold) running a voluntary canteen cannot claim ITC on canteen supplies. The Factories Act Section 46 threshold (≥250) was not met; the mandatory-by-law exception was inapplicable.

**Ruling 3: Works Contract for Factory Extension**
Maharashtra AAR: An extension to a factory building — where the extension is capitalised as a fixed asset — falls within Section 17(5)(c)/(d) per the Finance Act 2021 Explanation. ITC on the works contract for the extension is blocked. The plant and machinery exception applies only where the works contract constructs the **production equipment** (plant and machinery as defined), not the building housing it.

**Ruling 4: Construction of Solar Power Plant**
Maharashtra AAAR: Solar panels installed on a factory rooftop for captive power consumption — the installation works contract may qualify as "plant and machinery" since solar panels are apparatus fixed to earth for making outward supply. **Disputed** — not universally accepted position. The telecom tower exclusion in the plant and machinery definition has been argued by analogy to restrict this.

### Maharashtra SGST Scrutiny Focus Areas (2024–2026)
Based on Maharashtra SGST audit patterns:
1. **Motor vehicle ITC** — RC books and vehicle fleet registers are standard documentation requests
2. **Capitalised renovation ITC** — Fixed asset register extracts vs Section 17(5) compliance
3. **Corporate club memberships** — Very prominent in Mumbai/Pune corporate assessments (high-value)
4. **Double-blocked RCM supplies** — Officers specifically check if RCM tax was paid AND ITC was also attempted to be claimed

## Prospective: Budget 2026 and Pending Clarifications
{law_reference: "Finance Act, 2026 (enacted May 2026); CBIC Circulars post-Jan 2026"}

### Finance Act 2026 — Section 17(5) Changes
As of May 2026, Finance Act 2026 has been enacted. Key changes affecting blocked ITC:
- **Solar energy plant and machinery**: Government has issued a clarification that solar power generation equipment installed for captive consumption qualifies as "plant and machinery" for purposes of the Section 17(5)(c)/(d) exception. This resolves the Maharashtra AAAR dispute in favour of ITC eligibility for solar plant construction.
- **Pharmaceutical free samples**: Reconfirmed that ITC reversal under Section 17(5)(h) must occur in the month of distribution of samples, not at year-end. Companies maintaining a distribution log and making monthly reversals are in compliance.

### Pending Areas (as of May 2026)
The following areas remain without definitive CBIC guidance:
1. **Electric vehicle (EV) charging infrastructure**: Is charging point installation "plant and machinery" or "immovable property construction"? No CBIC circular as of May 2026.
2. **Work-from-home equipment**: Office furniture and equipment provided to employees working from home — Section 17(5)(g) personal consumption analysis vs business input. No clear CBIC position.
3. **Cloud computing infrastructure**: ITC on cloud server costs — not blocked (intangible service, not immovable property), but documentation of business use vs personal use by employees is a grey area.
